# Editorial QA SOP

Supplied by the site owner on 2026-10-21. Applies to Verdict Point, Fin Loom, Stack Row, Arizona Direct, and Wellness Signal. US English. Covers website blogs, articles, PR blogs, and every platform version.

**Core standard:** Every article must answer the reader's question, provide verified information, and add useful value beyond existing coverage. Style, formatting, and keyword placement cannot compensate for missing substance.

**Fin Loom precedence:** Where this SOP defers to "specific brand or brief" rules, the Fin Loom rules in `CLAUDE.md` apply. That includes the eight exact keyword occurrences, the hub-and-spoke silo links in `docs/SEO-AEO-GEO-SOP.md` (2 to 3 post links per spoke plus the pillar), and the FAQ and Key Takeaways structure.

## 1. Confirm the Assignment

Identify the primary reader question, audience and search intent, article type (statistics, research, explanation, process, comparison, review, news), essential information, primary keyword, brand requirements, word count, platform, link targets, and the proposed contribution beyond existing coverage.

QA check: Does the draft fulfill the assigned subject and angle without drifting?

## 2. Answer the Query Directly

- Give the central answer in the opening paragraph or first substantive section, normally within 100 words.
- Begin each substantive section with the answer its heading promises.
- Use roughly 40 to 60 words for direct-answer passages where helpful; vary only to preserve context and value.
- No introductions that only explain why the topic is important or promise an answer later.
- Key answer passages must stand alone: name the subject and keep essential conditions.

QA check: Can a reader find the answer quickly and understand it without another page?

## 3. Fulfill the Title's Promise

| Article type | Required substance |
|---|---|
| Statistics | Verified figures, comparisons or trends, source context, interpretation. |
| Research | Actual findings, study details, implications, limitations. |
| Definition or explanation | Clear definition, scope, mechanisms, examples, distinctions. |
| How-to | Usable steps, prerequisites, inputs, constraints. |
| Comparison | Consistent criteria, supported differences, tradeoffs, suitability. |
| Review | Verified capabilities, limitations, suitability; documented specs separated from hands-on experience. |
| News or prediction | What happened or is claimed, when, evidence, what remains unconfirmed. |

Reject a statistics article that mainly explains how to read statistics without presenting substantive figures.

## 4. Verify Statistics, Research, and Data

For each material claim verify the original source and passage, table, or dataset; publication date and measurement period; population, sample, and geography; units, denominator, definitions, and methodology; limitations; and whether it is an observed finding, survey response, estimate, forecast, calculation, or interpretation. Use primary sources. AI answers, search snippets, and competitor summaries are not verified evidence.

QA check: Does the source support the exact wording and level of certainty used?

## 5. Preserve Accurate Interpretation

Distinguish percentage changes from percentage-point changes, revenue from profit, accounts from users, access from outcomes, association from causation, and spending from effectiveness. Compare only compatible definitions, populations, and periods. Show verified inputs and formulas for calculations. Label forecasts, estimates, and hypotheticals. Keep qualifications beside the claim.

QA check: Could a reader draw a stronger conclusion than the evidence supports?

## 6. Provide Additional Reader Value

Each article needs at least one substantial contribution: a comparison across compatible findings, a clearer explanation of a mechanism, a transparent calculation, resolution of conflicting definitions, an evidence-based decision framework, or a specific supported example. Rewording existing articles is not added value.

QA check: Identify the specific passage that contributes beyond a summary of sources.

## 7. Never Invent Information

No fabricated statistics, studies, citations, quotations, interviews, expert commentary, product testing, firsthand experience, local visits, case studies, clients, outcomes, author identities, credentials, expert review, missing source details, or unsupported predictions.

## 8. Use Natural, Contextual Internal Linking

- Write the explanation first, then link descriptive phrases already serving it.
- Verify each destination and its specific reader benefit; keep the central answer complete on the page.
- Evidence citations are separate from internal-link limits.
- Never create a sentence or topic change only to fit a link.
- Avoid wrappers such as "FinLoom examines this in...", "Read our guide...", "For a deep dive...", "This breakdown covers...".

Three-part link test: (1) remove the hyperlink, is the sentence still useful and natural? (2) does the anchor describe the destination? (3) what specific need does the destination serve? If no suitable destination exists, flag linking as incomplete; never invent a URL.

## 9. Brand-Specific Checks (Fin Loom)

Preserve currency, periods, fees, assumptions, and definitions. Separate historical returns from projections. Avoid guaranteed outcomes.

## 10. Keyword Optimization

Record actual counts for the main heading, body, and metadata separately against the applicable requirement. Keep placement grammatical. Never bold the target keyword.

## 11. Language, Structure, and Presentation

US English. No em dashes, filler, unsupported superlatives, or formulaic promotion. Lists for steps or parallel points, tables for comparisons, FAQs only when useful. Tables, captions, graphics, metadata, and alt text must match verified claims. No padding or forced sales endings.

## 12. Final QA Status

| Status | Requirement |
|---|---|
| PASS | All applicable checks complete. |
| REVISE | Correctable problems remain. |
| HOLD | Essential evidence is missing, contradicted, fabricated, or cannot support the angle. |

Correct style or keyword counts never earn PASS while substantive failures remain. During drafting or authorized rewriting, fix failures before delivery. For audit-only requests, report issues and proposed corrections without silently rewriting.
