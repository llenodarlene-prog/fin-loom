# Average Credit Card Debt in America: Balances and Financial Strain in 2026

How much do Americans owe on credit cards? A single average credit card debt figure cannot answer that without first saying who is counted and whether a balance is costing interest. National totals, survey averages and bank statistics each measure something different.

In November 2026, the New York Fed reported $1.285 trillion in credit card balances at the end of the third quarter. That is a total across credit reports, not what a typical person owes. The Federal Reserve's household survey, linked to credit records, gives a second view: an average balance of $7,279 among matched respondents with cards in 2025, with growth concentrated among people who said they were struggling. This page sets out each figure with its source, period and population.

## Key Takeaways

- **Total balances hit $1.285 trillion:** the New York Fed reported card balances up $22 billion in Q3 2026 and $52 billion from a year earlier.
- **Survey average was $7,279:** among Fed survey respondents with cards whose records were matched, the 2025 average balance was up from $6,531 two years earlier.
- **Growth was uneven:** balances rose by more than $2,500, or 37%, among those "finding it difficult to get by," but by $59 among those "living comfortably."
- **Fewer people carry balances:** 45% of card owners said they carried a balance at least once in the prior year in 2025, down 12 points over a decade.
- **Interest is costly:** the average rate on accounts assessed interest at commercial banks was 22.36% in August 2026, per FRED.

## How Much Credit Card Debt Do Americans Have?

The [New York Fed's Q3 2026 household debt release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110), published November 10, 2026, uses anonymized Equifax credit data. It reported these totals for the end of September 2026.

| Measure | Figure | Period |
|---|---:|---|
| Credit card balances | $1.285 trillion | End of Q3 2026 |
| Quarterly change | Up $22 billion | Q2 to Q3 2026 |
| Annual change | Up $52 billion | Q3 2025 to Q3 2026 |
| Total household debt | $19.105 trillion | End of Q3 2026 |
| Increase in aggregate card limits | $115 billion | Q3 2026 |

Source: Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Household Debt and Credit Report, Q3 2026.

### What the Total Means

The $1.285 trillion figure is every reported card balance added together. It includes balances that will be paid in full by the due date. It does not show how many people revolve debt, how much interest they pay, or what the median person owes.

### Why Limits Count Too

Card limits rose by $115 billion in the quarter, far more than balances. Rising limits mean lenders were extending more credit. A larger limit with a steady balance lowers a borrower's utilization, which is one reason balance totals alone do not measure strain.

## What Is the Average Credit Card Balance?

The Federal Reserve's [Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED) for 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-credit.htm) links survey answers to credit records for respondents who agree. In 2025, 63% of respondents agreed to the match.

Among matched respondents with credit cards, the average balance was $7,279, up $748, or 11%, from $6,531 two years earlier. Both figures refer to the same 2025 respondents, so the change tracks those people over time.

| Financial well-being in 2025 | Average balance, 2023 | Average balance, 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Finding it difficult to get by | $6,735 | $9,265 |
| Just getting by | $6,782 | $8,581 |
| Doing okay | $6,668 | $7,308 |
| Living comfortably | $6,248 | $6,307 |
| All matched card holders | $6,531 | $7,279 |

Source: Federal Reserve, Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025, Box 1, Table A.

### Who Drove the Increase

The Fed found that people "finding it difficult to get by" or "just getting by" together accounted for 65% of balance growth in the 2025 survey, up from 40% in the 2023 and 2024 surveys. Those "living comfortably" accounted for just 3%, down from about one-fourth before.

### Why This Average Differs From Others

The SHED average is a mean across a matched sample of card holders. It is not a national figure for every adult. Other sources divide totals by all consumers, by all card holders or only by those with a balance, and each choice gives a different average credit card debt figure.

## How Many People Carry a Balance?

A balance on a credit report is not the same as debt carried from month to month. Someone who pays the full statement each month still shows a balance between payments.

The Fed's survey found that 82% of adults had a credit card in 2025, down from a high of 84% in 2021. Among card owners, 45% said they carried a balance at least once in the prior 12 months, down 12 percentage points over the past decade.

### Income and Carrying a Balance

The survey found that nearly all adults with income of $100,000 or more had a card. Slightly over half of card holders with income under $100,000 carried a balance from month to month, more than among higher-income card holders. Middle-income adults were the most likely to have a card they used to carry balances.

### Why This Distinction Is Central

Interest only applies to balances carried past the grace period. A person paying in full each month uses a card as a payment tool and may pay no interest at all. Mixing these two groups into one average credit card debt figure overstates the burden for some and understates it for others.

## How Fast Have Balances Grown?

The Fed's 2025 survey report notes that New York Fed data for Q3 2025 put aggregate card balances at $1.2 trillion, an all-time high and 14% higher than two years before. By Q3 2026, the New York Fed reported $1.285 trillion, another $52 billion more.

### Growth Is Not Automatically Bad

The Fed itself says higher balances can be hard to interpret on their own. They could reflect more spending from higher income or better conditions. They could also mean borrowers are using cards to cover bills they cannot otherwise pay, which is why the survey link to well-being is so useful.

### What the Survey Link Adds

By matching survey answers to credit records, the Fed could see who added debt. Its finding that most growth came from people under financial strain gives the rising total a clearer meaning than the headline number alone, and it shows why one national average can hide very different situations.

## What Interest Do Card Holders Pay?

FRED publishes Federal Reserve data on commercial bank card rates. In August 2026, the [average rate on accounts assessed interest](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TERMCBCCINTNS) was 22.36%. The average across all accounts, including those not charged interest, was 21.19%.

| Commercial bank card rate | August 2025 | August 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accounts assessed interest | 22.83% | 22.36% |
| All accounts | 21.39% | 21.19% |

Source: Federal Reserve G.19 via FRED (TERMCBCCINTNS, TERMCBCCALLNS), not seasonally adjusted.

### What a 22% Rate Costs

As a hypothetical illustration, a $7,279 balance at 22.36% would build about $1,628 in interest over a year if the balance stayed the same. Real interest depends on daily balances, payments and the card's terms, so this is a rough guide, not a quote.

### Rates Barely Moved

Card rates eased only slightly over the year to August 2026. Card rates are often tied to the prime rate plus a margin, and margins have stayed wide. For borrowers, that means paying down a balance can save far more than most savings accounts earn.

## How Do Banks Report Card Debt Problems?

FRED also carries bank-level data on card loans held by all commercial banks. These cover a different population from the New York Fed's credit panel, so their percentages should not be mixed.

The delinquency rate on credit card loans at all commercial banks was 2.85% in Q2 2026, seasonally adjusted, down from 3.04% a year earlier. The charge-off rate, which counts debt banks wrote off as unlikely to be repaid, was 3.82% in Q2 2026, down from 4.19% a year earlier.

| Commercial bank card measure | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Delinquency rate | 3.04% | 2.85% |
| Charge-off rate | 4.19% | 3.82% |

Source: Federal Reserve via FRED (DRCCLACBS, CORCCACBS), seasonally adjusted.

### Reading the Bank Data

Both measures improved over the year, even as total balances grew. That suggests borrowers as a group kept up with payments a little better. The household survey still shows strain is concentrated among people already struggling, so the averages hide two different stories.

## What About Average Credit Card Debt by Age?

Age shapes borrowing, but the Q3 2026 New York Fed release does not include an age table of average balances. Building an age ranking from other years or sources without labels would mix incompatible data.

A reliable age comparison would need the same data source, matching age bands, a stated denominator, and a clear choice of mean or median. Until that table is verified for 2026, this page does not publish age estimates.

### Fields Any Age Table Needs

The data year, the age bands, whether it counts all consumers or only card holders, whether it reports the mean or median, and whether balances are revolving or simply outstanding. Missing any one of these makes comparisons misleading, because two honest tables can differ widely on these choices alone.

### A Caution on Viral Tables

Many websites publish average credit card debt by age with no source year. Some reuse figures from earlier years. Readers should check the source and date before relying on any age table, including ones that look precise. A table without a year is a warning sign.

## Why Can Card Debt Rise Without Wider Strain?

Totals reflect prices, spending, the number of accounts, credit limits and payment habits. Rising balances can signal strain, but they also grow when people put more everyday spending on cards and pay it off later.

The bank delinquency and charge-off rates both fell over the year to Q2 2026. Combined with the SHED results, the picture is mixed: steady or slightly better repayment across all borrowers, alongside rising balances among households already under pressure.

### Inflation's Role

Higher prices raise the dollar amount of everyday purchases. A household buying the same goods pays more and may carry a larger statement balance. That alone can lift totals without any change in borrowing behavior, which is why comparing balances across years without adjusting for prices can exaggerate growth.

### Buy Now, Pay Later

Some spending has moved to buy now, pay later loans, which do not always appear in card totals. The Fed's survey found growing use of these products. That shift can make card totals look smaller than total short-term consumer borrowing.

## How Does Card Debt Compare With Other Household Debt?

Card balances are a small slice of what households owe. The New York Fed's Q3 2026 report breaks total household debt of $19.105 trillion into categories, and credit cards made up about 6.7% of it.

| Debt category | Balance, end of Q3 2026 | Change from Q2 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mortgages | $13.374 trillion | Up $257 billion |
| Auto loans | $1.734 trillion | Up $21 billion |
| Student loans | $1.656 trillion | Up $5 billion |
| Credit cards | $1.285 trillion | Up $22 billion |
| Other | $0.575 trillion | Up $7 billion |
| Home equity lines of credit | $0.481 trillion | Up $23 billion |

Source: Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Q3 2026.

### Why Card Debt Gets More Attention

Card debt is smaller than mortgage or auto debt, but it usually carries much higher interest and no collateral. A mortgage at a single-digit rate and a card balance above 20% are very different burdens per dollar. That cost gap explains why average credit card debt figures draw so much attention despite the smaller total.

### Reading the Average Credit Card Debt Figure in Context

A household with a large mortgage and no card balance can be on solid footing. Another with no mortgage but a few thousand dollars on cards at 22% may be under real strain. The average credit card debt figure alone cannot tell those households apart, so it works best alongside income, rates and savings.

## How Can a Household Use These Figures?

National averages are context, not a target. A household is better served by listing each card's balance, rate, minimum payment and any promotional rate end date. A small balance at a high rate can cost more than a larger one at a low rate.

FinLoom's [credit card payoff calculator](/money-banking/credit-card-payoff-calculator/) shows how long a balance takes to clear at a chosen payment. For building a cash cushion that can reduce reliance on cards, the [checking vs savings account](/money-banking/checking-vs-savings-account/) guide explains where short-term money can sit.

### Saving and Debt Together

The pillar guide on [saving vs investing](/money-banking/saving-vs-investing/) covers why short-term needs come first. Many people weigh paying down high-rate card debt against building savings. With card rates above 20%, reducing the balance usually earns more than a deposit account pays.

### When to Ask for Help

Anyone worried about missing a payment can contact the card issuer before the due date. Many issuers offer hardship programs. Nonprofit credit counseling agencies can also help build a repayment plan. Asking early, before an account falls behind, usually leaves more options open.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Average Credit Card Debt in 2026?

There is no single figure. The New York Fed reported $1.285 trillion in total balances for Q3 2026, and the Fed's survey found a $7,279 average among matched card holders.

### Is $1.285 Trillion the Amount Americans Pay Interest On?

No. It counts all reported card balances, including those paid in full each month. It does not separate balances that build interest from those cleared by the due date.

### Why Do Websites Show Different Average Balances?

They use different years, data sources and denominators. Some divide by all consumers, others by card holders or only people carrying a balance, which changes the result.

### How Many Americans Carry a Credit Card Balance?

In the Fed's 2025 survey, 45% of card owners said they carried a balance at least once in the prior 12 months, down 12 points over a decade.

### What Is the Average Credit Card Interest Rate?

Federal Reserve data on FRED show 22.36% in August 2026 for commercial bank accounts assessed interest. The average across all accounts was 21.19%.
