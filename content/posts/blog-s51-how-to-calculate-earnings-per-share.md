# Calculation of Earnings Per Share: Basic and Diluted EPS Explained

Earnings per share (EPS) looks simple on a stock quote page: a company's profit divided by its shares. In an annual report, the calculation of earnings per share is more careful. The company may pay preferred dividends, issue or buy back shares during the year, and hold stock options or awards that could add shares later. Those details explain why two reasonable EPS figures can differ.

Basic EPS divides the earnings available to common shareholders by the weighted-average number of common shares outstanding during the period. Diluted EPS also counts the possible effect of securities that could become common shares, such as some options, restricted stock units and convertible instruments. EPS helps compare profit on a per-share basis, but it works best alongside cash flow, sales, debt and share-count trends.

## Key Takeaways

- Basic EPS uses earnings available to common shareholders, which can differ from headline net income.
- The share count is a weighted average over the period, not the number of shares on the last day.
- Diluted EPS usually adds potential shares from options and awards, so it is equal to or lower than basic EPS for a profitable company.
- Apple reported fiscal 2025 basic EPS of $7.49 and diluted EPS of $7.46 on net income of $112.01 billion.
- Share buybacks can lift EPS even when profit barely changes, so check the share count before reading EPS growth.

## The EPS Formula at a Glance

The calculation of earnings per share starts with one basic formula.

**Basic EPS = (net income − preferred dividends) ÷ weighted-average common shares outstanding**

For a company without preferred shares, the top of the fraction is simply the net income that belongs to common shareholders. The bottom measures the average number of shares outstanding across the period, weighted for any changes in the share count. That is why dividing by the year-end share count from a quote page can give a different answer from the EPS in a company's annual report.

Diluted EPS is more involved, because different securities are treated differently under accounting rules. A simplified version is adjusted earnings available to common shareholders divided by weighted-average diluted shares. A real filing applies specific methods to options and convertible securities, and it leaves out any security whose inclusion would raise EPS.

## Example: Calculating Basic EPS

Consider a hypothetical company with $120 million of net income, $6 million of preferred dividends and 38 million weighted-average common shares. First subtract the preferred dividends: $120 million − $6 million = $114 million. Then divide by the weighted-average share count: $114 million ÷ 38 million = $3.00 basic EPS.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Reported net income | $120 million |
| Preferred dividends | $6 million |
| Earnings available to common shareholders | $114 million |
| Weighted-average common shares | 38 million |
| Basic EPS | $3.00 |

The units cancel out: millions of dollars divided by millions of shares gives dollars per share. In a real annual report, check whether preferred dividends have already been taken out of the figure you start from. Subtracting them twice would understate EPS.

## Why Weighted-Average Shares Count

Suppose the same company starts the year with 40 million shares and buys back 4 million exactly halfway through the year. Ignoring other changes, it has 40 million shares for half the year and 36 million for the other half. The weighted average is (40 million × 0.5) + (36 million × 0.5) = 38 million shares.

Using the 36 million year-end count instead would give $114 million ÷ 36 million = $3.17, which does not describe the average share count over the whole year. The weighted average better reflects how much profit was earned per share during the period. Real calculations use exact transaction dates and adjust for stock splits.

### What If the Company Issues New Shares?

New shares raise the weighted-average count from the date they are issued, which lowers EPS unless profit grows enough to offset the larger share count. That does not mean the new money was wasted. A company might issue shares to pay for an acquisition or expansion whose benefits show up in later years, and EPS alone cannot tell you whether that financing decision was sensible.

### What Happens After a Stock Split?

A stock split changes the number of shares and the share price without creating any new profit. Comparative per-share figures are usually restated for the split, so earlier years can be compared on the same basis. Do not mistake a mechanical change in EPS caused by a split for a real change in how the business performed.

## How Diluted EPS Is Calculated

Diluted EPS considers securities that could turn into common shares. Employee stock options, restricted stock units, warrants and convertible securities can all add shares, but the treatment depends on each instrument's terms and on accounting rules. The earnings figure may also change, especially for convertible debt, where assumed conversion would remove an interest cost.

Return to the hypothetical $114 million of earnings and assume that, after the proper calculations, 2 million extra potential shares are included and no earnings adjustment is needed. The diluted share count is 40 million, and diluted EPS is $114 million ÷ 40 million = $2.85. That is lower than the $3.00 basic EPS, because the same earnings are spread over more shares.

| Measure | Basic | Diluted (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings available to common shareholders | $114 million | $114 million |
| Weighted-average shares | 38 million | 40 million |
| EPS | $3.00 | $2.85 |

This example is deliberately simple. Do not calculate diluted EPS by adding every outstanding option to the share count. Accounting rules account for exercise prices, the cash a company would receive, conversions and any changes to earnings, and they exclude securities that would raise EPS.

## A Real Example: Apple's Fiscal 2025 EPS

Real filings show both measures side by side. [Apple's condensed consolidated statements](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/pdfs/fy2025-q4/FY25_Q4_Consolidated_Financial_Statements.pdf) for its fiscal year ended September 27, 2025 report the following figures, with share counts in thousands.

| Apple, fiscal 2025 | Basic | Diluted |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | $112,010 million | $112,010 million |
| Shares used in computing EPS | 14,948,500 thousand | 15,004,697 thousand |
| Reported EPS | $7.49 | $7.46 |

You can check the arithmetic yourself. $112,010 million divided by 14,948.5 million shares gives about $7.49, and dividing by 15,004.7 million diluted shares gives about $7.46. The diluted count is about 56 million shares higher, which trims three cents from EPS.

An EPS figure that simply divides net income by the latest share count skips the part of the calculation that carries the most weight.

## Basic EPS vs Diluted EPS

Basic EPS tells you how much profit the company earned per existing common share, on a weighted-average basis. Diluted EPS asks what that figure might look like if eligible potential shares were issued. When a profitable company has large equity awards or convertible securities, the gap between the two can be noticeable.

Diluted EPS is usually equal to or lower than basic EPS for a profitable company. When adding potential shares would raise EPS or shrink a loss per share, including them would be anti-dilutive, so they are generally left out. That is why a loss-making company can report the same basic and diluted loss per share even while it has options outstanding.

## Why Share Buybacks Can Raise EPS Without Raising Profit

Consider a hypothetical company that earns $100 million in each of two years. If its weighted-average shares fall from 50 million to 40 million, basic EPS rises from $2.00 to $2.50 even though profit did not change.

| Hypothetical year | Net income | Weighted-average shares | Basic EPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | $100 million | 50 million | $2.00 |
| Year 2 | $100 million | 40 million | $2.50 |

Apple's own figures show the share count moving the same way. Its basic weighted-average shares fell from 15,343,783 thousand in fiscal 2024 to 14,948,500 thousand in fiscal 2025, while net income rose from $93,736 million to $112,010 million. Basic EPS rose from $6.11 to $7.49, driven by both higher profit and fewer shares.

A buyback may create value, but it also uses cash or adds debt, and the price paid for the shares counts. Look at revenue growth, margins, free cash flow and the share count before treating rising EPS as proof that the business itself grew.

One practical check is to split EPS growth into its two parts. Work out how much net income grew, then how much the share count fell. In Apple's case, net income rose about 19.5% from fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2025, while basic weighted shares fell about 2.6%. Together those produce the roughly 22.6% rise in basic EPS, which shows that most of the gain came from higher profit rather than from buybacks alone. These percentages are FinLoom calculations from Apple's reported figures.

## EPS and the P/E Ratio

EPS is an accounting measure of profit. The price-to-earnings ratio (P/E) compares a stock's market price with an EPS figure, often the last 12 months of diluted EPS. If a stock trades at $60 and its EPS is $3, its P/E is 20. The ratio changes when either the price or the earnings figure moves.

The quality of the EPS figure shapes the P/E. A one-off gain, a cyclical peak in profit or a large buyback can make trailing EPS unusually high, which makes the P/E look unusually low. Compare earnings quality and history rather than treating one P/E number as a verdict.

EPS also connects to company size. [FINRA explains](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/market-cap) that market cap is the share price times shares outstanding, so dividing market cap by net income gives roughly the same answer as dividing the share price by EPS. ## Quarterly vs Annual EPS

Companies report EPS for each quarter and for the full year, and the two are calculated separately. Apple's fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 shows how a quarterly calculation of earnings per share works on its own period.

| Apple, three months ended September 27, 2025 | Basic | Diluted |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | $27,466 million | $27,466 million |
| Shares used in computing EPS | 14,815,307 thousand | 14,863,609 thousand |
| Reported EPS | $1.85 | $1.85 |

Each quarter uses its own weighted-average share count, so four quarterly EPS figures do not have to add up exactly to the annual figure. When a company buys back shares through the year, the share count shrinks quarter by quarter, and the annual calculation of earnings per share uses the average for the whole year instead. Always compare a quarter with the same quarter of the prior year, or a full year with a full year, rather than mixing the two.

## Where to Find EPS in a Company's Reports

Start with the income statement, where basic and diluted EPS usually appear for the current and prior periods. Apple's statements, for example, list both EPS figures directly below net income, followed by the shares used to compute each one.

Then look at the notes for a section titled earnings per share. The note usually explains the earnings figure used, the basic share count, the potential shares included and any securities left out because they would have been anti-dilutive. Reading that note helps when a quote site shows a surprising EPS figure or when the share count changed sharply during the year. If the note and a data site disagree, trust the company's own filing and check which period and share count the site used.

Screeners often let you filter by EPS growth or P/E. FinLoom's [stock screener metrics guide](/markets-stocks/stock-screener-metrics-guide/) explains which of those filters are useful and where they can mislead.

## Common Calculation Mistakes

The most common error in the calculation of earnings per share is dividing net income by the year-end share count rather than the weighted average. Others include forgetting preferred dividends, counting every option as a diluted share, mixing a quarter's earnings with a year's share count, and using a P/E from a different period than the EPS.

Units cause trouble too, and they are easy to miss when you copy figures from different tables. A filing that reports dollars in millions and shares in thousands, as Apple's does, needs matching units on both sides of the fraction. Getting that wrong shifts the answer by a factor of a thousand.

Another mistake is to assume EPS equals cash available to shareholders. Net income includes non-cash accounting items, while the cash a company can return depends on operating cash flow, investment spending and debt. A company can report healthy EPS and still choose not to pay a dividend, since [FINRA notes](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/stocks) that companies do not have to pay dividends and can cut them.

## EPS Is a Measurement Tool, Not a Forecast

The basic and diluted EPS figures in a filing summarize a defined accounting period. They are useful for comparisons, but future earnings depend on business and financing decisions that come later. EPS becomes more informative when you pair it with revenue, margins, cash flow, balance-sheet strength and the notes that explain changes in the share count.

FinLoom's guide to [stock analysis fundamentals](/markets-stocks/stock-analysis-fundamentals/) shows how EPS fits into a wider review of a company's financial statements, from revenue through cash flow. A careful calculation of earnings per share is one step in that review, not the whole of it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Formula for the Calculation of Earnings Per Share?

Basic EPS equals net income minus preferred dividends, divided by weighted-average common shares outstanding for the period. Diluted EPS also adds eligible potential shares to the share count.

### Why Is Diluted EPS Lower Than Basic EPS?

For profitable companies, diluted EPS includes extra potential shares from options and awards, spreading earnings over more shares. Securities that would raise EPS are left out under accounting rules.

### Can EPS Be Negative?

Yes. A net loss for common shareholders produces negative basic EPS. Potential shares that would shrink the loss per share are excluded, so basic and diluted figures often match.

### Does a Stock Split Increase EPS?

No. A split adds shares and lowers the share price without changing profit, so EPS falls in proportion. Companies usually restate earlier figures so periods stay comparable.

### Why Did EPS Rise When Revenue Fell?

EPS can rise because margins improved, a one-time gain occurred, costs fell or the share count dropped after buybacks. The income statement and the EPS note show which reason applies.

### Is Higher EPS Always Better?

No. Higher EPS can reflect stronger operations or simply fewer shares, and it can include one-time items. Compare EPS changes with cash flow, revenue and how buybacks were funded.
