# Sector Rotation Statistics: All 11 S&P 500 Sectors in October 2026

October 2026 was a hard month for anyone who assumed every U.S. stock was heading down together. The S&P 500 fell 0.9%, yet nine of its eleven sectors gained. Energy rose 14.0% while Communication Services lost 15.1%, a spread of 29.1 percentage points in one month.

That is sector rotation in unusually clear form: investors marked down one group of businesses while moving toward others. It did not prove the economy had changed overnight, and it did not guarantee that October's winners would keep winning. This page sets out all eleven sector returns for October 2026, explains what drove the leaders and laggards according to market reviews, and shows how to read a sector leaderboard without treating it as a forecast.

## Key Takeaways

- **Nine of eleven sectors rose:** only Communication Services and Information Technology fell in October 2026.
- **Energy led:** the Energy sector gained 14.0%, followed by Consumer Staples at 11.6% and Utilities at 10.2%.
- **The spread was 29.1 points:** from Energy's 14.0% gain to Communication Services' 15.1% loss.
- **The index still fell:** the S&P 500 lost 0.9% because its largest companies sit in the two declining sectors.
- **The pattern was global:** J.P. Morgan reported energy and defensive sectors also led developed markets outside the U.S.

## What Is Sector Rotation?

Sector rotation happens when market leadership shifts from one industry group to another. Investors may change their expectations about growth, interest rates, commodity prices, earnings or risk. A sector that was popular can lag while less fashionable businesses move ahead.

The key word is relative. Two sectors can both rise, with one rising much faster. Or the overall index can fall even while most sectors gain, because market-value-weighted indexes give some companies far more influence than others. October 2026 showed both effects at once, which is why it makes a useful case study for anyone learning how sector rotation works.

### How Sectors Are Defined

The S&P 500 is divided into eleven sectors under the Global Industry Classification Standard. Each company is assigned to one sector based on its main business. Some large digital platforms sit in Communication Services or Consumer Discretionary rather than Information Technology, which is why "tech" and "Information Technology" are not the same.

### Rotation Versus a Broad Decline

A broad decline pulls most sectors down together. Rotation moves money between sectors, so some rise while others fall. October 2026 was mainly rotation. The November 5 selloff that followed was broader, with losses across most of the market, including the Dow and smaller companies.

### The Spread Between First and Last

The gap between the best and worst sectors was 29.1 percentage points: Energy's 14.0% less Communication Services' negative 15.1%. A spread measures how widely returns varied. It says nothing about which sector is more likely to rise next month or how long the gap will last.

### How the Index Fell Anyway

FRED's [S&P 500 series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500) shows the index closing at 7,651.54 on September 30 and 7,580.07 on October 30, a 0.93% price decline. With nine sectors rising, the drop came from the weight of the two falling sectors, which hold several of the largest companies in the index.

## Why Did Energy and Defensive Sectors Lead?

Market reviews linked Energy's strength to rising crude oil prices and supply concerns. Defensive groups such as Consumer Staples, Utilities and Health Care also gained as investors moved away from concentrated growth exposure. These are reported explanations, not controlled tests proving one factor caused each gain.

J.P. Morgan Asset Management's [October market review](https://am.jpmorgan.com/fr/en/asset-management/liq/insights/market-insights/market-updates/monthly-market-review/) reported a similar pattern across developed markets: energy up 12.8%, consumer staples up 10.7%, health care up 9.9% and utilities up 7.9%. That suggests the rotation reflected global investor behavior, not only conditions in the U.S. market.

### Defensive Does Not Mean Risk-Free

Utilities can be sensitive to borrowing costs and regulation. Consumer staples companies can face margin pressure when input costs rise. Energy profits depend on commodity prices and how companies spend their cash. October's strong returns did not remove any of those risks.

### Why Defensive Sectors Gain in Uncertain Periods

Companies selling necessities, such as food, household goods, electricity and medicine, tend to have steadier demand when the economic outlook is uncertain. When investors grow cautious about high-growth businesses, money often moves toward these steadier earners. That pattern is common, but it does not hold every time.

## Why Did Technology and Communication Services Fall?

The two declining sectors hold most of the largest AI-related companies. Market reviews described investor concern over the scale of capital spending on data centers and how long it would take to produce cash flow. J.P. Morgan reported October declines of 18.7% for Alphabet and 15.2% for Meta, both in Communication Services, and 10.4% for Nvidia, in Information Technology.

FinLoom's [Magnificent Seven statistics](/markets-stocks/magnificent-seven-2026-selloff-statistics/) show how concentrated those sectors are in a handful of companies, and why their moves carry so much weight in the overall index.

### Size Amplified the Drag

Because these sectors contain the largest companies, their losses had an outsized effect on the standard S&P 500. The equal-weight S&P 500, which gives every member similar influence, rose 5.0% in October, according to Janus Henderson. The 5.9-point gap between the two shows how much the two falling sectors pulled on the headline index.

### Growth Versus Value

The rotation also showed up between investment styles. Janus Henderson reported that the Russell 1000 Value Index rose 4.4% while the Russell 1000 Growth Index fell 6.1% in October 2026. Value indexes hold more energy, financial and defensive companies, so they benefited from the same shift.

## What Made the October 2026 Rotation Unusual?

The striking feature was not just that Energy beat Technology. It was how much the index's weighting hid the strength underneath. The standard S&P 500 lost 0.9% while its equal-weight version gained 5.0%. Janus Henderson noted that was the equal-weight index's largest monthly lead since April 2009.

This is why a statement like "the stock market fell in October" needs a named index. The Russell 2000 gained 0.6%, according to Janus Henderson, and the Dow gained 1.8%, according to Nasdaq's review. Those differences are part of the story.

### Why the Weighting Story Repeats

The same pattern appears whenever a few very large companies move against the rest of the market. In strong years for large technology companies, the standard index beats the equal-weight version. In months like October 2026, the reverse happens. The weighting rule, not the sector mix alone, decides how the headline index behaves.

### What Came Next

The rotation did not protect the market from a broad selloff. On November 5, 2026, the S&P 500 fell about 4.4% in one session. FinLoom's [stock market crash 2026 statistics](/markets-stocks/stock-market-crash-2026-statistics/) set out how the losses spread that day, from the Nasdaq to the Dow and smaller companies.

## How the Sectors Compare Over Different Windows

A one-month table can look very different from a longer view. Sectors that led in October may have trailed for most of the year, and the reverse. Janus Henderson reported that, even after October, the S&P 500 was up 11.7% year to date and the equal-weight S&P 500 was up 15.5% through the end of October 2026.

Those year-to-date figures show that the equal-weight index, and the broader set of sectors it reflects, had been ahead for 2026 as a whole, not only in October. Any sector rotation analysis should state its window, because a monthly, yearly or multi-year view can point in different directions.

### Why Time Frame Changes the Story

A sector that rose 14% in a month may still be below its level from a year earlier. A sector that fell 15% may still be well ahead over five years. Without the time frame, a sector return cannot be compared fairly with anything else.

### Matching the Window to the Question

Use one month to understand a recent shift, one year to see the current trend, and several years to judge long-term growth. Each window answers a different question, and each should be labeled with its start and end dates so readers can compare like with like.

## How Should Investors Read a Sector Leaderboard?

A sector return is a snapshot of one period. Before acting on it, separate price performance from business results. Ask whether the sector's earnings outlook improved, whether its valuation rose, and whether its main risk changed.

A leaderboard can also hide variation inside a sector. A financially strong company can lag a rising sector, while a heavily indebted company can rally on a short-term shift in sentiment. Sector averages are not recommendations for every stock inside them. FinLoom's guide to [stock analysis fundamentals](/markets-stocks/stock-analysis-fundamentals/) explains how to look at individual companies.

### Using Sector Data to Check a Portfolio

Sector results are useful for spotting unwanted concentration. If one or two sectors make up most of your stock holdings, a rotation like October's will affect you more. Comparing your sector mix with a broad index shows where you differ from the market. Many fund providers publish sector weights on each fund's fact sheet, which makes this comparison quick to do once or twice a year without special tools.

### Why Chasing Last Month's Winner Is Risky

Buying whichever sector led last month assumes the trend will continue. Sector leadership has shifted many times, sometimes within a few months. A strong month often reflects news that prices have already absorbed, so the next month can look very different.

## Does a Sector Rotation Strategy Work?

A sector rotation strategy tries to move money into sectors expected to lead next, often based on the economic cycle. The idea is simple to describe and hard to do well, because it requires predicting both the economy and how prices will react, often before the data confirms it.

Trading between sectors also adds costs and, in taxable accounts, can trigger capital gains taxes. Many investors instead hold a broad index fund, which owns all sectors in proportion to their size, and rebalance on a set schedule. Neither approach guarantees better results. The right choice depends on goals, costs and how much time an investor wants to spend.

### Judging a Strategy Fairly

Any sector strategy should be judged on written rules, its full costs and its results over many years, including the periods when it did badly. A strategy shown only through its best months tells you little. Results that depend on knowing the future, such as buying a sector just before it rises, cannot be repeated in real time.

### A Simpler Use of Sector Data

For most people, sector returns are more useful as a check than as a trading signal. Reviewing them once a quarter shows how a portfolio's mix compares with the market and whether any sector has grown larger than intended after a strong run.

## How Rate Expectations Fit the Sector Picture

Interest rates often shape sector rotation. Growth companies are more sensitive to rates because more of their expected profit lies in the future, while utilities and real estate are sensitive because they tend to borrow heavily. Janus Henderson reported that the Federal Reserve left its policy rate unchanged at 3.75% to 4.00% in October 2026, as widely expected.

J.P. Morgan reported that U.S. government bonds returned 2.4% in October as yields eased from September's highs. Falling yields usually help growth stocks, yet technology fell anyway. That suggests October's sector rotation was driven more by doubts about AI spending than by a change in rate expectations.

### Commodity Prices and Energy

Energy's lead was tied to oil prices. Janus Henderson noted that higher gas prices accounted for more than half of September's monthly rise in U.S. consumer prices, and Nasdaq's October review linked the energy sector's gain to rising crude oil prices and supply concerns. When oil rises, energy producers' earnings outlook improves, and their shares often follow.

### Why These Links Are Not Rules

Rates, commodities and sector returns are related, but not in a fixed way. The same rate move can help a sector in one year and hurt it in another, depending on what else is happening. These links explain past moves better than they predict future ones.

## Common Mistakes When Reading Sector Rotation Data

The first mistake is treating the sector table as a forecast. October's leaders are not more likely to lead in November because they led in October. The second is confusing sectors with themes. "Tech" covers parts of several sectors, and "AI stocks" sit across more than one.

The third is ignoring weight. Nine sectors rising did not stop the index falling, because the two declining sectors are among the largest. The fourth is mixing sector index returns with sector fund returns, which include fees and may hold slightly different stocks. Keeping these distinctions makes any sector rotation figure easier to read.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Sector Performed Best in October 2026?

Energy led the S&P 500 sectors with a 14.0% gain in October 2026, according to market reviews from Janus Henderson and Nasdaq published November 2.

### Which Sector Performed Worst in October 2026?

Communication Services was the weakest of the eleven sectors, falling 15.1% in October 2026. Information Technology was the only other sector to decline, losing 6.8%.

### Can the S&P 500 Fall While Most Sectors Rise?

Yes. Its market-value weighting lets declines in very large companies outweigh gains elsewhere. In October 2026, nine sectors rose while the index fell 0.9%.

### What Is Sector Rotation in Simple Terms?

It is a shift in which industry groups lead the market. Investors move money between sectors as their views on growth, rates and risk change.

### Should I Buy Last Month's Best Sector?

Past sector leadership does not predict future leadership. A strong month often reflects news already in prices. Any decision should follow a plan, not a single month's ranking.
