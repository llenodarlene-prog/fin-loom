# Money Market Account vs Money Market Fund: Insurance, Yield and Access

Two products share the words "money market," and they are easy to confuse. One is a bank deposit. The other is an investment. The money market account vs money market fund question comes down to who stands behind the money, how the yield is set and how quickly the cash can move.

A money market deposit account sits at a bank or credit union and can be covered by deposit insurance. A money market mutual fund is a security sold through a fund company or broker, and the FDIC states plainly that mutual funds are not insured deposits. This page compares the two with dated official figures and explains what each number does and does not tell a saver.

## Key Takeaways

- **Different products:** a money market deposit account is a bank deposit; a money market fund is a mutual fund that buys short-term debt.
- **Different protection:** the FDIC lists money market deposit accounts as insured deposit products and lists mutual funds, including money market funds, as not insured.
- **Bank rates are low on average:** the FDIC's national rate for money market deposit accounts was 0.63%, published October 19, 2026, from late-September data.
- **Funds hold large sums:** retail money market funds held about $3.04 trillion in the week of October 5, 2026, according to Federal Reserve data on FRED.
- **Funds carry a fee:** ICI put the 2025 asset-weighted expense ratio for money market funds at 0.24%, which is taken out before the yield is paid.

## What Is a Money Market Deposit Account?

A money market deposit account is an interest-bearing account at a bank or credit union. Many offer a debit card or a limited number of checks, and many set a higher minimum balance than a basic savings account. The interest rate is variable, so the institution can change it at any time.

Because it is a deposit, the account is covered by FDIC insurance at an insured bank, up to the standard limit. At a federally insured credit union, similar coverage comes from the National Credit Union Administration.

### How the Rate Is Set

The bank decides the rate. It may tier the rate by balance, offer a higher introductory rate or tie the rate to other relationships with the customer. Averages hide large differences, so the rate a specific account pays can sit far above or below the national figure.

### Typical Account Features

Money market deposit accounts often combine savings and limited spending. A saver might pay a large bill by check straight from the account. In exchange, some accounts charge a monthly fee when the balance drops below a set level, so the fee schedule deserves a careful read.

## What Is a Money Market Fund?

A money market fund is a type of mutual fund. It pools investors' money and buys short-term, high-quality debt, such as Treasury bills, government agency notes and commercial paper. Investors own fund shares rather than holding a deposit.

Most money market funds aim to keep a stable share price of $1.00, and the income from their holdings is paid out as dividends. A stable price is a goal, not a guarantee, which is the key difference from an insured deposit.

### Government, Prime and Tax-Exempt Funds

Government funds hold mostly Treasury and agency debt and repurchase agreements backed by them. Prime funds can also hold corporate debt such as commercial paper. Tax-exempt funds hold short-term municipal debt, which may pay interest free of federal income tax. Each type carries a different mix of credit risk and tax treatment.

### Where Investors Buy Them

Brokerage accounts often use a money market fund as the place where uninvested cash sits. Fund companies also sell them directly. A bank may offer them through an affiliated brokerage, which is one reason people mistake them for bank products.

## Is a Money Market Fund FDIC Insured?

No. The [FDIC's deposit insurance guide](https://www.fdic.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/deposit-insurance-at-a-glance-english.pdf) lists money market deposit accounts among covered deposit products. It lists mutual funds, which include money market funds, among products that are not insured, even when bought at an insured bank.

This is the heart of the money market account vs money market fund comparison. One is a deposit protected by a federal agency up to the limit. The other is an investment whose value depends on the securities the fund holds.

| Feature | Money market deposit account | Money market fund |
|---|---|---|
| Legal form | Bank or credit union deposit | Mutual fund shares |
| FDIC insurance | Yes, at insured banks, up to limits | No |
| Who sets the return | The bank | The fund's holdings, minus expenses |
| Price per unit | Not applicable; balance in dollars | Usually managed to $1.00, not guaranteed |
| Ongoing fee | Possible monthly account fee | Expense ratio inside the fund |
| Where bought | Bank or credit union | Fund company or brokerage |

Source: FDIC, Deposit Insurance at a Glance. Product features vary by provider.

### The Standard Coverage Limit

FDIC insurance covers $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category. Balances in several deposit accounts at the same bank under the same ownership category are added together. A product name or account nickname does not create extra coverage.

### What Protects Fund Investors

Money market funds are regulated securities and must follow rules on the quality, maturity and diversification of what they hold. Those rules aim to reduce risk; they do not insure the investor's principal. A brokerage account may also carry SIPC protection, but that covers missing assets after a broker fails, not a drop in a fund's value.

## What Do the Latest Rate Figures Show?

The FDIC publishes national average deposit rates every month. The figures below were published October 19, 2026, and reflect rates available on the last business day of September 2026. The FDIC weights each institution by its share of domestic deposits.

| Deposit product | FDIC national rate |
|---|---:|
| Interest checking | 0.07% |
| Savings | 0.38% |
| Money market deposit account | 0.63% |
| 6-month CD | 1.39% |
| 12-month CD | 1.76% |

Source: [FDIC National Rates and Rate Caps, October 2026](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/bankers/national-rates/). Savings and checking use the $2,500 tier; money market and CD rates average the $10,000 and $100,000 tiers.

### Why the Average Looks Low

The national rate is weighted by deposits, so the largest banks, many of which pay low rates on standing balances, carry the most weight. Online banks and credit unions often pay well above the average. A saver should compare specific offers rather than assume the average is what the market pays.

### What This Table Cannot Show

The FDIC does not publish a national money market fund yield, because funds are not deposits. Fund yields move with short-term interest rates and are reported by each fund, usually as a seven-day yield net of expenses. Comparing a fund with a bank account means checking both numbers on the same date.

## How Much Money Sits in Money Market Funds?

Money market funds hold far more money than many savers realize. Federal Reserve data published on [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WRMFNS) show retail money market funds held $3,037.0 billion in the week of October 5, 2026, not seasonally adjusted.

The Federal Reserve's financial accounts, also on FRED, put total financial assets of all money market funds at about $8.44 trillion in the second quarter of 2026. That figure includes institutional funds used by businesses and other large investors, not just households.

| Measure | Figure | Period |
|---|---:|---|
| Retail money market fund assets | $3,037.0 billion | Week of Oct. 5, 2026 |
| All money market funds, total financial assets | About $8.44 trillion | Q2 2026 |
| Money market fund expense ratio, asset-weighted | 0.24% | 2025 |
| Money market fund expense ratio, asset-weighted | 0.22% | 2024 |

Sources: Federal Reserve via FRED (WRMFNS, MMMFFAQ027S); Investment Company Institute.

### Why Savers Use Funds

Large balances suggest many savers and businesses see funds as a place to hold cash at a market-linked yield. Fund yields track short-term rates closely, while many bank rates move slowly. That gap is the main reason money has flowed toward funds when short-term rates are high.

### What the Fee Does to Yield

ICI reported that money market fund fees edged up from 0.22% in 2024 to 0.24% in 2025, partly as funds pared back fee waivers while rates were high. The fee is taken before the yield is paid, so a fund's reported net yield already reflects it.

## Which Pays More?

Neither product always pays more. The answer depends on the specific bank account, the specific fund and the date. The FDIC national average for money market deposit accounts is low, but individual banks may pay several times that rate, and fund yields fall when short-term rates fall.

A fair comparison uses the bank's APY and the fund's seven-day net yield on the same day, for the balance you expect to keep.

### A Labeled Example

Consider a hypothetical $20,000 balance held for one year with no withdrawals. At an assumed 0.63% APY, matching the FDIC national average, it would earn about $126. At an assumed 3.50% yield, used only as an illustration, it would earn about $700. These rates are not offers and will change.

### Fees and Minimums Change the Answer

A $15 monthly account fee costs $180 a year, more than the interest in the first case. A fund may require a minimum first purchase. Net income after fees and conditions, not the headline rate, is the figure to compare.

## How Fast Can You Get the Money?

A money market deposit account usually allows transfers to checking at the same bank right away. Moving money to another bank can take one or more business days. Some accounts let customers write checks or use a debit card, subject to the bank's limits.

A money market fund is sold like other fund shares. In a brokerage account, a sale usually settles within a business day, and the cash can then move out. Some funds can, under defined conditions in their rules, impose fees on large redemptions, which is worth checking in the prospectus.

### Planning for Emergencies

Emergency money should be available when a bill arrives. Either product can work for that purpose, but the saver should test how long a real transfer takes before relying on it. A short delay is easier to absorb for a planned expense than for an urgent repair.

### Cash Inside a Brokerage Account

Many investors already hold a money market fund as the default cash setting in a brokerage account. Others hold cash in a bank sweep program, which places deposits at partner banks. Checking which one applies tells you whether the cash is insured or invested.

## Taxes on Each Product

Interest from a money market deposit account is generally taxable as ordinary income. Dividends from most taxable money market funds are also taxed as ordinary income. Government funds that hold Treasury securities may pass through income that is exempt from state income tax, depending on the fund and the state.

Tax-exempt municipal money market funds pay income that may be free of federal tax. Their yields are usually lower, so the benefit depends on the investor's tax bracket. A tax adviser can help with a specific situation.

### Reading the Year-End Form

A bank reports deposit interest on Form 1099-INT. A fund reports dividends on Form 1099-DIV, often with a breakdown of income from U.S. government securities. Those breakdowns can help with a state return. Keeping the forms together each year makes it easier to see how much each product earned after tax.

### Comparing After-Tax Yields

For a saver in a high-tax state, a government fund's state-tax exemption can narrow or reverse a gap with a bank account. The comparison works only when both yields are converted to after-tax figures using the same assumptions. A rough check is to multiply each yield by one minus the tax rate that applies to it.

### Questions to Ask Before Choosing

Is the money an emergency reserve or a planned expense? Is deposit insurance important for this balance? How quickly can money move to checking? What fees and minimums apply? What is the after-tax yield on the same date? Answers to those questions usually settle the money market account vs money market fund decision.

### Splitting the Balance

Some savers keep a small emergency buffer in an insured account and hold additional cash in a fund. That approach trades some yield for certainty on the first tranche. It is one option among several and depends on personal needs.

## Common Mistakes in the Money Market Account vs Money Market Fund Choice

The most common mistake in the money market account vs money market fund decision is assuming both are insured because both carry the same label. Only the deposit account is. A second mistake is comparing a fund's current yield with a bank's average rate from a different month.

### Comparing Different Dates

Rates on both products move. A money market account vs money market fund comparison is only fair when both numbers come from the same week. Write down the date next to each rate, and recheck both before moving a large balance, since a bank can change its rate overnight.

### Ignoring Balance Tiers

Bank rates often step up at higher balances, and the FDIC averages mix the $10,000 and $100,000 tiers. Compare the rate for the balance you will hold. In any money market account vs money market fund review, the tier you qualify for can change the answer more than the label does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Money Market Fund FDIC Insured?

No. The FDIC lists mutual funds, including money market funds, as products that are not insured, even when bought through an insured bank or its brokerage arm.

### Can a Money Market Fund Lose Money?

It can. Most funds aim to keep a $1.00 share price, but that goal is not guaranteed. Fund value depends on the short-term securities the fund holds.

### What Was the Average Money Market Account Rate in 2026?

The FDIC national rate for money market deposit accounts was 0.63%, published October 19, 2026, using late-September data. Individual banks can pay far more or less.

### Which Is Better for an Emergency Fund?

It depends on access, insurance needs and fees. An insured bank account gives principal protection up to limits, while a fund offers a market-linked yield without insurance.

### How Is a Money Market Fund Taxed?

Dividends from most taxable money market funds count as ordinary income. Government funds may pass through income exempt from state tax, and municipal funds may avoid federal tax.
