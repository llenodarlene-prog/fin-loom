# Market Breadth Statistics: Why the S&P 500 Hid October 2026's Rotation

When the S&P 500 closed October 2026 down 0.9%, the headline hid a sharp improvement underneath. The equal-weight version of the index rose 5.0%, and far more of its members ended the month above their short- and long-term moving averages than a month earlier.

That is what market breadth measures. It answers a question a headline index cannot: how many stocks are taking part in a move? This page explains the main breadth measures, sets out the dated October 2026 readings, and shows why a falling index and improving participation can happen in the same month. It also covers the limits of breadth, including why better participation in October did not prevent the broad selloff on November 5. Data runs through the November 6, 2026 close.

## Key Takeaways

- **Participation jumped in October:** 61% of S&P 500 members were above their 50-day moving average at October month-end, up from 21% in September, according to Nasdaq.
- **The longer view improved too:** 62% were above their 200-day average, up from 43%.
- **Equal weight beat the index:** the equal-weight S&P 500 rose 5.0% while the standard S&P 500 fell 0.9%, a 5.9-point gap.
- **Nine sectors rose:** only Communication Services and Information Technology declined in October.
- **Breadth is not a forecast:** better participation in October did not stop a 4.4% one-day S&P 500 drop on November 5.

## What Does Market Breadth Measure?

Market breadth describes how widely a move is shared among the stocks in a market or index. If an index rises because most of its members rise, breadth is strong. If it rises because a few large companies rise while most others fall, breadth is weak.

Common breadth measures include the number of advancing versus declining stocks each day, new highs versus new lows, the percentage of stocks above a set moving average, and the gap between equal-weighted and market-value-weighted returns. Each looks at participation from a different angle.

### Related Measures, Different Windows

These measures are related but not the same. An advance-decline count covers one day. A moving-average measure summarizes where prices sit relative to many past sessions. An equal-weight comparison shows how index design changes the combined result. One reading cannot stand in for the others, so each should be labeled.

### Why Breadth Exists as a Concept

Market-value-weighted indexes, such as the S&P 500, give the largest companies the most influence. When a handful of companies make up a big share of an index, the index can move in a direction that most of its members are not following. Breadth measures were developed to see past that effect.

## What Happened to Market Breadth in October 2026?

The table shows two moving-average breadth measures for S&P 500 members at the end of September and October 2026. The change column is our calculation from the published percentages.

| Measure | September 30, 2026 | October 30, 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 members above 50-day moving average | 21% | 61% | +40 points |
| S&P 500 members above 200-day moving average | 43% | 62% | +19 points |

The figures come from Nasdaq's October 2026 review, published November 2. They measure the share of index members trading above each moving average on the last trading day of each month. They are not daily advance-decline counts.

### Reading the 50-Day Jump

A move from 21% to 61% in one month is large. It means that by the end of October, most S&P 500 members had prices above their average of the previous 50 trading days, after a September in which most did not. That points to a broad short-term recovery among individual stocks.

### Reading the 200-Day Change

The 200-day measure moves more slowly because it averages a longer period. A rise from 43% to 62% suggests improvement over a longer stretch, with most members above their longer-term average by month-end. Together, the two measures show participation spreading in both the short and longer term.

## Why Did the S&P 500 Give a Different Impression?

The standard S&P 500 and its equal-weight version hold the same companies but weight them differently. The table compares their October 2026 returns, which come from Janus Henderson's October 2026 market review.

| October 2026 index | Monthly return |
|---|---|
| S&P 500, market-value weighted | -0.9% |
| S&P 500 Equal Weight | +5.0% |
| Gap, equal weight minus market-value weight | +5.9 points |

J.P. Morgan Asset Management's [October market review](https://am.jpmorgan.com/fr/en/asset-management/liq/insights/market-insights/market-updates/monthly-market-review/) reports the same two figures. Janus Henderson described the gap as the equal-weight index's biggest monthly lead since April 2009.

### Why the Gap Opened

The standard index gives larger companies larger weights. Equal weighting resets every member to a similar weight at each rebalance. When a handful of large growth companies fell in October, the standard index absorbed most of that pressure, while the equal-weight index reflected gains spread across many more companies.

### What the Gap Does Not Mean

A 5.9-point gap is not a free profit an investor could have captured by switching funds. Real portfolios face trading costs, fund fees, tracking differences and different risks. The gap shows that the spread of returns across members shaped the result, not that one approach is always better.

## A Simple Example of Narrow and Broad Moves

A small made-up index shows how breadth and weighting interact. These numbers are invented for illustration and do not describe any real index.

Imagine an index of ten stocks. Two large companies each make up 30% of its value, and eight smaller companies each make up 5%. In one month, the two large companies fall 10% and the eight smaller ones each rise 5%.

| Group | Weight | Monthly change | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two large stocks | 60% | -10% | -6.0 points |
| Eight smaller stocks | 40% | +5% | +2.0 points |
| Market-value-weighted index | 100% | Weighted average | -4.0% |
| Equal-weighted index | 10% each | Simple average | +2.0% |

In this example, eight of ten stocks rose, yet the weighted index fell 4.0%. The equal-weighted index, giving each stock 10%, rose 2.0%: two stocks at -10% and eight at +5% average out to +2.0%. Breadth was strong, but the headline was negative. October 2026 followed a similar pattern on a much larger scale.

## How Breadth Looked Across Index Sizes

Breadth can also be seen by comparing indexes of different sizes and styles. In October 2026, Janus Henderson reported the Russell 2000 of smaller companies rose 0.6%, and Nasdaq's review reported the Dow rose 1.8%. Both did better than the S&P 500's 0.9% decline.

Janus Henderson also reported the Russell 1000 Value Index rose 4.4% while the Russell 1000 Growth Index fell 6.1%. That 10.5-point gap is another sign that October's weakness was concentrated in large growth companies rather than spread across the market.

## What Do the 50-Day and 200-Day Measures Tell Us?

A 50-day moving average reacts to recent price changes faster than a 200-day average. When more stocks trade above their 50-day line, short-term participation has improved under that definition. A rising share above the 200-day line suggests improvement over a longer window.

There are limits. A stock can sit above its moving average after a short bounce while still trading well below a past peak. The average itself changes every day, and index membership changes can affect comparisons. These measures also say nothing about earnings, debt or valuation.

### The Strongest Signal in October

The most informative point in October was that both short and longer participation measures improved while the familiar index was weak. That combination says more than either measure alone. It showed a market where weakness was concentrated in a few large names while most stocks gained ground.

### What Moving Averages Leave Out

A moving average only uses past prices. It does not know about upcoming earnings, policy changes or shifts in interest rates. That is why breadth readings describe the recent past well and predict the future poorly, and why they work best as one input among several.

## Can Market Breadth and Sector Rotation Tell Different Stories?

They can. Sector leadership compares industry groups. Breadth asks how widely gains or losses are shared among individual stocks. A sector can rise because its largest stock soared while many smaller members fell. A sector can also fall even if most of its members gained, if one dominant stock dropped enough.

In October 2026, the two views agreed. Janus Henderson reported that nine of eleven S&P 500 sectors gained, and the moving-average readings showed most members above their averages. Those are separate statistics, though, and should stay labeled separately. FinLoom's [sector rotation statistics](/markets-stocks/sector-rotation-october-2026-statistics/) set out all eleven sector returns.

## What Happened to Breadth After October?

Improved breadth in October did not protect the market from a broad selloff. On November 5, 2026, the S&P 500 fell about 4.4% in one session. FRED's [S&P 500 series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500) shows the index closing at 7,353.73, down from 7,688.62 the day before. The Associated Press reported that the Russell 2000 of smaller companies fell 4.4% the same day.

That session showed how quickly participation can turn. A broad decline pulls most stocks down together, which lowers breadth readings as well as index levels. Month-end breadth figures for November will only be available after the month closes.

### Volatility Rose at the Same Time

Options-based volatility jumped on November 5 as well. FinLoom's [VIX index statistics](/markets-stocks/vix-index-statistics-2026/) show the index rising from 16.61 to 28.18 between the November 4 and November 5 closes. Breadth and volatility measure different things, but both shifted sharply in the same session.

### Why One Month Does Not Set a Trend

October's improvement was real under the measures used. It described one month. Breadth can strengthen and weaken several times within a year, and a strong reading in one month does not commit the market to anything in the next, in either direction.

## How Can Investors Use Market Breadth Without Overreading It?

Breadth works best as context. It shows whether an index move is broad or narrow, and whether participation is spreading or shrinking. It does not reliably identify the next market peak, guarantee a rally or tell anyone which stock to buy.

A practical review might compare an index's return with its equal-weight version, check the share of stocks above key moving averages, look at how widely sector returns are spread, and then compare all of that with the concentration of your own portfolio. Each step answers a different question.

### Checking Your Own Portfolio's Breadth

The same idea applies to a personal portfolio. If most of your gains or losses come from two or three holdings, your portfolio's breadth is narrow, whatever the number of positions. Looking at how much each holding contributed over a period shows how concentrated your results really are.

### Where to Find Breadth Data

Many charting tools and broker platforms show the share of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day and 200-day averages. FRED publishes daily index closes, and fund providers publish equal-weight index returns. Note the date and definition of each figure before comparing them. ## A Short Glossary of Market Breadth Terms

Breadth discussions use a handful of terms that are easy to confuse. The definitions below are general and may vary slightly between data providers.

### Advance-Decline Line

The advance-decline line adds up the daily difference between rising and falling stocks over time. When it rises alongside an index, participation is broad. When an index rises while the line falls, fewer stocks are taking part in the gain.

### New Highs and New Lows

This measure counts how many stocks reach a new 52-week high or low each day. A market making new highs while few stocks do the same can signal narrow leadership. Many new lows during a decline suggest broad selling rather than trouble in a few names.

## Why Market Breadth Draws Attention During Selloffs

Interest in market breadth tends to rise when the headline index and the average stock tell different stories. During long rallies led by a few companies, observers watch breadth for signs that leadership is too narrow. During selloffs, they watch it to see whether losses are spreading or staying contained.

October 2026 was an unusual case: the headline index fell while breadth improved. That is the opposite of the more familiar pattern, in which a rising index masks weak participation. Both patterns show the same underlying point: a single index level is an incomplete summary of what most stocks are doing.

### What to Watch at the End of November

The next useful reading will come at November month-end, when the share of S&P 500 members above their 50-day and 200-day averages can be compared with October's 61% and 62%. Until then, any statement about November breadth is based on partial-month data and should be labeled that way.

### Pairing Breadth With Fundamentals

Breadth shows how many stocks are moving. It does not show whether those companies are earning more, carrying too much debt or trading at high valuations. Pairing breadth readings with company-level analysis gives a fuller picture than either one alone.

## Common Mistakes With Market Breadth

The first mistake is treating breadth as a timing signal. Strong breadth in October 2026 did not prevent the November 5 selloff. The second is mixing measures, such as quoting a daily advance-decline count as if it were a month-end moving-average reading.

The third is assuming equal weight will always beat market-value weight after a period of narrow leadership. The fourth is forgetting that breadth uses prices only. It does not show whether the companies participating are financially strong. Keeping these limits in mind makes any market breadth figure easier to interpret.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Was Market Breadth Strong in October 2026?

By Nasdaq's moving-average measures, yes. At October month-end, 61% of S&P 500 members were above their 50-day average, up from 21% in September.

### What Is the Difference Between Equal Weight and Market-Cap Weight?

Equal weighting gives each member similar influence at rebalance. Market-cap weighting gives larger companies more influence, so a few large stocks can move the whole index.

### Is Strong Market Breadth a Buy Signal?

Not on its own. Breadth describes participation but not valuation, earnings or the next market move. Broad gains in October 2026 were followed by a sharp November selloff.

### What Is Market Breadth in Simple Terms?

It measures how many stocks take part in a market move. Broad participation means most stocks move together; narrow participation means a few large stocks drive the index.

### Why Did the Equal-Weight S&P 500 Beat the Index in October?

A few very large technology companies fell, dragging the market-cap-weighted index down. Most other members rose, which lifted the equal-weight index by 5.0%.
