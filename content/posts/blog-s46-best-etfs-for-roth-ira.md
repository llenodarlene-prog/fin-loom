# Best ETFs for Roth IRA Accounts in 2026: Six Funds for Different Portfolio Roles

A Roth IRA is a tax-advantaged account, not an investment. Inside it, you can hold diversified stock and bond ETFs, subject to your broker's lineup and the IRS rules. So the useful question is not which ETF will perform best, but which mix of funds belongs in the account given your age, time horizon and tolerance for losses.

The best ETFs for Roth IRA portfolios are the ones that each do a clear job. The six funds below illustrate six roles: broad U.S. stocks, an S&P 500 core, international stocks, whole-world stocks, U.S. bonds and international bonds. They are not meant to be bought together. Several overlap heavily, and choosing between them counts for more than adding another ticker. None of this is a recommendation to buy a specific fund.

## Key Takeaways

- VTI covers the broad U.S. stock market with 3,519 stocks, while VOO focuses on the large companies in the S&P 500.
- VXUS adds stocks outside the United States, and VT already combines U.S. and foreign stocks in one fund.
- BND and BNDX add bond exposure, which can soften stock swings but does not remove the risk of loss.
- All six list expense ratios between 0.03% and 0.07%, so overlap and allocation count for more than cost.
- The 2026 IRA limit is $7,500, or $8,600 at age 50 or older, and Roth eligibility phases out at higher incomes.

## VTI: Broad U.S. Stock Market Exposure

VTI, now named Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF, tracks the Morningstar US Total Market Index. [Vanguard's VTI fact sheet](https://fund-docs.vanguard.com/F0970.pdf) for September 30, 2026 shows 3,519 stocks in the fund, which reaches well beyond the large companies in the S&P 500.

Its 0.03% expense ratio keeps ongoing costs low, and its breadth gives exposure to companies at many stages of growth. That makes it a common core holding for someone who wants the whole U.S. market in one fund.

VTI is still U.S. stock risk. It can fall sharply in a broad selloff, and a portfolio made only of VTI has no international stocks and no bonds. It works as a building block when you plan to add other funds on purpose, not as a promise of safety.

## VOO: A Focused U.S. Large-Company Core

VOO tracks the S&P 500 and lists a 0.03% expense ratio. It is a familiar way to own large U.S. companies and suits someone who wants to measure the U.S. stock part of a portfolio against the S&P 500. Almost every broker supports it.

VOO and VTI overlap heavily, because every S&P 500 company is also held inside VTI. Buying both mainly shifts more weight onto large U.S. stocks. If you already hold VTI, a second broad U.S. fund adds little.

FinLoom's guide to the [best S&P 500 index fund](/investing/best-sp500-index-funds/) choices compares VOO with similar S&P 500 funds from Fidelity, BlackRock and State Street, including how small fee gaps translate into dollars.

## VXUS: International Stocks Beyond the U.S.

VXUS tracks the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index and lists a 0.05% expense ratio. Its fact sheet showed 8,901 stocks on September 30, 2026, with Japan as the largest country at 16.1% of the stock portfolio. The fund covers developed and emerging markets.

In a portfolio built on VTI or VOO, VXUS adds geographic diversification without picking individual countries or companies. International stocks bring currency, political and market risks, and their returns can differ from U.S. stocks for many years.

That difference is the point of the fund. International diversification exists so that your whole stock portfolio does not depend on the results of one country. Whether that suits you depends on how much you want to rely on U.S. markets alone. If you hold VTI and VXUS together, you control the split between U.S. and foreign stocks yourself, and you can rebalance back to it once a year or whenever the weights drift far from your target.

## VT: A Global Stock Fund That Cuts Maintenance

VT combines U.S. and international stocks in one fund and lists a 0.06% expense ratio. It tracks the FTSE Global All Cap Index, held 10,185 stocks on September 30, 2026, and had $101,182 million in total net assets.

On that date, U.S. stocks made up 62.4% of VT's stock portfolio, followed by Japan at 6.0% and the United Kingdom at 3.1%. Those weights shift as markets move, because VT follows each country's share of global market value.

Investors who do not want to rebalance between U.S. and foreign stocks may prefer one global fund, especially in the early years of funding an IRA. VT usually replaces a separate U.S. fund and international fund rather than being added to both. It holds no bonds, so a 100% VT account stays fully exposed to stock swings.

## BND: A Broad U.S. Bond Component

BND holds U.S. investment-grade bonds and lists a 0.03% expense ratio. Its fact sheet showed 11,434 bonds on September 30, 2026, with an average duration of 5.7 years and an average effective maturity of 8.3 years. It pays dividends monthly.

In a Roth IRA, BND can form part of a lower-volatility mix for someone who wants to soften the swings of a stock-heavy portfolio. It spreads money across many issuers and maturities rather than a single Treasury bond.

Bond funds can lose value when interest rates rise. A duration of 5.7 years means the fund's price would be expected to fall roughly 5.7% for a one-percentage-point rise in rates, all else equal. That is an approximation, not a forecast. Credit risk also remains, even with investment-grade holdings.

## BNDX: International Bond Diversification

BNDX holds bonds issued outside the United States and lists a 0.07% expense ratio. Its benchmark is a hedged index, which means the fund aims to reduce the effect of currency swings on returns. The fact sheet showed 6,837 bonds and an average duration of 6.4 years on September 30, 2026.

BNDX is most relevant after the basic split between stocks and bonds is settled, for someone who wants the bond portion itself to be globally diversified. For a small Roth IRA, it may add complexity without changing much.

A simpler portfolio is not automatically worse. The aim is for each holding to fill a role the other funds do not already cover. If BND already gives you the bond exposure you want, BNDX is optional.

## Three Illustrative Structures, Not Prescribed Portfolios

A reader building a diversified Roth IRA can start by deciding how much stock risk to take, rather than starting with a ticker. These three structures show how the funds can fit together. The weights are left open on purpose.

### One-Fund Global Stock Approach

This structure uses VT for worldwide stocks and adds a bond fund only if appropriate. It is the simplest to maintain, because the fund rebalances between countries on its own. The tradeoff is less control over how much of the portfolio sits in U.S. stocks, which was 62.4% at the end of September.

### Two-Fund Global Stock Approach

This structure pairs VTI with VXUS, so you set the U.S. and international shares yourself. It costs about the same as VT, but you rebalance between the two funds when their weights drift. Some investors prefer that control, while others find the extra decision a reason to stop investing regularly.

### Stock and Bond Approach

This structure combines a global stock fund, or VTI with VXUS, with BND and, if you want, BNDX. The bond share softens the swings of the stock share, and it gives you something to sell or rebalance from when stocks fall. How large that bond share should be depends on your time horizon and how you react to losses.

These are structural examples, not recommended allocations. The right proportions depend on when you might need the money, your other retirement savings and how you react to losses. Mixing VOO, VTI, VT and VXUS without checking overlap creates a cluttered portfolio still dominated by the same large U.S. companies.

## Do Low ETF Fees Count for Much Inside a Roth IRA?

They do, but only after the allocation makes sense. A 0.03% expense ratio is about $3 a year per $10,000 of average assets. A 0.07% ratio is about $7. Neither figure is a reason to ignore a fund's market exposure or risk.

Roth accounts suit assets with long growth horizons, because qualified withdrawals are tax-free. That does not mean the most volatile fund automatically belongs there. Tax advantages do not change the basics of diversification and risk control.

Some investors also like dividend-paying funds inside a Roth because dividends are not taxed each year in the account. FinLoom's explainer on [dividend and dividend yield](/investing/dividend-vs-dividend-yield/) shows how yield is calculated and why a high yield alone says little about a fund's total return.

## Roth IRA Rules Worth Knowing in 2026

The [IRS IRA contribution limits page](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-ira-contribution-limits) says that for 2026, total contributions to all your traditional and Roth IRAs cannot exceed $7,500, or $8,600 if you are 50 or older, or your taxable compensation if that is less. Roth eligibility also phases out at higher incomes, so the limit is not guaranteed for everyone.

[The IRS notes](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/saving-for-retirement-iras-are-an-option) that Roth contributions are made with after-tax money and are not deductible, qualified distributions are tax-free, and there is no required minimum distribution for the account owner. Withdrawals of contributions and earnings follow different ordering and qualification rules, so check them before you take money out.

## Stocks, Bonds and Other Assets in an IRA

Every fund on this list holds stocks or bonds. Some brokers also let you hold other assets in an IRA, including crypto through separate products, but those carry very different risks from a diversified ETF.

## How to Choose Among the Best ETFs for Roth IRA Accounts

The best ETFs for Roth IRA accounts are easier to choose once you answer a few questions in order. Working through them before you pick a ticker keeps the portfolio simple and stops you from buying overlapping funds.

### Start With Your Time Horizon

Decide roughly when you might need the money. A Roth IRA you will not touch for 30 years can usually hold more stocks than one you may draw from in five. That single answer shapes the split between stock funds such as VTI, VXUS or VT and bond funds such as BND or BNDX far more than any expense ratio does.

### Then Pick One Fund per Job

Once the split is set, choose one fund for each job: one for U.S. stocks or global stocks, one for international stocks if you are not using VT, and one for bonds if you want them. Among the best ETFs for Roth IRA portfolios, picking one per job avoids paying twice for the same companies and makes rebalancing easy to understand.

### Check What Your Broker Offers

Finally, confirm your broker offers the funds, supports recurring purchases and lets you buy fractional shares if you invest small amounts. A fund that is hard to buy on a schedule can lead to missed contributions, which costs far more over time than a few basis points of fees.

## Picking Fewer Funds Can Be a Stronger Decision

The value of these ETFs comes from giving each one a job, not from collecting six tickers. A U.S. core, an international stock fund and a suitably sized bond fund may already provide broad diversification. A single global stock fund can be an even simpler start for someone whose risk tolerance supports it.

Whatever structure you choose should be easy to explain, easy to fund every month and easy to hold through market declines. The best ETFs for Roth IRA accounts are the ones you will keep buying through good and bad markets, because steady contributions do most of the work over decades.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I Buy ETFs Inside a Roth IRA?

Yes, if your account provider offers the ETF. A Roth IRA is an account structure, and eligible investments can include ETFs, mutual funds and other assets your provider permits.

### Is VTI or VOO Better for a Roth IRA?

VTI covers the broad U.S. market, while VOO tracks the large companies in the S&P 500. Their holdings overlap heavily, so the choice depends on your allocation preferences.

### Do I Need Both VTI and VT?

Usually not. VT already holds U.S. stocks, which made up 62.4% of its stock portfolio in September 2026. Holding both mainly shifts your portfolio toward U.S. companies.

### Are Bond ETFs Appropriate in a Roth IRA?

They can be. Bond funds are used to manage portfolio risk and cash needs, but rising interest rates can reduce bond fund values, even inside a tax-advantaged retirement account.

### What Is the Roth IRA Contribution Limit in 2026?

The total IRA limit for 2026 is $7,500, or $8,600 at age 50 or older, capped at taxable compensation. Roth eligibility also phases out at higher income levels each year.

### What Are the Best ETFs for Roth IRA Beginners?

A single global stock fund such as VT, or VTI paired with VXUS, keeps things simple. Bond funds can be added later as your time horizon and risk tolerance change.
