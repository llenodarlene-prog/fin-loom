# Why Are Tech Stocks Down? Nasdaq and AI Selloff Statistics for 2026

The 2026 technology selloff was not a simple verdict that artificial intelligence had stopped growing. Investors were asking something narrower: how much future profit would justify the money already being spent on chips, power and data centers. That distinction explains how strong company earnings and falling share prices can happen at the same time.

If you are asking why are tech stocks down, start with what the market did. In October 2026, the Nasdaq Composite lost 5.3% and the S&P 500 Information Technology sector lost 6.8%, while the equal-weight S&P 500 gained 5.0%. Then on November 5, the Nasdaq fell another 5.5% in one session. This page sets out the figures, dates each one, and explains what investors said they were worried about. Data runs through the November 6, 2026 close.

## Key Takeaways

- **Tech led October's losses:** the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% and the Nasdaq-100 about 6.6% in October 2026, while the equal-weight S&P 500 rose 5.0%.
- **Chips fell hardest:** the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 15.3% in October, according to Janus Henderson.
- **Communication Services fell further:** that sector, home to several large digital platforms, lost 15.1% in October.
- **November 5 deepened the slide:** the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% and the Nasdaq-100 about 6.4% in one session.
- **Earnings stayed strong:** Nasdaq's review reported about 30.2% blended S&P 500 earnings growth with roughly 60% of companies reported by October 30.

## How Much Did Technology Stocks Decline in October?

Several benchmarks track technology, and they did not fall by the same amount. The table compares them over the same month, September 30 to October 30, 2026.

| Benchmark | October 2026 return | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Nasdaq Composite | -5.3% | All Nasdaq-listed stocks, heavy in technology |
| Nasdaq-100 | -6.6% | 100 large non-financial Nasdaq companies |
| S&P 500 Information Technology | -6.8% | Large U.S. technology sector |
| S&P 500 Communication Services | -15.1% | Sector holding major digital platforms |
| Philadelphia Semiconductor Index | -15.3% | Semiconductor companies |
| S&P 500 Equal Weight | +5.0% | Same large companies, equal influence |

The Nasdaq Composite and sector figures come from Janus Henderson's October 2026 market review, published November 2. The Nasdaq-100 figure is our calculation from the [Nasdaq-100 series on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQ100), which fell from 30,408.50 on September 30 to 28,384.85 on October 30, a 6.65% price decline.

### Monthly Figures Are Not Daily Lows

These are month-end to month-end changes. Prices moved more within the month. FRED's Nasdaq Composite series shows a record close of 27,599.89 on October 6, so the drop from that peak to October 30 was larger than the monthly figure suggests. A monthly return and a peak-to-trough decline answer different questions.

### Sectors Are Not the Same as Indexes

The sector rows are not interchangeable with the Nasdaq Composite, which has its own membership and weighting. Some large platform companies are classified in Communication Services or Consumer Discretionary rather than Information Technology. That is why "tech stocks," "AI stocks" and "the Nasdaq" should not be treated as one thing.

## Why Did Investors Question AI Spending?

An AI product can gain users quickly without every company behind it earning a good return on the infrastructure. Cloud platforms may commit to years of spending on processors, networking gear, electricity and buildings before that demand turns into steady cash flow.

Nasdaq's October review described a shift in investor attention from headline AI earnings growth toward capital spending, financing and free cash flow. J.P. Morgan Asset Management's [October market review](https://am.jpmorgan.com/fr/en/asset-management/liq/insights/market-insights/market-updates/monthly-market-review/) pointed to the same concern, citing worries over the scale of upfront capital committed to data centers alongside growing Chinese competition.

### A Valuation Question, Not a Demand Verdict

If more cash must be reinvested to deliver the same expected growth, investors may pay a lower multiple for each dollar of earnings. Higher borrowing costs can also lower the present value of profits expected years from now. Neither point proves AI spending will fail. They describe a market that became less willing to treat every dollar of spending as automatically valuable.

### What J.P. Morgan Reported About Individual Names

J.P. Morgan listed October declines for several large technology-related companies: Nvidia fell 10.4%, Broadcom 7.2%, Alphabet 18.7% and Meta 15.2%. Those are monthly moves for individual stocks in October 2026. They show how widely the repricing reached across chip makers and platform companies.

## Why Did the Nasdaq Fall More Than the Wider Market?

The Nasdaq's heavy exposure to large growth companies amplified the decline. Janus Henderson reported that the Russell 1000 Value Index rose 4.4% in October while the Russell 1000 Growth Index fell 6.1%, a gap of 10.5 percentage points. The market rewarded utilities and consumer staples while marking down high-growth platforms.

The equal-weight S&P 500's 5.0% gain against the standard index's 0.9% loss shows the same pattern from another angle. When a few very large technology companies fall, market-value-weighted indexes absorb most of the damage. FinLoom's [stock market crash 2026 statistics](/markets-stocks/stock-market-crash-2026-statistics/) show how that gap played out across every major index.

### The Same Companies Appear Everywhere

Many investors hold the same handful of large technology companies through several funds at once. An S&P 500 fund, a Nasdaq-100 fund and a technology fund may overlap heavily. When those companies fall together, holdings that look diversified by name can fall together too.

### Growth Versus Value in One Month

A 10.5-point gap between growth and value in a single month is wide. It reflects a sharp change in which kinds of companies investors favored. It does not mean growth investing has stopped working or that value will keep leading. Leadership between styles has changed many times before.

## What Changed on November 5?

The repricing intensified after the U.S. midterm elections. The Associated Press reported a 5.5% decline in the Nasdaq Composite on November 5, and FRED's [Nasdaq Composite series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM) confirms it: the index closed at 24,315.00, down from 25,736.51 the day before. The Nasdaq-100 fell about 6.4% the same day, from 28,437.17 to 26,629.81.

Reuters reported that investors were weighing possible limits on data-center expansion and the effect of financing conditions on AI projects. Any specific rules, their timing and their effects were still uncertain at the time. A political development can shift expectations without confirming a final policy.

### The Selloff Reached Beyond Software and Chips

Data centers need power equipment, property, cooling systems and financing. A concern about construction can therefore reach companies that would not appear on a list of pure AI stocks. That helps explain why November 5 losses spread to the Dow and to smaller companies, not only the Nasdaq.

### Why One Announcement Rarely Explains Everything

It is tempting to say one headline "caused" the selloff. Markets price several risks at once, including valuation, interest rates, policy and competition. A falling price does not come with a breakdown of which factor did what. Reported causes describe what investors discussed, not a measured split of the decline.

## A Timeline of the 2026 Tech Selloff

Putting the dates in order makes the sequence easier to follow. Each step below uses closing prices or published monthly figures, so readers can match them to the tables above.

October 6, 2026 marked the Nasdaq Composite's record close of 27,599.89 on FRED. Through the rest of October, technology and communication stocks fell while energy, staples, utilities and health care rose, ending with the Nasdaq down 5.3% for the month. Early November brought mixed sessions, with the Nasdaq rising on November 4 to 25,736.51. On November 5, the index fell 5.5% to 24,315.00, leaving it 11.9% below its October record. On November 6, it recovered about 1.1% to 24,579.42.

That sequence is the short answer to why are tech stocks down as of early November 2026: a month of rotation away from large technology names, followed by one sharp session that pushed the Nasdaq into a correction. What comes next is not known.

## Can Strong Earnings and Falling Stock Prices Happen Together?

Yes. A share price reflects both expected future results and what investors will pay for them today. If earnings rise but expectations were even higher, the price can fall. If a company raises its spending plans faster than its expected cash generation, investors may question when the returns will arrive.

Nasdaq's October review reported that by October 30, roughly 60% of S&P 500 companies had reported quarterly results and blended year-over-year earnings growth was about 30.2%. That strong reading did not stop technology shares from falling. It shows the gap that can open between operating results and how the market values them.

### What a Company-Level Check Looks Like

A sector-wide drop cannot replace company analysis. A more useful approach compares one company's revenue, capital spending, operating cash flow and free cash flow over several consistent quarters, alongside management's stated investment timeline. FinLoom's guide to [stock analysis fundamentals](/markets-stocks/stock-analysis-fundamentals/) explains how to read those figures.

### Why Free Cash Flow Drew Attention

Free cash flow is the cash left after a company pays for its operations and capital spending. When capital spending rises faster than operating cash, free cash flow shrinks, even if reported earnings grow. That is one reason investors began watching spending plans as closely as profits during the 2026 selloff.

## The Tech Selloff Outside the United States

The October repricing was not only a U.S. story. J.P. Morgan reported that technology-heavy Asian markets fell sharply: Korea lost 15.6% and Taiwan 8.4% in U.S. dollar terms, and MSCI Asia ex-Japan fell 8.2%. It linked those moves to concerns about the scale of AI investment and growing Chinese competition.

Those markets hold large chip and hardware makers that supply U.S. technology companies. When investors doubt the pace of data-center spending, suppliers abroad can fall as much as the U.S. buyers. Anyone asking why are tech stocks down in 2026 should look at the global supply chain, not only U.S. indexes.

### Bonds Moved the Other Way

While technology shares fell, government bonds rallied. J.P. Morgan reported that U.S. government bonds returned 2.4% in October as yields eased from September's highs. Janus Henderson noted the Federal Reserve left its policy rate unchanged at 3.75% to 4.00%. Falling yields usually help growth stocks, so October's tech decline happened despite, not because of, the move in rates.

### Why That Contrast Is Useful

The bond rally suggests investors were moving toward safer assets as stock volatility rose. It also shows that the tech selloff was not mainly a reaction to higher interest rates, a common explanation in past downturns. The focus was on AI spending and its payoff, which is a different kind of risk.

## How the Chip Slump Fits the Bigger Picture

Semiconductors fell further than most technology benchmarks in October. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index's 15.3% decline was about 10 points worse than the Nasdaq Composite's 5.3% loss. Chip makers, equipment suppliers and memory producers all depend on the pace of data-center spending, so they reacted strongly to doubts about it.

FinLoom's [semiconductor stock statistics](/markets-stocks/semiconductor-stocks-october-2026-statistics/) look at why different parts of the chip industry face different risks, and why an index decline does not mean every chip company fell by the same amount.

## What the Selloff Means for Diversified Investors

A diversified portfolio may still carry large indirect exposure to a small group of technology companies. Market-value-weighted funds give a company more weight as its value grows. After years in which a few companies supplied much of the market's gains, many broad funds became more concentrated than their names suggest.

The response is not necessarily to abandon growth investing. It is to understand what drives a portfolio. Checking the top holdings of each fund you own, and how much they overlap, shows how much of your money depends on the same few companies. The questions here are general, not personal advice. Another useful check is time horizon. Money needed within a few years is more exposed to a long technology slump than money set aside for decades, so the same selloff can carry very different weight for different goals.

## How to Track the Tech Selloff Yourself

Figures change daily, so it helps to know where to check them. Two free tools cover most of what a reader needs to answer why are tech stocks down on any given day.

### Use Daily Closes, Not Headlines

FRED publishes daily closes for the Nasdaq Composite, the Nasdaq-100 and the S&P 500. Comparing two closes gives a clean percentage change for any window you choose. That avoids mixing intraday moves, which news reports sometimes quote, with closing figures.

### Compare Against an Equal-Weight Benchmark

Comparing the standard S&P 500 with its equal-weight version shows whether losses are concentrated in large companies or spread across many. In October 2026, the gap was 5.9 points, a clear sign that the decline sat in a small group of large technology stocks.

## Common Mistakes When Asking Why Are Tech Stocks Down

The first mistake is treating every technology benchmark as the same. In October, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% while Communication Services fell 15.1%. The second is assuming a reported cause is proven. News reports describe what investors discussed, not a measured breakdown.

The third is reading a price decline as a verdict on demand. AI demand and AI stock prices can move in different directions over short periods. The fourth is ignoring dates. A monthly figure, a single-day drop and a fall from a record high are three different numbers, and each answer to why are tech stocks down depends on which one you use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why Were Tech Stocks Down in October 2026?

Investors reassessed AI capital spending, financing needs and valuations while leadership rotated to other sectors. No single factor can be shown to explain every stock's decline that month.

### How Much Did the Nasdaq Fall on November 5?

The Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.5% that day, closing at 24,315.00 according to FRED. The Nasdaq-100 fell about 6.4%. Both are single-session figures.

### Did Semiconductor Stocks Fall More Than the Nasdaq?

Yes. Janus Henderson reported a 15.3% October decline in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, against a 5.3% decline in the Nasdaq Composite over the same month.

### Does a Tech Selloff Mean AI Demand Is Disappearing?

No. Share prices reflect expectations, risk and valuation as well as demand. Prices can fall while demand keeps growing, especially when spending rises faster than expected cash flow.

### Why Are Tech Stocks Down When Earnings Are Strong?

Prices reflect what investors expect and will pay, not only reported profit. If expectations were higher, or spending is rising faster than cash flow, prices can fall despite strong earnings.
