# Expense Ratio Statistics: How ETF and Mutual Fund Fees Reduce Returns

An expense ratio of 0.40% sounds small. On a $100,000 portfolio it works out to about $400 a year at that balance. The bigger question is what happens when different fee rates run for decades, and why one source says the average fund costs 0.40% while another says 1.10%.

Both numbers can be correct. They measure different things. This page sets out the latest Investment Company Institute (ICI) figures, explains how each average is built, and shows with labeled examples how an annual fee compounds. Fund data cover 2024 and 2025, as reported by ICI.

## Key Takeaways

- **Asset-weighted fees are low:** ICI put the 2025 average expense ratio at 0.40% for equity mutual funds and 0.14% for index equity ETFs.
- **Averages differ by method:** for 2024 equity mutual funds, the asset-weighted average was 0.40%, the simple average 1.10% and the median 1.00%.
- **Fees have fallen for decades:** ICI reports equity mutual fund expense ratios dropped 62% from 1996 to 2024.
- **Small gaps compound:** in a labeled model, $10,000 at 7% gross for 20 years ends near $37,980 at a 0.10% fee and $32,071 at 1.00%.
- **The ratio is not the whole cost:** spreads, advisory fees, account charges and taxes can add to what an investor pays.

## What Is an Expense Ratio?

An expense ratio is a fund's yearly operating costs expressed as a percentage of its average net assets. It can cover portfolio management, administration, recordkeeping, shareholder servicing and, in some funds, distribution fees. The cost comes out of the fund's assets, so it reduces returns rather than showing up as a separate bill.

A ratio of 0.25% equals 25 basis points, or roughly $25 a year for every $10,000 invested at a steady balance. A 1.00% ratio is 100 basis points, about $100 on the same $10,000.

### Gross Versus Net Ratios

A fund prospectus may show two figures. The gross expense ratio is the full cost before any waiver. The net ratio is what the fund charges after a temporary fee waiver or reimbursement. If a waiver expires, the investor's cost can rise to the gross figure, so it is worth checking the end date.

### What the Ratio Leaves Out

Trading commissions, bid-ask spreads, advisory fees, platform charges and account fees sit outside the fund's expense ratio. Taxes on distributions are also separate. A fund with a low ratio can still be expensive to own if it is held through a high-fee account or traded often.

## What Are the Latest Expense Ratio Statistics?

ICI's [2025 fund fee research](https://www.ici.org/news-release/mutual-fund-and-etf-fees-remained-near-historic-lows-in-2025), published March 25, 2026, reported these asset-weighted averages. An asset-weighted average gives more influence to funds that hold more investor money.

| Fund category | 2025 asset-weighted average |
|---|---:|
| Equity mutual funds | 0.40% |
| Bond mutual funds | 0.36% |
| Index equity ETFs | 0.14% |
| Index bond ETFs | 0.09% |
| Money market funds | 0.24% |

Source: Investment Company Institute, 2025 data released in 2026. These are category averages, not the fee of any specific fund.

### What Changed From 2024

ICI's March 2025 report put the 2024 averages at 0.40% for equity mutual funds, 0.38% for bond mutual funds, 0.14% for index equity ETFs, 0.10% for index bond ETFs and 0.22% for money market funds. Equity funds and index equity ETFs held steady into 2025, while bond fund averages edged lower.

### Why Money Market Fees Rose

Money market funds are the one category above that rose, from 0.22% to 0.24%. ICI's 2024 report noted that some money market funds had been paring back fee waivers while short-term interest rates were high. When rates are high, funds have more room to charge their full fee and still pay a positive yield.

## Why Do Sources Report Such Different Averages?

ICI's [2024 detailed report](https://www.ici.org/system/files/2025-03/per31-01.pdf) shows three measures for the same group of funds. Each one answers a different question, and mixing them up is the most common error in fee articles.

| 2024 equity mutual fund measure | Expense ratio | What it describes |
|---|---:|---|
| Asset-weighted average | 0.40% | Average weighted by dollars invested |
| Simple average | 1.10% | Every share class counted equally |
| Median share class | 1.00% | Middle share class in the list |
| 10th percentile | 0.50% | Cheaper end of the range |
| 90th percentile | 1.87% | Costlier end of the range |

Source: ICI and Morningstar, Figure 2 of "Trends in the Expenses and Fees of Funds, 2024."

### What the Gap Tells Investors

The asset-weighted figure is far below the simple average because investors have moved most of their money into cheaper funds. Many costly share classes still exist, but they hold a smaller share of assets. So the typical dollar pays about 0.40%, while the typical fund on a list charges closer to 1.00%.

### Which Number to Use

For a sense of what investors pay in total, the asset-weighted figure is the better guide, and ICI says it prefers it for that purpose. For a sense of how expensive a randomly picked fund is likely to be, the median is more useful. A good article names which one it uses.

## How Much Have Fees Fallen Over Time?

ICI's 2024 report found that from 1996 to 2024, the average expense ratio for equity mutual funds fell 62% and for bond mutual funds fell 55%. That long slide reflects several shifts in how people buy funds.

The biggest driver, per ICI, was a move toward no-load funds. In 2024, 92% of gross sales of long-term mutual funds went to no-load funds without 12b-1 fees, compared with 46% in 2000.

### The Role of Index Funds

Index funds have also pulled the average down, and their share of fund assets has grown steadily over the past two decades. ICI's figures show index equity mutual funds had an asset-weighted average of 0.05% in 2024. At year-end 2024, the average index equity mutual fund held $13.6 billion, compared with $2.5 billion for the average actively managed equity fund, and larger funds can spread fixed costs further.

### Costs by Fund Size and Sponsor

Smaller fund companies tend to charge more. ICI reported that actively managed domestic equity funds at small fund complexes averaged 0.99% in 2024, against an industry average of 0.64% for that group. Those firms often offer niche strategies, which can cost more to run.

## How Fees Vary Across Fund Types

The same 2024 ICI table shows wide gaps between categories. Fund type explains much of the difference, so comparisons should match like with like.

| 2024 category | Asset-weighted | Simple average |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equity: growth | 0.58% | 1.07% |
| Equity: sector | 0.68% | 1.32% |
| Equity: blend | 0.23% | 0.91% |
| Bond: investment grade | 0.26% | 0.69% |
| Bond: high-yield | 0.60% | 0.93% |
| Target date funds | 0.29% | 0.66% |
| Index equity mutual funds | 0.05% | 0.49% |

Source: ICI and Morningstar, 2024.

### Why Sector Funds Cost More

Sector and specialty funds often involve more research and smaller asset bases, so their costs are spread over less money. Blend funds that track broad indexes sit at the low end. A sector fund is not wrong because it costs more, but its fee should be compared with other sector funds rather than with a broad index fund.

### Target Date Funds

Target date funds bundle several underlying funds into one portfolio that shifts toward bonds as a set year approaches. ICI's 2024 asset-weighted figure of 0.29% includes the costs of the underlying funds. Many workplace plans use them as the default choice, so their fee affects a large number of savers.

## How Much Does an Expense Ratio Cost Over Time?

The model below is hypothetical. It starts with $10,000, adds nothing more, and assumes a steady 7% gross annual return. Net growth is approximated as 7% minus the fee. Real funds accrue fees daily and returns are never steady, so this is not a forecast. For context, [Aswath Damodaran's NYU Stern data](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) show the S&P 500 compounded at about 10.0% a year from 1928 to 2025, so 7% is a deliberately modest round number, not an estimate of future returns.

| Annual expense ratio | Modeled net growth | Value after 20 years |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.10% | 6.90% | $37,980 |
| 0.40% | 6.60% | $35,904 |
| 1.00% | 6.00% | $32,071 |

All figures are rounded, pre-tax and exclude other costs. The FinLoom [expense ratio calculator](/investing/expense-ratio-calculator/) lets you change the balance, return and period.

### Why the Gap Grows

Each year's fee shrinks the base that earns next year's return. After 20 years in this model, the 1.00% fund ends about $5,900 behind the 0.10% fund, even though the yearly fee gap is only 0.90 percentage points. The longer the holding period, the wider the gap.

### A Larger Example

The same math on $100,000 over 30 years gives about $740,169 at a 0.10% fee and $574,349 at 1.00%, under the same hypothetical 7% gross return. The difference of roughly $165,800 is large enough to change a retirement plan, which is why fee comparisons deserve a few minutes of attention.

## Do Regular Contributions Change the Math?

Most investors add money over time rather than investing once. Each new contribution starts paying the fee from the day it arrives, so total fees grow as the balance grows. The gap between a cheap and a costly fund widens in dollar terms as more money goes in.

FinLoom's [dollar cost averaging statistics](/investing/dollar-cost-averaging-statistics/) explain how regular contributions work. The fee question is separate from the timing question, and over long periods the fee usually has a larger effect on the ending balance.

### Fees on a Growing Balance

A 0.40% fee on a $10,000 balance is about $40 a year. On a $250,000 balance it is about $1,000. The percentage stays fixed, but the dollar cost rises with every contribution and every year of growth, which is why a small ratio can still add up to a large sum.

### Why Percentages Can Mislead

People often judge costs by the dollar amount on a statement. Because the expense ratio comes out of the fund's assets, there is no line item to notice. Converting the ratio to dollars at your current balance makes the cost easier to see and compare.

## Does a Low Expense Ratio Mean a Better Fund?

No. A very cheap fund that does not match an investor's goals, risk tolerance or need for diversification is still a poor fit. A narrow sector fund and a broad total-market fund can have similar fees but very different risks.

Two funds that track the same benchmark can be compared on tracking difference, structure, tax treatment, trading costs and fees. FinLoom's FXAIX vs VOO comparison shows how a mutual fund and an ETF on the same index can differ in ways beyond the fee.

### Tracking Difference

Tracking difference is the gap between a fund's return and its index return. It includes the expense ratio plus trading costs, cash drag and any securities lending income. A fund can have a slightly higher fee but a smaller tracking difference, so both numbers are worth a look.

### Active Funds and Fees

An actively managed fund needs to beat its benchmark by more than its fee to add value. Its higher cost is a fixed hurdle, while any outperformance is uncertain. That does not make every active fund a bad choice, but the fee should be weighed against what the manager is trying to do.

## ETF Fees Versus Mutual Fund Fees

ETFs trade on exchanges during the day, so investors may pay a bid-ask spread on each trade. Mutual funds usually trade once a day at net asset value, subject to their own terms. Neither structure is always cheaper.

ICI's figures show index equity ETFs at 0.14% in 2025 and index equity mutual funds at 0.05% in 2024, asset-weighted. The mutual fund figure is lower partly because very large, very cheap index mutual funds hold so much of that category's money.

### Comparing Like With Like

A fair comparison sets index ETF against index mutual fund, or active fund against active fund, on similar benchmarks. Broad category averages describe the market but do not rank unmatched products. An index bond ETF at 0.09% and an active equity fund at 0.70% are doing very different jobs, so their fees say little about which is the better deal.

### Check the Current Prospectus

Expense ratios can change each year, and waivers can lapse. Before comparing two funds, check each one's current prospectus or fact sheet. A figure in an article, including this one, can go out of date. The prospectus fee table also lists any sales loads, redemption fees and 12b-1 fees, which can cost more than the headline ratio for some share classes.

## How Fees Fit a Long-Term Plan

The pillar guide on [crypto vs stocks](/investing/crypto-vs-stocks/) covers how asset choice and risk drive returns. Fees are the one part of investing an investor controls with certainty. Returns are uncertain; the expense ratio is known in advance.

That makes cost a sensible early filter. After narrowing a list to funds that match the goal and risk level, comparing fees among similar funds can add meaningful value over decades without any forecast about markets.

### A Quick Fee Checklist

Note the net and gross ratio, any waiver end date, the fund's tracking difference, account and advisory fees, and trading costs. Then convert the total to dollars at your current balance, and repeat the check each year when fund documents update. That short list catches most of the costs that an expense ratio alone can hide.

### What Fees Cannot Fix

A low fee cannot protect a portfolio from a market fall, poor diversification or selling at the wrong time. It improves the odds slightly in every year, but it is one factor among several. Behavior and asset mix usually have a bigger effect than a few basis points.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is a Good Expense Ratio?

It depends on the fund type. ICI's 2025 asset-weighted average was 0.14% for index equity ETFs and 0.40% for equity mutual funds, so compare funds with similar strategies.

### Does an Expense Ratio Come Out of My Cash Balance?

No. The fee is taken from the fund's assets and reflected in its share price and returns. It does not appear as a separate charge to your brokerage cash.

### Are ETFs Always Cheaper Than Mutual Funds?

No. Fees vary by fund type, share class and strategy. In ICI data, index equity mutual funds averaged 0.05% in 2024, below the index equity ETF average.

### Why Is the Average Expense Ratio Sometimes Quoted Near 1%?

That figure usually counts every share class equally. ICI's 2024 simple average for equity mutual funds was 1.10%, while the asset-weighted average was 0.40%.

### How Often Do Expense Ratios Change?

Funds can change them, usually once a year when the prospectus updates. Fee waivers can also expire. Check the current prospectus before comparing funds or relying on an older figure.
