# HSA vs HRA in 2026: Who Owns the Money and Which Benefits Can You Combine?

Two benefit plans can both help pay medical bills while giving you very different rights to the money. That is the core of the HSA vs HRA comparison. You own a health savings account (HSA) and can generally keep it when you change jobs. A health reimbursement arrangement (HRA) is an employer plan, and what happens to unused money depends on the plan's terms.

The two can also affect each other. A general-purpose HRA can make you ineligible to contribute to an HSA, even if your health insurance otherwise qualifies. Limited-purpose and post-deductible HRAs work differently. So rather than choosing on tax benefits alone, it helps to understand exactly how your employer's coverage is built.

## Key Takeaways

- You own an HSA, and you, your employer or others can contribute within the yearly limits.
- An HRA is funded only by your employer and reimburses medical costs under the plan's rules.
- A general-purpose HRA usually blocks HSA contributions, while limited-purpose and post-deductible HRAs can be exceptions.
- The 2026 HSA limits are $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage.
- The $2,200 figure for 2026 applies only to an excepted benefit HRA, not to every HRA.

## HSA vs HRA Comparison

The table sets out the main HSA vs HRA differences. It describes the standard designs, and individual employer plans can add their own rules.

| Feature | HSA | HRA |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns or sponsors it | You, as the account owner | Your employer, as the plan sponsor |
| Who funds it | You, your employer and possibly others | Your employer only |
| Health plan requirement | A qualifying high-deductible health plan and no disqualifying coverage | Depends on the type of HRA and the plan |
| Payroll contributions from you | Often allowed | Not allowed through salary reduction |
| Yearly limit | Federal HSA contribution limits | No general limit on employer contributions |
| Unused money | Stays in your HSA | Can be carried forward, under the plan's terms |
| Investing | Offered by many HSA providers | A reimbursement allowance, not an account you invest |
| Changing jobs | The account stays with you | Usually tied to the employer's plan |

Several types of HRA carry extra rules, including individual coverage HRAs (ICHRAs), excepted benefit HRAs and retirement HRAs. Your employer's benefit summary should say which type it offers.

## How an HSA Works

An HSA is a tax-advantaged account linked to qualifying health coverage. To contribute, you generally need coverage under a high-deductible health plan (HDHP), no disqualifying other coverage, no Medicare enrollment, and no one able to claim you as a dependent. [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969) explains the full rules.

You own the account even when your employer contributes. The money can pay qualified medical costs for you and, under IRS rules, for your spouse and dependents. Unused money stays in the account from year to year, which makes an HSA very different from a reimbursement budget your employer sets.

### 2026 HSA Contribution and Coverage Limits

The IRS sets the yearly limits and the HDHP thresholds that decide whether your plan qualifies. These are the 2026 figures from [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-19](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-21_IRB), before any special adjustments.

| 2026 rule | Self-only HDHP | Family HDHP |
|---|---|---|
| HSA contribution limit | $4,400 | $8,750 |
| Minimum annual deductible | $1,700 | $3,400 |
| Maximum out-of-pocket | $8,500 | $17,000 |

Publication 969 says eligible owners aged 55 or older at year-end can add $1,000. Your limit can also depend on how many months you were eligible and on employer contributions, which count toward the same yearly limit rather than adding to it.

### What Happens If You Leave Your Employer?

The HSA stays yours. You can keep it with the same provider or move the balance to another HSA. Publication 969 says a rollover must be completed within 60 days, and you can make only one rollover contribution in a 1-year period. If you lose HSA-eligible coverage, you cannot make new contributions for those months, but you can still spend the money already saved on qualified medical costs.

## How an HRA Works

An HRA is not an account you own. Publication 969 says an HRA must be funded solely by an employer, and the contribution cannot be paid through a voluntary salary reduction agreement. Employees are reimbursed tax-free for qualified medical expenses up to a maximum dollar amount for each coverage period.

Because your employer designs the plan, two HRAs can behave very differently. One might reimburse deductibles and copays, while an ICHRA can help pay for individual health insurance premiums. Publication 969 says unused amounts in an HRA can be carried forward for later years, but whether you can still use them after leaving your job depends on the written plan terms.

Publication 969 also lists the tax benefits. Employer contributions can be excluded from your gross income, you pay no federal income or employment tax on them, and reimbursements for qualified medical costs are tax-free. Self-employed people are not eligible for HRAs.

### Is There an Annual HRA Contribution Limit?

There is no single yearly federal limit that covers every HRA. Publication 969 says there is no limit on the amount of money your employer can contribute to an HRA. Specific types of HRA can still carry their own limits or restrictions set by other rules.

### The $2,200 Excepted Benefit HRA Limit

For plan years beginning in 2026, Revenue Procedure 2025-19 caps the amount that can be newly made available under an excepted benefit HRA at $2,200. That figure applies only to that specific type of HRA. It should not be quoted as a general HRA limit, because most HRAs are not excepted benefit HRAs.

## Can You Have an HSA and an HRA Together?

Sometimes, but the HRA's design decides it. Publication 969 says an employee covered by an HDHP and an HRA that pays or reimburses qualified medical expenses generally cannot contribute to an HSA. The issue is not whether you submit a claim. The coverage the HRA makes available is what counts.

### Limited-Purpose HRAs

A limited-purpose HRA can sit alongside an HSA. Publication 969 says these arrangements can pay for items listed under other health coverage, such as dental and vision care, but not long-term care, as well as preventive care. Because they do not pay general medical bills before the deductible, they do not block HSA contributions.

### Post-Deductible, Suspended and Retirement HRAs

A post-deductible HRA pays nothing for medical costs incurred before the HDHP's minimum deductible is met, so it can work with an HSA. Publication 969 also describes a suspended HRA, which you can elect to stop before a coverage period, and a retirement HRA that pays only for costs after retirement. Once you retire with such an HRA, you can no longer make HSA contributions.

Before you contribute to an HSA, confirm with your benefits administrator that your HRA is HSA-compatible. Getting it wrong can create an excess contribution, which Publication 969 says generally faces a 6% excise tax for each year it stays in the account.

## Example: A Qualifying HDHP With a General-Purpose HRA

Suppose an employee enrolls in a health plan marketed as HSA-eligible, and also receives a general-purpose employer HRA that pays ordinary medical costs from the first dollar. The HRA coverage can disqualify HSA contributions, even though the insurance itself meets the deductible and out-of-pocket rules.

Reading only the name of the insurance plan would miss the problem. The employee needs to look at what the HRA covers, not just what the health plan is called.

## Example: A Qualifying HDHP With a Limited-Purpose HRA

Another employee has qualifying HDHP coverage and an HRA limited to dental and vision costs. If the arrangement follows the rules and the employee otherwise qualifies, HSA contributions can continue alongside the HRA.

The deciding detail is the written reimbursement scope, not whether the employer calls the benefit an HRA. Two plans with the same name can have opposite effects on HSA eligibility. Ask for the plan's summary plan description, which lists the expenses the HRA reimburses, and check whether general medical costs below the deductible are on that list. If they are, the HRA is general-purpose for HSA purposes, whatever the plan is called.

## How ICHRAs Interact With Marketplace Coverage

An individual coverage HRA can reimburse premiums for individual health insurance and other permitted medical costs. It also affects the premium tax credit for Marketplace coverage.

The [IRS premium tax credit questions and answers](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit), updated February 19, 2026, say that if your employer offers you an ICHRA, you cannot get the premium tax credit for Marketplace coverage unless the ICHRA is considered unaffordable and you opt out of its reimbursements. Tax regulations set out when an ICHRA counts as affordable.

That makes the type of HRA one of the first facts to confirm when an employer offers one. An ICHRA, an excepted benefit HRA and a general-purpose HRA each carry different rules.

## Which One Offers More Flexibility?

An HSA offers more personal ownership and portability, because the account and its investments stay with you. That helps someone who expects to change jobs or wants to build health savings over many years. HSA eligibility is narrower, though, and fees and investment options depend on the provider.

An HRA can still be valuable to an employee who would not qualify for an HSA. Employer-paid reimbursements lower out-of-pocket costs without you setting aside any of your own pay. The trade-off is that the plan's design, rules and limits are set by your employer rather than by an account you control. An HRA also cannot follow you the way an HSA does, so its value is tied to staying with that employer and that plan.

The real HSA vs HRA question is not which acronym sounds better. It is which costs the benefit covers this year, whether you need to put in your own money, and what happens to unused amounts if you change jobs.

## HSA vs HRA in Common Situations

The HSA vs HRA question looks different depending on your job and coverage. These three situations show how the IRS rules play out.

### Your Employer Offers Only an HRA

If your employer offers a general-purpose HRA and no HSA-eligible plan, you cannot build an HSA through work. The HRA still lowers your out-of-pocket costs with employer money and no payroll deductions. Use it fully, and remember that access to unused amounts after you leave depends on the plan's written terms.

### You Are Self-Employed

Publication 969 says self-employed people are not eligible for HRAs, because an HRA is an employer-established benefit plan. A self-employed person with a qualifying HDHP can still open an HSA directly with a provider and contribute up to the yearly limit, which makes the HSA the main tax-advantaged option for health costs.

### You Are Close to Medicare

Enrolling in Medicare ends your eligibility to make new HSA contributions, though you can keep spending the balance on qualified costs. Some employers offer a retirement HRA that pays only for costs after retirement. Publication 969 notes that once you retire with such an HRA, you can no longer contribute to an HSA.

## Comparing HSAs With FSAs Too

Many employers offer a flexible spending account as well as, or instead of, an HRA. An FSA is funded by your own salary reductions, has its own 2026 limit, and follows carryover and grace period rules that differ from both HSAs and HRAs.

## Questions to Ask During Open Enrollment

The HSA vs HRA choice is clearer when you ask the right questions before enrolling. These cover the points that most often decide eligibility and value:

1. **Is the health plan an HSA-eligible HDHP?** Check it against the 2026 thresholds.
2. **What type of HRA is offered?** Ask whether it is general-purpose, limited-purpose, post-deductible or an ICHRA.
3. **Does the HRA affect HSA eligibility?** Get the answer in writing from your benefits administrator.
4. **How much does the employer provide?** Confirm the yearly HRA amount and which costs qualify.
5. **What happens to unused amounts?** Ask about carryover and access after you leave your job.

If the arrangement is an ICHRA, also ask how it affects Marketplace subsidies before you choose your coverage.

## The Decision Depends on the Plan, Not the Account Name

An employee-owned HSA and an employer-funded HRA can each lower the cost of medical care, but they rest on different foundations. HSAs give you control within eligibility and contribution limits. HRAs let employers design reimbursements within the law and their plan documents.

The best comparison uses the actual benefit plan you are being offered rather than a generic list of tax advantages. Read the plan documents, confirm the HRA type, and check HSA eligibility before you put money in.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an HRA the Same as an HSA?

No. You own an HSA and can contribute to it if eligible. An HRA is an employer plan funded only by your employer and governed by the plan's written terms.

### Can an Employer Contribute to Both an HSA and an HRA?

Yes, but the HRA must not make the employee ineligible for HSA contributions. Limited-purpose and post-deductible HRAs are the usual designs that allow both to work together.

### Do You Lose Your HRA When You Change Jobs?

Usually access depends on the employer's plan terms. Unlike HSA money, HRA balances are generally not yours to take with you, so check the plan before you leave.

### Is HRA Money Taxable Income?

Generally no. IRS Publication 969 says employer HRA contributions can be excluded from income, and reimbursements for qualified medical expenses are tax-free under a qualifying plan.

### Can HRA Money Be Invested Like HSA Money?

No. An HRA is an employer-funded reimbursement allowance, not an account you own and invest. Many HSA providers, by contrast, let you invest your balance once you meet their requirements.

### Is the 2026 HRA Limit $2,200?

Only for excepted benefit HRAs, for plan years beginning in 2026. Most other HRAs have no general federal limit on employer contributions, so that figure does not apply.
