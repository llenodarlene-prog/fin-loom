# Savings Interest Calculator: See How Your Savings Can Grow

This savings interest calculator estimates how a balance grows when you know its APY, your starting amount, your monthly contributions, and how long you plan to save. Change any input to see the ending balance, the interest earned, and a year-by-year breakdown.

```calculator
savings
```

The calculator works month by month. It converts the APY you enter into an equivalent monthly rate, applies it to the balance each month, and adds your contribution at the start or end of the month. The sections below explain the method, the assumptions, and why the result is an estimate rather than a promise.

## Key Takeaways

- A savings interest calculator estimates growth from a starting balance, an APY, monthly contributions, and time.
- APY is already an effective annual rate, so the monthly rate is (1 + APY)^(1/12) − 1, not APY divided by 12.
- At 5% APY, $10,000 with no contributions grows to about $10,500 after one year.
- Adding $500 a month to $10,000 at 5% APY for five years gives about $46,669.68, including about $6,669.68 of interest.
- Contributing at the start of each month earns slightly more than contributing at the end.
- The calculator assumes a constant APY, which variable-rate savings accounts do not guarantee.
- Fees, taxes, withdrawals, and rate changes are not included and can lower actual results.

## How Much Can Your Savings Grow?

The answer depends on four inputs: how much you start with, the APY, how much you add each month, and how many years you save. The savings interest calculator above combines them into one projection.

The default example starts with $10,000, adds $500 at the end of each month, and assumes a 5% APY for five years. The result is an ending balance of about **$46,669.68**: $10,000 of starting balance, $30,000 of contributions, and about $6,669.68 of interest. Interest makes up roughly 14% of the final balance. These are Fin Loom calculations from the same engine that runs the calculator.

### Small Contributions Add Up

Contributions often drive more of the final balance than interest, especially over shorter periods. Starting from $1,000 and adding $200 a month at 4.5% APY for three years reaches about $8,824.07, of which $7,200 is contributions and about $624 is interest. The habit of adding money each month does most of the work.

### Time Lets Interest Catch Up

Over longer periods, interest earns interest and becomes a larger share of the balance. Adding $500 a month at 4% APY for ten years, with no starting balance, reaches about $73,347.96 on $60,000 of contributions. These illustrations assume the APY never changes, which real savings accounts rarely guarantee.

## How Savings Interest Is Calculated

APY already expresses an effective annual yield after compounding. The [CFPB's Regulation DD definition](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1030/2/) describes APY as a rate reflecting the total interest paid on an account, based on the interest rate and the frequency of compounding over a 365-day period.

Because of that, the calculator does not divide the APY by 12. Instead it uses:

**Monthly rate = (1 + APY)^(1/12) − 1**

At a 5% APY, that gives a monthly rate of about 0.407%. Applying it for twelve months to $10,000 returns exactly the 5% annual yield, about $10,500, which is the check the APY figure should pass.

### Why Dividing by 12 Overstates Growth

Dividing 5% by 12 gives about 0.417% a month. Compounded over twelve months, that produces more than a 5% annual yield, because it treats the APY as if it were a nominal rate that still needs compounding. Using the equivalent monthly rate keeps the calculation consistent with what APY already means.

### How the Monthly Engine Works

Each month, the calculator applies the monthly rate to the current balance and adds the result as interest. It adds your contribution either before or after that step, depending on the timing you choose. The year-by-year table and the summary come from the same month-by-month loop, so the two always agree.

## How Monthly Contributions Change the Result

Contributions are often the biggest lever a saver controls. The rate is set by the bank, but the amount added each month is a personal choice. In the default example, contributions account for $30,000 of the five-year balance, far more than the interest earned.

### Start of Month vs End of Month

Timing makes a small difference. Contributing $500 at the start of each month in the default example gives about $46,807.83 after five years, compared with about $46,669.68 for end-of-month contributions. Money added earlier earns one extra month of interest each time, which adds up to roughly $138 here.

### Reading the Chart and Table

The chart shows two lines: the total balance and the running total of your starting balance plus contributions. The gap between them is interest. The table beneath it lists each year's starting balance, contributions, interest, and ending balance, so you can see exactly when interest begins to add up.

## How APY Affects Savings Growth

A higher APY increases interest, but its effect depends on the balance and the time involved. Comparing the same plan at two APYs shows the difference: $10,000 with no contributions at 4% APY reaches about $12,166.53 after five years, a Fin Loom calculation.

To see how an interest rate and compounding schedule turn into an APY in the first place, the [APY calculator](/money-banking/apy-calculator/) converts any nominal rate. For the definition and the disclosure rules behind the number, [APY meaning](/money-banking/apy-meaning/) explains how annual percentage yield works.

### Use the Disclosed APY

The APY to enter is the one your bank or credit union discloses. Under the [CFPB's Regulation DD Appendix A](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1030/a/), institutions calculate that figure assuming principal and interest stay on deposit for the term. Using the disclosed APY keeps your estimate consistent with how the account itself is described.

### Variable Rates Change the Picture

Most savings-account APYs are variable, so the rate in a five-year projection is an assumption, not a commitment from the bank. Running the calculator at a lower and a higher APY gives a range rather than a single figure, which is a more honest way to plan.

## What the Calculator Does Not Include

The savings interest calculator gives an estimate under simple assumptions. Real accounts can differ for several reasons:

- **Rate Changes:** Variable APYs can rise or fall after you calculate.
- **Fees:** Monthly charges reduce the balance that earns interest.
- **Taxes:** Interest earned can be taxable, which the calculator does not deduct.
- **Withdrawals:** Taking money out lowers future interest.
- **Promotional Rates:** An introductory APY may end partway through the period.
- **Balance Tiers:** Some accounts pay the top APY only on part of the balance.
- **Crediting Practice:** A bank's interest-crediting schedule can differ from the monthly model.

Two more limits are worth knowing before relying on any savings interest calculator result.

### Inflation Is Not Shown

The results are in today's dollars without any adjustment for rising prices. A balance can grow in dollars while buying less over time if inflation outpaces the APY. For long-term goals where that risk grows, [saving vs investing](/money-banking/saving-vs-investing/) explains when savings stop being the right tool.

### Deposit Insurance Still Applies

The CFPB notes that deposit accounts such as [money market accounts](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-money-market-account-en-1007/) are insured by the FDIC or NCUA up to $250,000 per owner. Very large balances may need to be spread across ownership categories or institutions to stay within coverage, which is a separate question from the interest the calculator estimates.

## When to Use a Savings Interest Calculator

A savings interest calculator is most useful for planning a specific goal: an emergency fund, a home deposit, or a large purchase a few years away. Entering a target timeline shows how much a monthly contribution can build at a realistic APY.

### Planning Toward a Goal

Try adjusting the monthly contribution until the ending balance reaches your target. The year-by-year table shows whether the goal is reachable on schedule and how much of the result relies on interest rather than deposits, which is useful when the APY is uncertain.

### Comparing Accounts

Running the same plan at the APYs of two accounts shows the dollar difference between them. If that difference is small, fees, access, and convenience may count for more than the yield gap, and if it is large, it can justify a little extra effort to move the money.

## A Savings Interest Calculator Is a Planning Tool

A savings interest calculator turns an APY into a clear projection of balance, contributions, and interest. Its strength is consistency: it applies the APY correctly month by month, so different plans and accounts can be compared fairly.

Its limit is that the future rarely follows a fixed rate. Treat the result as an estimate, revisit it when your bank changes its APY, and use the disclosed account terms alongside the calculator when deciding where to keep your savings.

## How to Use This Savings Interest Calculator

The savings interest calculator at the top of the page needs four numbers and one choice about timing. Each input has a clear job, and changing one at a time shows which one moves the result most.

### Starting Balance and APY

Enter the amount already saved and the APY the account discloses. A starting balance of zero works if you are opening a new account. The APY should be the bank's figure, entered as a percentage, such as 4.5 for 4.5%, rather than the account's nominal interest rate.

### Contributions, Years, and Timing

Enter how much you plan to add each month, including zero, and how many years to project. Then choose whether deposits arrive at the start or end of each month. Start-of-month deposits earn one extra month of interest each time, which slightly raises the ending balance.

A gap of a few hundred dollars over five years is meaningful, but it can be offset by monthly fees, minimum-balance rules, or a promotional rate that ends early. Comparing the dollar difference with each account's terms gives a clearer answer than the APY alone. The account with the higher net result is the better fit for that plan.

## The Yield Shown on Your Statement

Once savings are in an account, the statement shows how the APY played out in practice, which is a useful check on the savings interest calculator.

### APY Earned on a Statement

Appendix A also defines the "annual percentage yield earned" shown on periodic statements. It reflects the interest credited during the statement period and the average daily balance. In the CFPB's example, an account holding $1,500 for 15 days and $500 for 15 days averages $1,000, earns $5.25 in a 30-day period, and shows an APY earned of 6.58%.

### Why Statement Yields Can Differ

The disclosed APY assumes the balance stays put, while the APY earned reflects what really happened in the period. If deposits or withdrawals move the balance, the two figures can differ. Comparing them shows whether the account delivered its advertised yield on the money you held.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Is Interest on Savings Calculated?

Interest is calculated on the balance at the account's rate. This calculator converts the APY into a monthly rate and applies it each month, adding contributions as you schedule them.

### Why Not Divide APY by 12 for a Monthly Rate?

APY already includes compounding. Dividing it by 12 and compounding again overstates growth, so the calculator uses (1 + APY)^(1/12) − 1 to stay consistent with the annual yield.

### How Much Will $10,000 Earn at 5% APY in a Year?

About $500 if the balance stays untouched and the APY holds for the full year. Fees, withdrawals, or a rate change during the year would lower that amount.

### Do Monthly Contributions Earn Interest?

Yes. Each contribution starts earning interest after it is added. Contributions at the start of the month earn slightly more than those added at the end.

### Is the Calculator Result Guaranteed?

No. It is an estimate that assumes a constant APY and no fees, taxes, or withdrawals. Variable savings rates can change, so actual interest earned may differ.
