# Credit Card Delinquency Rate Statistics: Where U.S. Borrowers Are Falling Behind

A rise in credit card debt does not always mean more missed payments. And no single credit card delinquency rate captures every way researchers measure strain. The share of balances already late is different from the share newly falling behind in a quarter, and both differ from the share a bank writes off.

The New York Fed's Q3 2026 household debt report, published November 10, 2026, shows why these distinctions are worth the effort. Card balances rose in the quarter, while the rate at which card balances moved into serious delinquency was slightly lower than a year earlier. Federal Reserve bank data tell a similar story through mid-2026. This page lays out each measure with its source, period and population.

## Key Takeaways

- **Serious delinquency flow eased:** the New York Fed reported 6.87% of card balances moving into 90-plus-day delinquency in Q3 2026, annualized, down from 7.05% a year earlier.
- **Early delinquency improved slightly:** the same release said transitions into early delinquency improved slightly for credit cards in Q3 2026.
- **All debt worsened:** 5.0% of all household debt was in some stage of delinquency at the end of Q3 2026, up 0.3 points, driven by other loan types.
- **Bank data agree:** the card delinquency rate at commercial banks was 2.85% in Q2 2026, down from 3.04% a year earlier, per FRED.
- **Write-offs fell:** the bank card charge-off rate was 3.82% in Q2 2026, down from 4.19% a year earlier.

## What Is the Credit Card Delinquency Rate in 2026?

The [New York Fed's Q3 2026 release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110) measures delinquency as a flow. It reports the annualized share of balances that newly became at least 90 days late during the quarter, divided by balances that were less than 90 days late the quarter before.

| Debt category | Q3 2025 | Q3 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Credit cards | 7.05% | 6.87% |
| Auto loans | 2.99% | 3.02% |
| Mortgages | 1.28% | 1.60% |
| Home equity lines of credit | 1.27% | 1.17% |
| Student loans | 14.26% | 7.72% |
| Other | 5.35% | 5.13% |
| All debt | 3.03% | 2.64% |

Source: Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Household Debt and Credit Report, Q3 2026. Annualized flow into serious delinquency (90 or more days late).

### What the 6.87% Means

The figure is an annualized share of balances, not a share of people. It does not say that 6.87% of card holders missed a payment. It says that, at the pace seen in the quarter, about 6.87% of card balances that were not seriously late would become so over a year.

### Cards Versus Other Loans

Card debt had the second-highest serious delinquency flow after student loans. That is typical: cards are unsecured, carry high rates, and are often the first bill to slip when money is tight. Student loan rates swung sharply as reporting of missed payments resumed, so year-to-year comparisons there need extra care.

## How Did Overall Delinquency Change?

The same report found that 5.0% of all outstanding household debt was in some stage of delinquency at the end of September 2026, up 0.3 percentage points from the prior quarter. This covers every debt type, so it is not a credit card delinquency rate.

The New York Fed noted that transitions into early delinquency kept ticking up for auto loans and mortgages but improved slightly for cards. Serious delinquency transitions improved slightly for cards and student loans, held flat for auto loans and home equity lines, and worsened for mortgages, though mortgages remain low by historical standards.

### Why the Total Rose While Cards Improved

A rising share of all debt in delinquency can come from mortgages and auto loans even when cards improve. Mortgages are by far the largest category, so small shifts there move the total. Reading the card line separately avoids blaming card borrowers for a change driven elsewhere.

### What Early Delinquency Signals

Early delinquency, usually 30 days late, often comes before serious delinquency. A slight improvement in the early card transition suggests fewer borrowers were starting to fall behind. It is one quarter of data, so it hints at a direction rather than proving a trend.

## What Counts as a Delinquent Payment?

An account becomes delinquent when a required payment is not made by its due date. Analysts group late accounts by how far behind they are, commonly 30, 60 and 90 or more days. These stages are not interchangeable.

A payment a few days late may bring a late fee under the card agreement. Credit bureaus generally report lateness in 30-day steps. At 90 or more days, the New York Fed treats a balance as seriously delinquent.

### Stock Versus Flow

A stock rate asks what share of balances is late on a given date. A flow rate asks what share moved into delinquency during a period. The New York Fed's headline card figure is a flow. Banks typically report a stock. Both can be correct and still differ widely.

### Charge-Offs

A charge-off happens when a lender decides a debt is unlikely to be repaid and removes it from its books as a loss. The borrower still owes the money, and it may be sold to a collector. Charge-off rates show the final cost of delinquency to lenders.

## What Do Federal Reserve Bank Data Show?

FRED carries Federal Reserve data for card loans held by all commercial banks. The [delinquency rate on credit card loans](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRCCLACBS) is seasonally adjusted and measures delinquent card balances as a share of card loans held at banks on the reporting date.

| Quarter | Bank card delinquency rate | Bank card charge-off rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Q2 2024 | 3.22% | 4.56% |
| Q3 2024 | 3.20% | 4.69% |
| Q4 2024 | 3.08% | 4.57% |
| Q1 2025 | 3.05% | 4.44% |
| Q2 2025 | 3.04% | 4.19% |
| Q3 2025 | 2.99% | 4.18% |
| Q4 2025 | 2.95% | 4.09% |
| Q1 2026 | 2.91% | 3.82% |
| Q2 2026 | 2.85% | 3.82% |

Source: Federal Reserve via FRED (DRCCLACBS, CORCCACBS), seasonally adjusted.

### A Steady Decline

The bank card delinquency rate fell in each quarter shown from Q3 2024 to Q2 2026. Charge-offs peaked at 4.69% in Q3 2024 and eased to 3.82% by early 2026. Both point to gradually improving repayment among bank card borrowers over this period.

### Why Bank and New York Fed Figures Differ

The bank series covers loans on bank balance sheets and uses a point-in-time stock measure. The New York Fed uses credit reports across lenders and a 90-day flow. They describe related but different things, so the two should never be joined on one chart or quoted as one credit card delinquency rate.

## Did Repayment Conditions Improve in 2026?

On both the New York Fed's serious delinquency flow and the bank delinquency rate, card measures improved modestly over the year. That is a narrower claim than saying strain has disappeared.

Card balances reached $1.285 trillion in Q3 2026, up $22 billion in the quarter, according to the New York Fed. Larger balances with slightly better repayment rates can both be true at once.

### Strain Is Uneven

The Federal Reserve's [2025 household survey credit chapter](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-credit.htm) found that average card balances among matched respondents rose by more than $2,500, or 37%, among those "finding it difficult to get by," but by $59 among those "living comfortably." National rates can hide that concentration.

### Interest Keeps Pressure On

Federal Reserve data on FRED show the average rate on bank card accounts assessed interest was 22.36% in August 2026. At that rate, a carried balance grows quickly, which can push a borrower who is already behind further into delinquency.

## Why Does Borrower Income Shape Delinquency?

Two households with the same balance can have very different ability to pay. A $3,000 balance may be easy for one and hard for another. Job loss, medical costs, high rates and available credit all change the path toward missed payments.

The Fed's survey found that slightly over half of card holders with income under $100,000 carried a balance from month to month, more than among higher earners. Carrying a balance is not delinquency, but it raises exposure if income drops.

### Credit Access

The same survey found that 33% of adults applied for any credit in 2025. Among those who applied, one-third were denied or approved for less than requested. Limited access to new credit can leave borrowers with fewer options before a payment is missed.

### Buy Now, Pay Later Lateness

The survey also found that slightly more than one-fourth of buy now, pay later users made a late payment. Those loans sit outside card totals, so card delinquency rates miss some short-term payment stress.

## What Would Be a Misleading Reading?

It would be misleading to call the 5.0% all-debt figure the share of card holders who are delinquent. It would also be wrong to read the 6.87% flow as the share of card users who missed a payment in the quarter.

Delinquency figures also do not show how much interest a borrower paid, whether an account was charged off, or what a household has left for essentials. Those questions need other data.

### Mixing Series

Combining the bank stock rate with the New York Fed 90-day flow on one line creates a number no source published. Each series should carry its own name and definition wherever it appears.

### Comparing Different Periods

The New York Fed's latest data are for Q3 2026, while the latest bank series data are for Q2 2026. Comparing them as if they covered the same quarter can hide a turn in either direction.

### Before a Payment Is Missed

Anyone worried about falling behind can call the card issuer before the due date. Many issuers offer hardship programs or payment plans. Acting early usually leaves more options than waiting until an account is already 30 days late.

### After a Payment Is Missed

A single late payment reported at 30 days can affect a credit report. Bringing the account current quickly limits further damage. Setting up automatic minimum payments can prevent a repeat, though paying more than the minimum reduces interest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Credit Card Delinquency Rate in 2026?

The New York Fed reported a 6.87% annualized flow of card balances into 90-plus-day delinquency in Q3 2026. Bank data showed a 2.85% delinquency rate in Q2 2026.

### Does 6.87% Mean 6.87% of Card Holders Are Delinquent?

No. It is an annualized share of card balances moving into serious delinquency, not a count of people. Different borrowers hold very different balance sizes.

### Is 30 Days Late the Same as 90 Days Late?

No. They are different stages of delinquency. Many reports, including the New York Fed's, track early and serious delinquency separately because they signal different levels of strain.

### Why Do Delinquency Reports Show Different Percentages?

They use different lender populations, late-payment thresholds, dates and methods. A bank stock rate and a 90-day credit report flow rate measure different things.

### What Is a Credit Card Charge-Off Rate?

It is the share of card loans a bank writes off as unlikely to be repaid. FRED data show a 3.82% bank card charge-off rate in Q2 2026.
