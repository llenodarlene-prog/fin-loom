# Net Worth by Age: Median and Average Wealth in America in 2025

What is a normal net worth at 30, 45 or 65? The answer depends on what counts as wealth and on which statistic is used. A headline average can look far larger than what the middle family in the same age group holds.

The newest official benchmark for net worth by age is the Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF), released October 9, 2026. It replaces the 2022 tables that still circulate widely. The survey measures family balance sheets, not individuals, and reports every dollar figure in 2025 dollars. This page sets out the age table, explains the gap between median and mean, and shows how housing, income and debt shape the results.

## Key Takeaways

- **Median family net worth was $215,900:** the mean was about $1.24 million in 2025, both in 2025 dollars.
- **Median wealth rose 2% in real terms:** mean wealth rose 7% from 2022 to 2025, according to the Federal Reserve.
- **Under-35 median fell 23%:** to $33,000, while the 75-and-over median rose 37% to $504,900.
- **Homeowners and renters differ sharply:** the median homeowner family held $458,900, against $10,600 for renters.
- **Top-income gains were largest:** families in the top 10% of usual income saw median net worth rise 31% to $3,669,900.

## What Is the Median Net Worth in America in 2025?

The [Federal Reserve's 2025 SCF release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20261009a.htm) reported median family net worth of $215,900 and mean net worth of about $1.24 million. Net worth equals a family's total assets minus its debts.

The Fed's full report, [Changes in U.S. Family Finances from 2022 to 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/scf26.pdf), says real median net worth rose 2% between the surveys, while real mean net worth rose 7%. It describes wealth inequality as little changed since 2022.

| National family measure | 2022 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Median net worth | $211,100 | $215,900 | +2% |
| Mean net worth | $1,164,100 | $1,241,500 | +7% |

Source: Federal Reserve, SCF 2025, Table 2, in 2025 dollars.

### Why the Mean Is So High

The mean gives every dollar equal weight. A small number of very wealthy families pulls it far above the middle. The median is the family exactly in the middle of the distribution, so it is a better guide to what a typical family holds.

### Real Versus Nominal

The Fed adjusts 2022 values into 2025 dollars using a consumer price index. So the 2% and 7% changes are real gains after inflation. A table that compares unadjusted dollars from different years would show larger, misleading increases, because part of the rise would simply be inflation.

## Net Worth by Age: 2025 Federal Reserve Figures

The SCF groups families by the age of a reference person. The table below shows median and mean net worth for each age group in 2022 and 2025.

| Age of reference person | Median 2022 | Median 2025 | Mean 2022 | Mean 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Under 35 | $42,700 | $33,000 | $200,800 | $135,400 |
| 35 to 44 | $148,400 | $155,600 | $601,500 | $681,900 |
| 45 to 54 | $270,600 | $253,700 | $1,067,900 | $1,244,500 |
| 55 to 64 | $398,900 | $411,900 | $1,714,700 | $1,878,000 |
| 65 to 74 | $448,600 | $431,300 | $1,963,900 | $1,897,600 |
| 75 and older | $367,300 | $504,900 | $1,777,400 | $1,962,200 |
| All families | $211,100 | $215,900 | $1,164,100 | $1,241,500 |

Source: Federal Reserve, SCF 2025, Table 2, in 2025 dollars.

### Young Families Lost Ground

The under-35 median fell 23% and the mean fell 33%. The Fed notes that groups that typically have less wealth, including young families and renters, often saw median and mean declines. The survey samples different families each time, so this describes the group, not the same people tracked over time.

### Older Families Gained

The 75-and-over median jumped 37%, the largest gain of any age group. The Fed said the share of families over 75 grew in the 2025 survey and that their net worth gains were driven mainly by rising retirement account values.

## Why Are Average Net Worth Figures So Much Higher Than Medians?

In every age group, the mean is several times the median. For the 45 to 54 group, the median was $253,700 and the mean $1,244,500. A few families with tens of millions of dollars lift the average for everyone.

It would be misleading to tell a young adult that a typical family their age has $135,400. That is the mean, pulled upward by the top of the distribution. The median of $33,000 is closer to the middle family's position.

### When the Mean Is Useful

The mean is the right tool for measuring total wealth or concentration. If the question is how much wealth a whole age group holds, the mean times the number of families gives the answer. Neither number should be dropped; they answer different questions.

### Percentiles Tell More

The SCF also groups families by where they sit in the wealth distribution. The bottom quarter had a median net worth of $1,700 in 2025, while the top 10% had a median of $4,935,200. That range shows how wide the spread is within any average.

## How Do Homeownership and Debt Affect Net Worth?

Housing is the single biggest dividing line in the SCF data. The median homeowner family held $458,900 in net worth in 2025, up 6% from 2022. The median renter family held $10,600, down 7%.

The Fed reported that the homeownership rate was 66% in 2025, about unchanged from 2022. Among homeowners, median net housing value, meaning home value minus home-secured debt, rose from $218,900 to $230,000.

| Housing status | Median 2022 | Median 2025 | Mean 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Homeowner | $433,600 | $458,900 | $1,812,900 |
| Renter or other | $11,400 | $10,600 | $153,500 |

Source: Federal Reserve, SCF 2025, Table 2, in 2025 dollars.

### Debt Is Common at Every Age

The share of families with any debt stayed about 77% from 2022 to 2025, according to the Fed. About 42% of families had debt secured by their home in both years, and the median amount of that debt rose from $170,200 to $183,600.

### Card Debt in the Survey

The SCF found that about 45% of families carried a credit card balance after their last payment, unchanged from 2022. Among them, median card debt rose $150 to $3,100. FinLoom's [average credit card debt](/money-banking/average-credit-card-debt-statistics/) page compares this with other card data sources.

## How Does Income Shape Net Worth?

The SCF also groups families by usual income. Lower-income families saw small declines, while higher-income families gained. For the lowest fifth by income, median and mean net worth fell 6% and 4%. Families between the 40th and 80th income percentiles saw growth of 5% to 15%.

The top 10% by income saw median net worth rise 31% to $3,669,900. The Fed said changes in wealth largely reinforced existing patterns of inequality across family types.

| Usual income percentile | Median net worth 2025 | Change from 2022 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Below 20th | $14,400 | -6% |
| 20th to 39.9th | $77,100 | -1% |
| 40th to 59.9th | $188,200 | +8% |
| 60th to 79.9th | $362,500 | +8% |
| 80th to 89.9th | $814,600 | 0% |
| 90th to 100th | $3,669,900 | +31% |

Source: Federal Reserve, SCF 2025, Table 2, in 2025 dollars.

### Income and Age Together

Income and age overlap. Older families have often had more years of earning and saving. A net worth by age table therefore mixes the effects of income, time and asset prices, and it cannot separate them on its own. Comparing within an income group gives a sharper picture.

### Education Differences

The survey also reports wealth by education. Families with a college-educated reference person had a median net worth of $505,400 in 2025, compared with $106,700 for those with a high school diploma. Like age, education is linked to income and does not explain wealth alone.

## How Does Net Worth Differ by Race and Ethnicity?

The SCF reports wealth by the race or ethnicity of the survey respondent. Gaps are large and persistent. These figures describe groups and reflect many factors, including income, inheritance, homeownership and age, which the table does not separate.

| Respondent race or ethnicity | Median 2022 | Median 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| White non-Hispanic | $311,900 | $332,100 |
| Black non-Hispanic | $49,100 | $36,800 |
| Hispanic or Latino | $67,400 | $79,600 |
| Asian | $586,600 | $677,600 |

Source: Federal Reserve, SCF 2025, Table 2, in 2025 dollars.

### Reading the Gaps

The median for Black families fell 25% between the surveys, while the medians for Hispanic and Asian families rose 18% and 16%. Group medians can shift with sampling and with changes in each group's age mix, so a single survey change should be read alongside earlier waves.

### Age Mix Across Groups

Groups with younger average ages tend to show lower wealth, because net worth by age rises over a working life. Comparing groups within the same age band would give a fairer picture, but the published table does not provide that cross-breakdown.

## Do Cities and Rural Areas Differ?

The SCF splits families by whether they live in a metropolitan statistical area. In 2025, the median net worth for metropolitan families was $231,300, up 6%. For families outside metro areas, it was $150,100, down 6%.

Mean wealth outside metro areas fell 19%, from $713,700 to $577,100. Housing values, local wages and business ownership all differ between places, and the survey does not isolate a single cause.

### Why Location Shapes Wealth

Home values are often higher in metro areas, so homeowners there tend to hold more equity. Wages can also be higher. At the same time, costs are higher, so a larger net worth in a city does not always mean a higher standard of living.

### What the Split Cannot Show

The metro and non-metro groups each contain wide variation. A small city and a large coastal metro both count as metropolitan. Readers comparing their own net worth by age should keep local housing costs in mind rather than relying on a single national figure.

## How Can You Calculate Your Own Net Worth?

Add the current value of what you own: cash, investment accounts, retirement accounts, vehicles and real estate. Then subtract what you owe, including mortgages, loans and card balances.

For illustration, a fictional household with $25,000 in cash, $80,000 invested, $250,000 of net home equity and $15,000 of other assets has $370,000 in listed assets after the mortgage is already netted out of home equity. If it also owes $20,000 in other loans, its net worth is $350,000. Do not subtract the mortgage twice.

### Valuation Dates

House prices, stock values and business values change. Use a single date for every item so the total reflects one point in time. Updating the figure once or twice a year is enough for most planning purposes, and keeping the same method each time makes changes easier to read.

### Retirement Accounts in the Total

Retirement accounts count toward net worth, but withdrawals may be taxed and early access can bring penalties. FinLoom's [retirement savings by age](/investing/retirement-savings-by-age/) page breaks out those balances using the same 2025 survey. Some planners show retirement assets separately, or after estimated taxes, to give a clearer view of spendable wealth.

## What Role Do Stocks and Businesses Play?

Financial markets also shaped the 2025 results. The Fed reported that stock market participation, direct or indirect, fell slightly from 58% of families to 56%, while median stock holdings among holders grew 36% to $77,400. Families that owned stock gained from rising prices; those that did not missed that boost.

More than 15% of families owned a privately held business in 2025, a slight increase from 2022. Ownership was far more common at the top: 40% of families in the top income decile owned one, compared with 8% in the bottom half.

### How Asset Mix Shapes the Averages

Older and higher-income families hold more stock and business equity, which tend to rise and fall more than cash. That helps explain why their net worth grew faster in a period of rising stock prices, and why it could fall faster in a downturn.

### Business Values Are Hard to Measure

A private business has no daily market price, so survey respondents estimate its value. The Fed found that, among owners, mean business value was lower in 2025 than in 2022 for families in the bottom 90% of income and about unchanged for the top 10%. Estimates like these add uncertainty to wealth figures.

## Is Net Worth the Best Measure of Financial Security?

Not on its own. Net worth measures accumulated wealth but says little about cash on hand or monthly income. A family with large retirement assets may not be able to withdraw them freely, while another with lower net worth might have more liquid savings.

The pillar guide on [saving vs investing](/money-banking/saving-vs-investing/) explains why an emergency reserve and long-term investments serve different purposes. Both affect financial security, but only one shows up quickly when a bill arrives.

### Home Equity Is Not Cash

Many families hold most of their net worth in their home. Turning that into spending money means selling or borrowing against it, both of which carry costs. A household rich in home equity can still struggle with monthly bills, especially if income falls.

### Using the Numbers Well

A net worth by age statistic describes a population. It is not a score, a target or a judgment. Clearly labeled by survey year, dollar basis and median or mean, it gives useful context. Treated as a personal goal, it can mislead.

## Common Mistakes With Net Worth by Age Tables

The most common mistake is quoting the mean as if it were typical. A second is using 2022 survey figures without saying so; many popular pages still do. A third is comparing a single person's balance with a family figure that may combine two earners.

### Mixing Sources

Some articles blend SCF figures with numbers from other surveys or apps. Each source has its own sample and definitions. A sound comparison uses one source and one measure across all age groups, and states the survey year on every table.

### Ignoring Uncertainty

The SCF is a survey, so its figures carry sampling error. The Fed publishes standard errors; for the national median, it was about $6,400 in 2025. Small differences between groups, or between survey years, may fall within that margin and should be read with caution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Median Net Worth for People Under 35?

The 2025 SCF median for families with a reference person under 35 was $33,000, in 2025 dollars. This is a family figure, not an individual one.

### What Is the Average Net Worth by Age in 2025?

SCF means ranged from $135,400 for families under 35 to $1,962,200 for those 75 and older. Medians were far lower, from $33,000 to $504,900.

### Why Does Average Net Worth Exceed the Median?

Large fortunes pull the mean upward. The median is the middle family when all families are ranked by wealth, so it is less affected by the very rich.

### Does Net Worth Include Home Equity?

Yes. Net worth counts the home's value minus the mortgage, along with financial assets, vehicles and other property, minus all other debts.

### How Often Is the Survey of Consumer Finances Updated?

The Federal Reserve runs the survey every three years. The 2025 results were released October 9, 2026, following the 2022 survey.
