# How Long Does a Wire Transfer Take? Domestic vs International Timing

How long does a wire transfer take? A domestic U.S. wire can often arrive the same business day, especially when the sender submits it before the sending bank's cutoff time. An international wire can take several business days, because more banks, currency conversion, compliance reviews, time zones, and foreign banking systems may sit between the sender and the recipient.

The Federal Reserve's Fedwire Funds Service processes accepted domestic payments in real time, but that does not mean every customer wire shows up instantly. The sending bank, the receiving bank, and the time of day the instruction goes in all shape the final timeline. This guide walks through each of those steps using the Fedwire schedule in force as of October 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- Fedwire processes accepted payment orders in real time during its operating hours.
- Domestic bank wires are commonly completed the same business day when submitted before the bank's cutoff.
- Consumer banks often set earlier cutoff times than the Fedwire network itself.
- International wires can take several business days; Citi, for example, says up to 5 business days.
- Weekends, holidays, compliance checks, missing details, intermediary banks, and currency conversion can delay delivery.
- Fedwire payments are final and irrevocable once processed.
- A bank may be able to stop a wire before release, but a completed wire is hard to recover.
- Qualifying consumer international remittances generally carry a 30-minute federal cancellation window.
- As of October 2026, Fedwire does not run on Saturdays, Sundays, or Federal Reserve holidays; a six-day schedule is planned for no earlier than 2028.

## Typical Wire Transfer Timing

The table below gives practical ranges rather than guarantees. Each financial institution sets its own processing steps and customer cutoff times, so the same transfer can move at different speeds at different banks.

| Transfer Type | Typical Timing |
|---|---|
| Domestic wire submitted early in the day | Often the same business day |
| Domestic wire submitted after the bank's cutoff | Usually the next business day or later |
| International bank wire | Several business days; Citi cites up to 5 |
| Wire held for a compliance review | Can take longer |
| Wire with incorrect beneficiary details | Can be delayed, rejected, or returned |

Every row depends on the bank's own rules. The rest of this guide explains why a wire that moves in seconds across Fedwire can still take hours or days from the customer's point of view.

## Fedwire Itself Operates in Real Time

The Fedwire Funds Service is a real-time gross settlement system owned and operated by the Federal Reserve Banks. Once a participating institution submits a payment order that Fedwire accepts, the system processes and settles that payment individually. A properly formatted, funded payment does not wait in a batch for the end of the day.

Consumers, however, do not connect to Fedwire directly. They ask their own bank or credit union to send the wire, and the bank completes its own checks before it releases the payment into the network. That extra layer explains why the customer experience may not feel instant even though the settlement step is.

### What Real-Time Gross Settlement Means

"Real time" means accepted payments are processed promptly when received during operating hours. "Gross settlement" means each payment settles on its own, rather than being netted against other payments in a group. If one bank sends a large payment to another, the system does not need to wait for other transactions before settling that one transfer, which is why wires suit time-critical payments.

### Where the Delays Come From

Most of the waiting in a wire happens outside Fedwire. Time spent at the sending bank, at any intermediary bank, and at the receiving bank adds up. Verification, fraud screening, manual repairs to incomplete instructions, and posting the credit to the recipient's account all take place before or after the brief moment of settlement.

## Current Fedwire Operating Hours in 2026

According to the Federal Reserve's [Fedwire Funds Service operating hours](https://www.frbservices.org/resources/financial-services/wires/fedwire-funds-service-national-settlement-service-operating-hours/) page, the funds-transfer business day begins at 9:00 p.m. ET on the preceding calendar day and ends at 7:00 p.m. ET. A Monday business day, for example, opens at 9:00 p.m. ET on Sunday.

The same page lists 6:45 p.m. ET as the cutoff for core customer transfers at the network level. A retail bank can stop accepting customer wires much earlier than that, and that bank-level cutoff is the time most consumers need to check before they send money.

### Weekends and Holidays Still Count

Under the current schedule, the Federal Reserve Banks treat all Saturdays, all Sundays, and the holidays on their published holiday schedule as non-business days. A wire requested on a Saturday morning will generally not settle through Fedwire until the next business day opens, even if the bank's app accepts the request right away.

### A Six-Day Schedule Is Planned, Not Live

On October 9, 2025, the Federal Reserve announced plans to expand Fedwire and the National Settlement Service to include Sundays and weekday holidays. The Reserve Banks said the change would come in several years, no earlier than 2028. Until then, the existing business-day calendar applies, and planned expansion should not be mistaken for today's schedule.

## Why a Bank's Cutoff Can Be Earlier Than Fedwire's

Banks need time to prepare a wire before they release it. A typical sending bank will:

- **Verify the Customer:** Confirm that the person requesting the wire is authorized on the account.
- **Screen for Fraud:** Look for signs that the instruction is unusual or that the account has been taken over.
- **Review Compliance Alerts:** Check sanctions lists and anti-money-laundering rules.
- **Confirm Funds:** Make sure the balance covers the wire amount and any fee.
- **Validate Beneficiary Data:** Check that names, account numbers, and routing details look complete.
- **Format the Instruction:** Build the payment message in the format the network requires.

Because of those steps, a bank may stop accepting same-day retail wires in the early afternoon while Fedwire keeps processing until the evening. The bank's published cutoff, not the network's closing time, decides whether a wire can still go out today.

## How Long Does a Wire Transfer Take Inside the United States?

For a domestic payment, the answer to how long does a wire transfer take is often "the same business day." A simple domestic wire moves through a short chain: the sender, the sending bank, Fedwire, the receiving bank, and the recipient. When the instruction is correct and submitted before the cutoff, it can reach the receiving bank the same business day.

Citi's guide to [how long a wire transfer takes](https://www.citi.com/banking/personal-banking-guide/basic-finance/how-long-does-a-wire-transfer-take), updated September 25, 2026, says domestic wire transfers usually go through within 24 hours. That is one bank's stated expectation for its own customers rather than a network rule, but it lines up with the real-time design of Fedwire.

### Posting at the Receiving Bank

Settlement between banks is only part of the story. The receiving institution still has to credit the money to its customer's account under its own procedures. Some banks post incoming wires within minutes, while others review them in batches during the day, so the recipient may see the funds a few hours after settlement.

### What Speeds a Domestic Wire Up

Domestic wires tend to move fastest when the sender submits early in the business day, uses details copied from a trusted source, has enough money in the account, and is sending to a common destination bank. Each of those factors removes a reason for the bank to pause the payment for a manual review.

## How Long Does a Wire Transfer Take Across Borders?

International payments usually involve more parties, and every handoff adds time. A cross-border wire may pass through the sending bank, one or more correspondent or intermediary banks, a currency-conversion step, the receiving bank, and the destination country's local clearing system.

Citi says international wire transfers may take up to 5 business days because they can face different fraud detection processes and banking regulations. That figure describes one bank's experience; other providers quote their own ranges, and no single industry standard applies to every route. Different national holidays and time zones can stretch the timeline further.

### What Is a Correspondent Bank?

A correspondent bank acts as a go-between when two financial institutions do not hold accounts with each other. A U.S. bank may route a payment through a larger international bank that has a relationship with the destination institution. Correspondent banking makes global payments possible, but it can add processing time, extra fees, compliance checks, and reconciliation steps.

### Currency Conversion and Local Systems

When money changes currency, a bank or foreign-exchange provider converts it at some point along the route. The destination country's own payment system then delivers the funds to the recipient's bank. If that local system is closed for a holiday or the time zone has already ended its business day, the wire waits.

## Compliance Reviews Can Delay an Otherwise Fast Wire

Banks must follow legal and regulatory requirements before they move money. A transfer can be paused for several reasons:

- **Sanctions Screening:** A name that resembles one on a sanctions list may need a manual check.
- **Anti-Money-Laundering Review:** Unusual amounts or patterns can prompt extra questions.
- **Fraud Concerns:** A sudden large wire from a normally quiet account may trigger a call to the customer.
- **Incomplete Details:** Missing beneficiary addresses or reference fields can stop processing.
- **Mismatched Information:** A name that does not match the account number can lead to a hold.

A delayed wire therefore does not mean the payment system has failed. The bank may simply be reviewing the instruction before it lets the payment proceed, and answering the bank's questions quickly is often the fastest way to move it along.

## Incorrect Details Are a Common Cause of Delay

Errors can lead to rejection, investigation, or manual repair. Common problems include a wrong account number, a wrong routing number, an incorrect SWIFT/BIC code, an incorrect IBAN, a beneficiary name that does not match the account, an incomplete address, the wrong currency, or missing intermediary-bank information.

The sending bank should confirm which fields the destination requires. Copying details from the recipient's official documents, rather than retyping them from memory, removes many of these problems before the wire is ever sent.

## How Can You Tell Whether a Wire Was Sent?

Fedwire attaches message identifiers to every payment it handles. Federal Reserve Financial Services notes that successfully processed messages contain Input Message Accountability Data (IMAD) and Output Message Accountability Data (OMAD) identifiers. These references let banks trace a specific payment through the network.

Bank customers can ask their financial institution for a wire confirmation or the IMAD reference when they need to show that a payment left. For international transfers, the bank may provide other reference numbers, depending on the network and message format used along the route.

### What Does "Pending" Mean?

A pending wire can be at several stages. It may be waiting for bank approval, scheduled for a future date, under fraud or compliance review, waiting for the next business day, sent but not yet posted by the receiving bank, or held because beneficiary details need correction. The single word "pending" covers all of those situations.

### Questions to Ask the Sending Bank

The most useful question is whether the bank has only received the request or has released it into the payment system. Asking for the release time, the reference number, and the expected arrival date helps the sender understand what options remain and gives the recipient's bank something specific to search for.

## Can a Wire Be Stopped Before It Is Sent?

Sometimes. If the bank has not yet released the instruction, it may be able to cancel the request. The sender should contact the bank right away rather than relying on an online status label.

A "pending" status in an app does not guarantee that cancellation is still possible. The exact stage of the payment decides what the bank can do, and those stages can change within minutes during the business day.

## What Happens After Fedwire Settlement?

Federal Reserve Financial Services states that payments processed over the Fedwire Funds Service are made in central bank money and are final and irrevocable once made. After settlement, the sending bank can send a return request, but that request is not the same as reversing a card charge.

The recipient or the receiving institution may need to agree before the money comes back. This is why banks repeatedly warn customers to verify wire instructions before sending, and why the time spent checking details at the start is usually time well spent.

## International Consumer Transfers Can Have a Cancellation Window

Some international consumer transfers are legally defined as remittance transfers under Regulation E. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that senders of covered transfers can usually [cancel an international money transfer](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-i-cancel-an-international-money-transfer-en-1737/) within 30 minutes of paying for it.

That right applies when the transfer meets the definition of a covered remittance, the funds have not been picked up or deposited, and the sender gave enough information for the provider to find the transfer. For transfers scheduled in advance, the CFPB says the sender may cancel up to three business days before the transfer is made.

### What If an International Transfer Never Arrives?

Covered remittance transfers also carry federal error-resolution rules. The CFPB says consumers generally have 180 days from the disclosed date of availability to report certain errors. Providers generally have 90 days to investigate, and possible remedies for certain errors include a refund or having the transfer resent.

### How These Rights Differ From Fedwire Finality

These consumer protections sit on top of the banking system rather than changing how settlement works. Fedwire finality applies between banks, while remittance rules govern the relationship between a consumer and the provider that sold the transfer. A domestic wire between two U.S. accounts does not carry the same 30-minute right.

## How Wire Timing Compares With ACH

ACH payments can also move quickly. Nacha estimates that about 80% of ACH payments, both credits and debits, settle in one banking day or less through regular and Same Day ACH. Nacha also reported that Same Day ACH handled 470.4 million payments in the third quarter of 2026, up 36.7% from a year earlier.

For routine payments, ACH can therefore be fast enough at a lower customer cost. The advantage of a wire usually comes from the combination of urgency, high value, and settlement finality rather than speed alone. The full comparison of cost, reversibility, and best uses appears in [ACH vs wire transfer](/payments/ach-vs-wire-transfer/).

## How Long Does a Wire Transfer Take to Go Through on a Friday?

Timing near the end of the week deserves its own check. A domestic wire submitted on Friday morning before the bank's cutoff can often settle that day. A wire submitted on Friday evening after the cutoff will generally wait until Monday's business day, which opens at 9:00 p.m. ET on Sunday under the current Fedwire schedule.

Federal holidays push the timeline further. If Monday is a Federal Reserve holiday, a late-Friday wire may not settle until Tuesday. Senders facing a deadline, such as a home closing, often plan to submit the wire a day early to leave room for questions from the bank.

## Before Sending an Urgent Wire

A short checklist helps avoid the most common delays. Before sending a time-sensitive wire, confirm:

- **Bank Cutoff:** The latest time the bank accepts same-day wires.
- **Account Number:** The recipient's full account number, copied from a trusted source.
- **Routing or SWIFT Details:** The correct ABA routing number for domestic wires, or the SWIFT/BIC code abroad.
- **Beneficiary Name:** The exact name on the receiving account.
- **Fees:** Outgoing, incoming, and any intermediary charges.
- **Currency:** The currency the recipient expects to receive.
- **Intermediary Banks:** Whether the route needs a correspondent bank.
- **Arrival Date:** The date the bank expects the funds to arrive.
- **Instructions Check:** The recipient's details confirmed through a phone number or contact method already known to the sender.

For real-estate or business wires, emailed changes to payment instructions deserve extra caution. The basics of how wires work, including the details banks ask for, are covered in [what is a wire transfer](/payments/what-is-a-wire-transfer/).

## Wire Timing Fits a Wider Payments Picture

Wires are one of several ways money moves between accounts, alongside ACH, cards, and digital wallets. Consumer payment platforms such as PayPal handle very large volumes of everyday transfers, a scale shown in [PayPal statistics](/payments/paypal-statistics/), while wires remain the tool for urgent, high-value, or cross-border bank payments.

So how long does a wire transfer take in practice? For most domestic wires sent early on a business day with correct details, the money arrives the same day. International wires, late submissions, holidays, and compliance reviews can each add time, which is why checking the bank's cutoff and the recipient's details first is the most reliable way to keep a wire on schedule.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Long Does a Domestic Wire Transfer Take?

A domestic U.S. wire often arrives the same business day when it is sent before the bank's cutoff and no fraud, compliance, or beneficiary-detail problems arise.

### How Long Does an International Wire Take?

It varies by route and provider. Citi says international wires may take up to 5 business days, and intermediary banks, currency conversion, holidays, or errors can add time.

### Do Wire Transfers Go Through on Weekends?

Not through Fedwire under the October 2026 schedule. Saturdays, Sundays, and Federal Reserve holidays are non-business days, and expanded operating days are planned no earlier than 2028.

### Why Is My Wire Transfer Taking So Long?

Common causes include bank cutoffs, missing or mismatched details, fraud or sanctions reviews, intermediary banks, time zones, weekends, holidays, and slow posting at the receiving bank.

### Can a Completed Wire Be Reversed?

Fedwire settlement is final and irrevocable once processed. The sending bank can request a return, but recovery depends on the receiving side and is not guaranteed.
