# Semiconductor Stocks in 2026: What the October Rout Revealed

Semiconductor stocks were among the hardest-hit parts of the October 2026 reversal in AI-linked shares. Janus Henderson reported that the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, often called the SOX, fell 15.3% in October. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% over the same month. That gap is large, but it does not mean every chip company lost 15.3%.

The more useful lesson is about expectations and business models. Companies across the chip supply chain earn money from different parts of technology spending, face different production limits, and carry different risks when big customers rethink their budgets. This page sets out the dated figures through the November 6, 2026 close and explains what they do and do not show about semiconductor stocks.

## Key Takeaways

- **The chip index fell hardest:** the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 15.3% in October 2026, against 5.3% for the Nasdaq Composite.
- **The gap was 10 points:** chips trailed the Nasdaq by 10.0 percentage points over the same month.
- **Large names fell too:** J.P. Morgan reported October declines of 10.4% for Nvidia and 7.2% for Broadcom.
- **November 5 brought more losses:** the Associated Press reported one-day drops of 9.3% for Nvidia and 17.3% for AMD.
- **Asian chip markets fell as well:** J.P. Morgan reported October losses of 15.6% for Korea and 8.4% for Taiwan in U.S. dollar terms.

### What the 10-Point Gap Means

The chip index trailed the Nasdaq Composite by 10.0 percentage points in October. That compares two published returns for the same month. It is not an estimate of any portfolio's loss, and it does not describe the result for every chip company in the index.

### Monthly Change Versus Peak-to-Trough

These are month-end figures. Prices moved more within the month, and the November 5 session added further losses. A monthly return, a single-day move and a drop from a record high are three separate measures, and each should be labeled.

## Why Did Chip Shares React So Sharply to AI Concerns?

The semiconductor supply chain often prices in growth several years ahead. When investors expect strong data-center spending, they may pay high valuations for designers of AI accelerators, suppliers of memory and networking chips, foundries that make advanced silicon, and makers of equipment used to build new chip capacity.

A change in assumptions about the size or timing of that spending can affect every link in the chain at once. Orders may not disappear, but the expected pace of new orders, inventory turnover or profit margins can change. Because share prices reflect expected future earnings, even a modest cut to a high-growth forecast can cause an outsized price move.

### What Investors Said They Were Watching

Nasdaq's October review identified capital spending, financing and free cash flow as the issues drawing investor scrutiny. J.P. Morgan Asset Management's [October market review](https://am.jpmorgan.com/fr/en/asset-management/liq/insights/market-insights/market-updates/monthly-market-review/) pointed to concerns over the scale of upfront capital being committed to data centers, alongside growing Chinese competition. These themes offer a reasonable framework without proving the reason for any single stock's move.

### Why High Expectations Raise Risk

When a stock price assumes years of rapid growth, small changes in that outlook carry large effects. A company can keep growing and still see its shares fall if growth comes in below what the price assumed. That is a valuation risk, separate from whether the business itself is healthy.

## Not All Semiconductor Companies Share the Same Exposure

The word "semiconductor" covers several different businesses. Each earns money differently and faces different pressures when spending plans change.

**Chip designers** depend on product demand, intellectual property and the ability to hold pricing. **Foundries** depend on factory use, manufacturing yields and customer contracts. **Memory producers** are sensitive to supply cycles and chip prices. **Equipment suppliers** depend on manufacturers' investment budgets. **Packaging and testing firms** face their own bottlenecks.

### End Markets Differ Too

One chip company may earn much of its revenue from data-center accelerators. Another may earn more from cars, industrial equipment or consumer devices. A sector index is useful for describing overall sentiment, but it cannot replace company-level evidence about where revenue comes from.

### How to Compare Two Chip Companies Fairly

Use the same fiscal period for each company and separate reported revenue from management forecasts. Check whether capital spending is funded by internal cash flow, borrowing or other financing. Do not assume a company's exposure from its ticker or a marketing description. FinLoom's guide to [stock analysis fundamentals](/markets-stocks/stock-analysis-fundamentals/) explains the financial statements that hold this information.

## What Happened on November 5?

The October decline was followed by another severe session. The Nasdaq-100, which holds many large chip makers, fell from 28,437.17 to 26,629.81 on November 5, about 6.4%, according to FRED. Reuters reported that semiconductor shares were at the center of that day's technology selloff.

The Associated Press reported individual moves that day, including a 9.3% fall for Nvidia and a 17.3% drop for AMD. These are single-session stock returns for November 5, 2026, not October month-end results.

### Why Single-Day Leaders Can Mislead

Picking the largest one-day loser produces a dramatic headline but says little about the typical company, the index's weighting or the next session. A responsible statistic shows its denominator and its date. For a sector view, the index figure and a range of company results tell more than one extreme number.

### The Next Session

The Nasdaq-100 closed at 26,989.99 on November 6, about 1.4% above its November 5 close, according to FRED. A partial bounce the next day does not reverse a month of losses or confirm a turning point. It is one more data point in a volatile stretch, and it should be read alongside the month's larger moves rather than on its own.

## The Global Side of the Chip Selloff

The October decline reached chip-heavy markets abroad. J.P. Morgan reported that Korea fell 15.6% and Taiwan 8.4% in U.S. dollar terms in October 2026, and that MSCI Asia ex-Japan fell 8.2%. It linked those moves to concerns about the scale of AI investment and growing Chinese competition.

Many of the world's largest foundries, memory makers and component suppliers are listed in Asia. When investors question data-center spending in the U.S., those suppliers can fall alongside, or even more than, U.S. chip designers. A view of semiconductor stocks that looks only at U.S. listings misses much of the industry. Currency moves add another layer. J.P. Morgan reported the Asian figures in U.S. dollar terms, so part of each move reflects exchange rates rather than share prices alone, which is worth remembering when comparing them with U.S. index returns.

## A Better Semiconductor Risk Checklist

Three questions reveal more than a daily leaderboard. First, which end customers account for expected growth, and how concentrated are they? A company selling mainly to a few large cloud providers carries different risk from one with many small customers.

Second, how much additional capacity or equipment spending is needed before revenue arrives? Factories and tools take years to pay off, so long lead times raise the risk of misjudging demand. Third, how much of the expected profit depends on a continued premium valuation rather than actual cash generation?

### Different Answers for Different Investors

These questions apply differently to a foundry and a chip designer. They also differ for an investor buying a broad semiconductor fund and one owning a single company. Concentration can exist at the customer level, the industry level and the portfolio level at the same time.

### How the Chip Slump Connects to Big Tech

Several of the largest technology companies are among the biggest buyers of AI chips. When those buyers reconsider spending, chip suppliers feel it. FinLoom's [analysis of why tech stocks fell in 2026](/markets-stocks/why-tech-stocks-fell-2026/) looks at the buyer side of the same story, and the [Magnificent Seven statistics](/markets-stocks/magnificent-seven-2026-selloff-statistics/) show how concentrated that group has become in broad indexes.

## What the October Rout Does Not Prove

The 15.3% index loss does not show that industry revenue fell 15.3%. It does not reveal the future size of the AI market, and it does not show that every chip maker is overvalued. It is an observed change in quoted prices under one index's methodology over one month.

Semiconductors remain a diverse industry serving cloud computing, industrial systems, telecommunications, cars and consumer hardware. October's lesson was that high expected growth can create large valuation risk, especially when investors start asking how and when spending turns into cash.

### How Earlier Chip Downturns Ended

The chip industry has a long record of boom and bust cycles tied to inventory and capacity. Past downturns have ended at different speeds, some within months and some over years. That history is a reason for caution about predicting the path of semiconductor stocks from a single month's move in either direction.

### What the Rout Does Show

The October figures do show how sensitive semiconductor stocks are to changes in expectations. A 15.3% monthly fall in an index of established companies is large. It shows that even profitable, growing chip makers can see sharp price swings when investors revise their view of future spending, and that diversification within one industry offers limited protection when the whole industry is repriced.

## How Semiconductor Stocks Sit Inside Broad Funds

Many investors own semiconductor stocks without choosing them directly. Broad index funds weight companies by market value, and several chip makers grew into some of the largest companies in the U.S. market during the AI boom. That growth gave them more weight in S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 funds.

As a result, a chip selloff can hit a portfolio that holds no dedicated semiconductor fund at all. The Nasdaq-100's 6.6% October decline and its 6.4% fall on November 5 partly reflect that weight. Checking a fund's top holdings shows how much of it sits in chip companies and their largest customers.

### Overlap Between Funds

An investor holding an S&P 500 fund, a Nasdaq-100 fund and a technology fund may own the same chip makers three times. Each fund looks different by name, but the largest holdings can overlap heavily. Adding a semiconductor fund on top raises that concentration further.

### Why This Is Not a Recommendation Either Way

Concentration is not automatically good or bad. It means a portfolio's results depend more on a narrow group of companies. Whether that suits an investor depends on their goals, time horizon and comfort with swings, which this page cannot judge. The point is to know the exposure rather than discover it during a selloff.

## How to Follow Semiconductor Stocks From Here

Tracking chip shares well means watching more than the daily price. Quarterly earnings reports show revenue by end market, inventory levels and capital spending plans. Guidance from large cloud providers on their own spending can signal changes in demand before chip makers report them.

### Data Points Worth Watching

Inventory is one of the clearest signals. When chip inventories rise faster than sales, it can point to slowing demand ahead. Order backlogs at equipment makers show whether manufacturers are still expanding capacity. Both can be found in company filings and earnings releases.

### Keep the Time Frame Clear

Whatever figures you follow, note the window each covers. A quarterly revenue figure, a monthly index return and a single-day share move answer different questions. Keeping the dates attached prevents the common mistake of mixing them into one misleading story about semiconductor stocks.

## Questions to Ask Before Adding Chip Exposure

A sharp drop can make semiconductor stocks look cheap. A lower price is not the same as a low price, and the questions below help separate a planned decision from a reaction. They are general, not personal advice.

### Questions About the Investment

Is the company or fund exposed mainly to data centers, or to a wider mix of end markets? How much of its revenue depends on a few large customers? Is its capital spending funded from cash flow or borrowing? How did it perform in past chip downturns, and how long did recovery take?

### Questions About Your Portfolio

How much chip exposure do you already hold through broad funds? Would adding more make one industry a large share of your stock holdings? Could you hold the position through a further decline without needing to sell? Clear answers make it easier to act on a plan rather than on a headline.

## Common Mistakes When Reading Semiconductor Stock Statistics

The first mistake is treating the index figure as every company's result. The SOX fell 15.3%, but individual chip stocks ranged widely. The second is mixing windows, such as quoting a November 5 single-day loss as if it were an October figure.

The third is assuming a selloff reflects falling demand. Prices can drop on changed expectations while revenue keeps growing. The fourth is ignoring overseas suppliers. Much of the chip supply chain trades in Asia, and October's declines there were as sharp as in the U.S.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index?

It is a widely followed stock index covering semiconductor companies, often called the SOX. Its return describes the index as a whole, not every company in the chip supply chain.

### How Much Did Semiconductor Stocks Fall in October 2026?

Janus Henderson reported a 15.3% October 2026 decline in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, against a 5.3% fall in the Nasdaq Composite over the same month.

### Why Are Semiconductor Stocks Down?

Investors questioned how quickly AI-related spending would turn into cash flow, according to market reviews. Chip stocks price in years of growth, so changes in expectations hit them hard.

### Are Semiconductor Stocks the Same as AI Stocks?

No. Many chip companies supply AI infrastructure, but their revenue sources differ, and many AI-related businesses operate outside the semiconductor industry entirely.
