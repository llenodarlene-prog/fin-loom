# Magnificent 7 Stocks in 2026: Concentration Statistics From the Pullback

A broad stock index can look diversified while depending heavily on a handful of companies to set its direction. October 2026 showed this clearly. Nasdaq's Magnificent Seven index fell 4.7%, the standard S&P 500 slipped 0.9%, and the S&P 500 Equal Weight Index rose 5.0%.

That contrast says more than a list of seven tickers. It shows how the weight given to a company changes what investors experience, even when two indexes hold largely the same businesses. This page explains what the magnificent 7 stocks are, sets out the dated October figures, and shows why concentration shapes the returns of ordinary index funds. Data runs through the November 6, 2026 close.

## Key Takeaways

- **The group fell in October:** Nasdaq's Magnificent Seven index dropped 4.7% in October 2026, according to its November 2 review.
- **The average stock rose:** the equal-weight S&P 500 gained 5.0% while the standard S&P 500 fell 0.9%.
- **Some members fell much further:** J.P. Morgan reported October declines of 18.7% for Alphabet, 15.2% for Meta and 10.4% for Nvidia.
- **Growth lagged value:** the Russell 1000 Growth Index fell 6.1% and the Value Index rose 4.4%, a 10.5-point gap.
- **Concentration cuts both ways:** the same weighting that lifted index returns in past rallies deepened the drag in October.

## What Are the Magnificent 7 Stocks?

The group usually includes Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Nvidia, Meta Platforms and Tesla. They are grouped together because of their size and influence on U.S. stock indexes and technology-driven investor stories, not because they share one official stock-market sector.

Their businesses differ widely. The group includes cloud computing, digital advertising, consumer devices, online retail, electric vehicles and chip design. Their capital needs, cash flows and regulatory risks differ. Treating them as one business model hides those differences.

### Why the Label Exists

The name is a market shorthand, not an index committee's official category. Different providers may track slightly different baskets, and the companies' rankings by market value change over time. When a figure is quoted for "the Magnificent Seven," check which index or basket it refers to.

### How They Are Classified

Under standard sector classifications, these companies sit in several sectors. Apple, Microsoft and Nvidia are generally in Information Technology. Alphabet and Meta are generally in Communication Services. Amazon and Tesla are generally in Consumer Discretionary. That spread is why a sector table alone cannot capture the group.

### Individual Members Moved Differently

J.P. Morgan reported October declines for several large technology-related companies, including Alphabet down 18.7%, Meta down 15.2% and Nvidia down 10.4%. A group index summarizes all seven, so some members fell more than 4.7% while others fell less or rose. A group figure is not seven identical results.

## Why Did Concentration Weigh So Heavily in October?

In a market-value-weighted index, the largest companies receive the largest weights. If those companies fall, gains among many smaller members may not offset the losses. Equal weighting does not remove risk, but it changes where the risk sits.

October showed this without any need to estimate each company's exact contribution. Janus Henderson reported that only two of eleven S&P 500 sectors fell in October, yet the standard index still declined. It also noted the equal-weight index's lead was its largest since April 2009.

### The Same Weighting Helped in Past Rallies

Concentration is not only a downside risk. When the largest companies rose strongly in earlier years, their heavy weights lifted market-value-weighted index returns above the average stock's. October 2026 showed the same mechanism working in reverse, pulling the standard index down while the average member of the S&P 500 rose.

### Fund Names Do Not Guarantee Variety

One common trap is assuming that an S&P 500 fund, a Nasdaq-100 fund and a technology fund provide three separate exposures. All three may hold many of the same magnificent 7 stocks near the top of their holdings. Several fund names do not necessarily mean several different economic drivers.

## Does a Group Index Tell Us Which Member Did Best?

No. A group index summarizes returns under one methodology. It does not report each member's return, weight or earnings in a comparable way. A fair company ranking needs all seven measured with the same start date, end date, treatment of dividends and corporate actions.

J.P. Morgan's figures for Alphabet, Meta and Nvidia are useful examples, but they are not a complete ranking of all seven from one source. This page avoids building a league table from mismatched sources, because combining figures with different dates or methods can produce a misleading order.

### What a Fair Ranking Would Need

A sound comparison would use daily closing prices for all seven companies from one data provider, the same start and end dates, and a stated choice between price return and total return. Without those, any ranking is a mix of different measurements.

### Why Single Numbers Mislead

A headline saying "the Magnificent Seven fell 4.7%" can hide an 18.7% fall in one member and a smaller move in another. The spread inside the group counts as much as the average for anyone who owns individual shares rather than an index.

## What Does AI Spending Have to Do With the Group?

Several members are major buyers, sellers or enablers of AI infrastructure. Cloud providers finance data centers, chip designers supply processing power, and advertising and consumer-platform businesses build AI into their products. The same story can therefore move several members at once, even though their revenue sources differ.

The risk is not only that a product disappoints. A company can grow revenue and still see its shares fall if investors lower the value they place on its future profits. Heavy capital spending, borrowing costs and delayed returns can all affect that value. ### Buyers and Suppliers in One Group

Within the Magnificent Seven, Nvidia sells AI chips while others buy them in large quantities. A rise in spending can help the supplier and weigh on the buyers' cash flow at the same time. That is one reason the members do not always move together, even when the same theme is in the news.

### Spending Plans as a Market Signal

Investors increasingly treat each company's capital spending plans as news in their own right. When a large cloud provider raises its data-center budget, chip suppliers may rise while the buyer's shares fall on cash-flow concerns. When budgets are trimmed, the reverse can happen. Watching these plans helps explain why magnificent 7 stocks can split in opposite directions on the same day.

## How Big Is the Group's Influence?

The influence of these seven companies comes from their size. Market-value weighting means each company's share of an index rises and falls with its share price. As several of them grew into the largest companies in the world, they came to make up a large part of the S&P 500 and an even larger part of the Nasdaq-100.

That influence shows up in the gap between equal-weight and standard indexes. In October 2026, the standard S&P 500 trailed the equal-weight version by 5.9 points, the widest monthly gap since April 2009, according to Janus Henderson. A gap that wide is a sign that a small group of large companies moved very differently from the rest.

### How the Nasdaq-100 Compares

The Nasdaq-100 is even more concentrated in large technology companies than the S&P 500. FRED's Nasdaq-100 series shows a 6.65% price decline in October 2026, from 30,408.50 to 28,384.85, deeper than the S&P 500's 0.93% price decline. The difference reflects how much more of the Nasdaq-100 sits in the largest technology names.

### Why Size Changes the Risk

A company that makes up a large share of an index can move that index on its own. When several such companies share the same business theme, such as AI spending, they can move it together. That is the core reason concentration risk rises as a few companies grow.

## How Can an Investor Check Portfolio Overlap?

Look past the fund name to the holdings. Compare the largest positions across your funds, not just their categories. Then list the shared economic risks: reliance on advertising budgets, cloud spending, chip demand or interest-rate expectations.

### Dollar Weight Versus Risk Weight

There is a second kind of concentration worth checking. Two holdings of equal dollar value do not carry equal risk if one moves far more than the other. A volatile stock can drive most of a portfolio's swings even at a smaller weight.

### A Simple Overlap Exercise

Write down the top ten holdings of each fund you own, with their weights. Add up how much of your total portfolio sits in each of the seven companies across all funds. The total often surprises people who thought they were spread across hundreds of stocks.

## Does October Prove Equal Weight Is Better?

No. October shows that index construction strongly affects what "the market" means. It does not prove that equal-weight investing will always win. Equal-weight funds rebalance more often, which can raise costs, and they give more weight to smaller companies, which carry their own risks.

In periods when the largest companies lead, market-value-weighted indexes have tended to do better. In periods like October 2026, equal weighting did better. Neither approach wins every month, and past patterns are not a guide to which will lead next or for how long. ## How Concentration Shows Up in Everyday Investing

Most people never buy a Magnificent Seven fund on purpose, yet many hold a large slice of these companies. Workplace retirement plans, target-date funds and broad index funds often weight holdings by market value. As the largest companies grew, so did their share of these everyday products.

That means a selloff in a handful of large technology companies can show up in an account statement even for someone who thinks of their savings as broadly spread. It is not a reason to avoid index funds. It is a reason to understand what they hold.

### Checking a Retirement Plan

Most plan providers publish fund fact sheets listing the top holdings and their weights. Comparing those lists across the funds in a plan shows how much overlap exists. If the same companies appear at the top of every fund, the plan is more concentrated than its number of funds suggests.

### When Concentration Changes

Index weights move with prices. After a selloff in the largest companies, their weights fall and other companies' weights rise. After a strong rally, the reverse happens. Checking holdings once a year, or after a large market move, keeps the picture current.

## What the October Figures Do Not Show

The October data describes one month of prices. It does not show that the magnificent 7 stocks have stopped growing as businesses, that their earnings have fallen, or that the market will keep favoring smaller and value-style companies. It is a record of how investors priced these companies over a specific window of about one month.

It also does not show what any single investor earned. Someone who held the group through a fund, bought individual shares or held a broad index fund would each have had a different October, depending on their weights and entry dates.

## Common Mistakes With Magnificent 7 Stocks Figures

The first mistake is reading the group return as each member's return. The index fell 4.7%, while Alphabet alone fell 18.7%. The second is mixing sources and dates to rank the seven, which produces an unreliable order.

The third is assuming broad funds avoid the group. Most large U.S. index funds hold the magnificent 7 stocks near the top. The fourth is treating October as a permanent shift. Leadership between large and small, growth and value, has changed many times before, and one month says little about the next.
