# Highest Yield Dividend Stocks: 6 Large-Cap Income Names and Their Risks

The highest yield dividend stocks among large U.S.-listed companies in late October 2026 included Energy Transfer, UPS, Verizon, Altria, Pfizer, and Realty Income, each with an indicated yield between about 5.7% and 6.7%. A yield that high means more cash income per dollar invested only if the payout holds. It can also mean the market expects slower growth, heavy debt, falling earnings, or a future cut.

This list does not sort the whole market from highest yield to lowest. A raw screen of the highest yield dividend stocks tends to surface distressed small companies, mortgage REITs, business development companies, and preferred securities, which are hard to compare with large operating businesses. Instead, Fin Loom reviewed six established large-cap income names and checked each payout against company releases.

Every yield below was calculated by Fin Loom from the October 23, 2026 close and the latest declared regular payout, annualized and assumed to be maintained. Yields change whenever prices or payouts change. Inclusion here is not a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any security.

## Key Takeaways

- Energy Transfer had the highest indicated yield in this group, about 6.70%, after its 20th consecutive quarterly distribution increase.
- UPS followed at about 6.49%, but its annualized dividend equals about 91% of its 2026 adjusted earnings guidance.
- Verizon's indicated yield was about 6.34%, backed by $10.2 billion of first-half 2026 free cash flow.
- Altria's indicated yield was about 5.97% after an August 2026 increase to $1.11 per quarter.
- Pfizer's indicated yield was about 5.73%, with patent expirations and lower COVID-product revenue as central risks.
- Realty Income also yielded about 5.73% and measures payout coverage with AFFO rather than ordinary EPS.
- Energy Transfer is a master limited partnership, so its distributions and tax reporting differ from corporate dividends.
- A high yield works as a starting point for research, not as a ranking of quality or safety.

### What $10,000 Would Generate at These Yields

As an illustration, $10,000 at each indicated yield would produce about $670 a year from Energy Transfer, $649 from UPS, $634 from Verizon, $597 from Altria, and $573 each from Pfizer and Realty Income. These are Fin Loom calculations, before taxes, and they hold only if every payout and the purchase price stay exactly as stated.

### Why the Income Gap Between Them Is Small

The spread between the top and bottom of this group is about one percentage point, or roughly $97 a year on $10,000. That gap is small next to the effect of a single payout cut, which can remove a large share of the income at once. The business behind each payout carries more weight than the ranking itself.

## 1. Energy Transfer: About 6.70%

Energy Transfer is one of the largest U.S. midstream energy partnerships, with pipelines, storage, gathering, processing, and related infrastructure. On October 22, 2026, it announced its 20th consecutive quarterly increase, raising the distribution to $0.3425 per common unit, or $1.37 annualized. The distribution is payable November 19, 2026, to unitholders of record on November 6.

At the October 23 close of $20.45, that implies a yield of about 6.70%, calculated as $1.37 divided by $20.45. The company described the new rate as more than 3% above the third quarter of 2025.

### What Supports the Distribution

Second-quarter 2026 results showed net income attributable to partners of $2.09 billion, compared with $1.16 billion a year earlier, adjusted EBITDA of $5.07 billion, and distributable cash flow of $2.59 billion. Management also raised its 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $18.8 billion to $19.1 billion, up from $18.2 billion to $18.6 billion.

### The Risk Behind the Yield

Energy Transfer is a master limited partnership, not a conventional corporation. Holders receive partnership distributions rather than corporate dividends and generally receive a Schedule K-1 for taxes. The business is capital-intensive and carries substantial debt, so the high yield comes with structural and tax complexity that a headline percentage does not show.

## 2. UPS: About 6.49%

UPS is one of the world's largest package delivery and logistics companies. On August 5, 2026, it declared a quarterly dividend of $1.64 per share, payable September 3. That equals $6.56 a year if maintained. At the October 23 close of $101.06, the indicated yield was about 6.49%, a Fin Loom calculation of $6.56 divided by $101.06.

### What Supports the Dividend

UPS reported second-quarter 2026 revenue of $22.8 billion and raised its full-year adjusted diluted EPS guidance to about $7.22. The company said it expects about $5.4 billion of dividend payments in 2026, subject to board approval. That stated amount gives readers a sense of how much cash the dividend consumes in a year.

### Why the Yield Is High

The annualized dividend of $6.56 equals about 91% of the $7.22 adjusted EPS guidance, a Fin Loom calculation. That does not make a cut inevitable, but it leaves a thinner earnings cushion than a lower payout ratio would. UPS is also working through network restructuring, volume shifts, and a changing customer mix, which affect future cash generation.

## 3. Verizon: About 6.34%

Verizon operates one of the largest U.S. wireless and broadband networks. Its [dividend history page](https://www.verizon.com/about/investors/dividend-history) shows a quarterly dividend of $0.7075 per share declared September 9, 2026, and payable November 2, unchanged from the prior quarter. That is $2.83 annualized if maintained. At the October 23 close of $44.66, the indicated yield was about 6.34%.

### Cash Flow Is the Main Support

Verizon generated $10.2 billion of free cash flow in the first half of 2026, up from $8.8 billion in the first half of 2025. Second-quarter free cash flow alone was $6.4 billion. The company also reported 20 consecutive years of dividend increases as of its June 2026 declaration.

### The Main Tradeoff

Telecommunications needs heavy, continuing capital investment in networks and spectrum. Verizon had $136.5 billion of unsecured debt at the end of the second quarter of 2026, with net unsecured debt of $128.7 billion. Recurring subscription cash flow supports the dividend, while debt and competition limit how much room the company has.

## 4. Altria: About 5.97%

Altria owns leading U.S. tobacco and nicotine brands, including Marlboro through Philip Morris USA and the on! nicotine pouch brand through Helix. On August 27, 2026, [Altria raised its quarterly dividend](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/bizwire-2026-8-27-altria-increases-quarterly-dividend-to-111-per-share) by 4.7% to $1.11 per share from $1.06. The new annualized rate is $4.44 per share. At the October 23 close of $74.31, the indicated yield was about 5.97%.

### A Long Dividend Record

The company said the 2026 increase marked its 61st dividend increase in 57 years. It also restated a goal of mid-single-digit annual dividend-per-share growth through 2028. That stated goal is a target, not a promise, and it depends on future earnings and board decisions.

### How the Same Dividend Produced Two Yields

Altria's own release put the yield at 6.4%, based on a closing price of $69.12 on August 26, 2026. By October 23, the stock had risen to $74.31 and the same $4.44 dividend produced a yield of about 5.97%. Nothing changed in the payout. Only the price moved, which shows how quickly an indicated yield shifts.

### The Core Risk

U.S. cigarette volumes face a long-term structural decline. The investment case increasingly depends on pricing, cost control, and growth in smoke-free products. A high yield from Altria therefore cannot be separated from the long-term direction of the nicotine categories that produce its cash.

## 5. Pfizer: About 5.73%

Pfizer is a global pharmaceutical company with products across vaccines, oncology, internal medicine, and other areas. On October 6, 2026, it declared a fourth-quarter dividend of $0.43 per share, payable December 7 to holders of record on November 6. That is $1.72 annualized if maintained. At the October 23 close of $30.03, the indicated yield was about 5.73%.

### Dividend Continuity

Pfizer said the fourth-quarter 2026 payment will be its 352nd consecutive quarterly dividend. The company has repeatedly stated that it is committed to maintaining and, over the longer term, growing the dividend. That statement describes intent and is not a contractual obligation to shareholders.

### The Main Risk

Pfizer is working through lower COVID-product revenue and approaching loss of exclusivity on certain medicines. On August 4, 2026, it raised its 2026 revenue guidance to $60.5 billion to $62.5 billion and kept adjusted diluted EPS guidance at $2.80 to $3.00. The $1.72 dividend equals about 59% of the $2.90 midpoint, a Fin Loom calculation.

## 6. Realty Income: About 5.73%

Realty Income is a large net-lease REIT known as The Monthly Dividend Company. On September 8, 2026, it [raised its monthly dividend](https://www.realtyincome.com/sites/realty-income/files/09%2008%2026%20136th%20Common%20Stock%20Monthly%20Dividend%20Increase%20Declared%20by%20Realty%20Income.pdf) to $0.2715 per share, an annualized $3.258. The company said that was its 136th increase since its 1994 NYSE listing and that it owned over 15,500 properties as of June 30, 2026. At the October 23 close of $56.89, the indicated yield was about 5.73%.

### The Dividend Record Is Unusual

On October 13, 2026, Realty Income announced its 676th consecutive monthly dividend, payable November 13 to holders of record on October 30. The company also reports 116 consecutive quarterly dividend increases. A long record shows consistency in the past, but it does not remove future risk.

### Why AFFO Is the Better Coverage Measure

REIT depreciation can make standard EPS a weak guide to dividend coverage, so REITs report adjusted funds from operations, or AFFO. Realty Income reported that dividends paid in the second quarter of 2026 equaled 74.5% of its diluted AFFO per share of $1.09. That is the more relevant payout measure for this business.

### The Main Risk

Net-lease REITs are sensitive to interest rates, financing costs, tenant credit quality, acquisition returns, and property values. Higher borrowing costs can squeeze the spread between what a REIT earns on new properties and what it pays for capital. A strong dividend record does not remove those pressures.

## Why This List Is Not Ranked Only by Yield

The highest yield in a stock screener is often not the most secure income. A yield rises automatically when the share price falls, so a stock can show a very high yield right before a cut is announced. That is why this review checked more than the headline percentage for each company:

- **Latest Declared Payout:** The most recent regular payout, not an older or special amount.
- **Current Price:** One closing date for every company, so the yields are comparable.
- **Company Structure:** Corporation, REIT, or partnership, since each pays and reports differently.
- **Cash-Flow Support:** Free cash flow, distributable cash flow, or AFFO, depending on the business.
- **Recent Results:** The latest quarterly report and guidance.
- **Material Risks:** Debt, competition, regulation, and long-term demand.

A yield, even among the highest yield dividend stocks, is best treated as a starting point for research. The underlying business decides whether the payout can continue, which is the focus of [stock analysis fundamentals](/markets-stocks/stock-analysis-fundamentals/) such as cash flow, margins, and balance sheet strength.

## How Market Conditions Change Dividend Yields

Market direction affects every name on a list of the highest yield dividend stocks. In a broad sell-off, prices fall and yields rise across the board even when no payout changes. In a strong rally, yields fall for the same reason. Reading a yield without knowing the market backdrop can make a stock look cheaper or more generous than its business justifies.

### Falling Prices Can Inflate Yields

When a stock drops sharply, its yield climbs mechanically. Sometimes that reflects a broad market decline, and sometimes it reflects a specific concern about the company. The difference between a falling market and a falling company is explained in [bear vs bull stock market](/markets-stocks/bear-vs-bull-market/) cycles, which shape how far and how long prices move.

### Rising Prices Can Shrink Them

The Altria example shows the reverse. A price increase from $69.12 to $74.31 cut the indicated yield from 6.4% to about 5.97% with no change in the dividend. Investors comparing yields across dates should check that every figure uses the same closing date, as the table in this review does.

## The Tax Structure Changes the Comparison

The six names are not tax-equivalent, so a 6% yield from one can leave a different after-tax amount than a 6% yield from another. The structure of each company decides how the cash is reported and taxed.

### Corporations

Verizon, Altria, Pfizer, and UPS pay corporate dividends. Whether an individual dividend receives lower U.S. federal tax rates depends on IRS rules, including how long the shares were held around the ex-dividend date. The rules for [qualified dividends](/investing/qualified-dividends/) explain the holding period and the 0%, 15%, and 20% rate bands.

### REITs and Partnerships

Realty Income notes that its dividends are mostly taxed as ordinary income, though each year's breakdown can include different components. Energy Transfer is a partnership, and its distributions involve partnership tax reporting, generally on Schedule K-1. Comparing these with qualified corporate dividends on the headline yield alone can overstate the after-tax income.

## What to Check When Evaluating a High-Yield Stock

A consistent checklist makes it easier to compare highest yield dividend stocks on more than one number. The items below come from the same review applied to the six companies above:

- **Cash Generation:** Free cash flow or distributable cash flow relative to the payout.
- **Payout Ratio:** Measured with the metric that fits the business, such as AFFO for a REIT.
- **Debt Load:** Total borrowing and interest expense compared with cash flow.
- **Dividend History:** The length of the record and any past cuts.
- **Growth Trend:** Whether revenue and earnings are rising, flat, or falling.
- **Capital Needs:** How much the business must reinvest to stay competitive.
- **Cyclicality:** How sensitive the industry is to the economy.
- **Guidance:** What management has said about future results and the payout.
- **Valuation:** What the price implies about expectations.

No single item settles the question. A higher yield adds income only when the business can keep paying it, and the checklist is a way to judge that more carefully.

## Highest Yield Dividend Stocks Need Context, Not Just Rankings

The highest yield dividend stocks in this group paid between about 5.7% and 6.7% at the October 23, 2026 close. Each one pairs that income with a distinct risk: partnership structure for Energy Transfer, a high payout ratio for UPS, heavy debt for Verizon, declining cigarette volumes for Altria, patent expirations for Pfizer, and interest-rate sensitivity for Realty Income.

Reading those risks alongside the yield gives a fuller picture than any ranking. Yields will change as prices move and boards declare new payouts, so every figure here is a snapshot of one date rather than a forecast.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is Considered a High Dividend Yield?

There is no universal threshold. A yield is best compared with the company's industry, business risk, cash flow, interest rates, payout history, and the broader market at the time.

### Why Can a Falling Stock Have a Higher Dividend Yield?

Dividend yield equals the annual dividend divided by the share price. If the price falls while the dividend stays the same, the calculated yield rises automatically.

### Is the Highest Dividend Yield the Best Stock?

Not necessarily. Very high yields can signal financial stress or an expected cut. Cash flow, debt, earnings, and business quality need review before drawing any conclusion about a stock.

### Are REIT Dividends the Same as Corporate Dividends?

No. REIT distributions often receive different U.S. tax treatment from qualified corporate dividends, and AFFO is usually more useful than EPS for judging REIT payout coverage.

### Why Is Energy Transfer Different From the Other Five?

Energy Transfer is a master limited partnership. It pays partnership distributions and generally issues Schedule K-1 tax forms instead of the dividend reporting used by corporations.
