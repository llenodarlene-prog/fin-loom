# CPI vs PPI vs PCE: How the Three Inflation Measures Differ

Three price indexes dominate U.S. inflation news. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) and the Producer Price Index (PPI) come from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). The Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index comes from the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA). The CPI vs PPI vs PCE question is really about whose prices each one tracks and how each is built.

Those choices produce different numbers from the same economy. This guide explains each index from its official source, shows why the readings diverge, and lines up September 2026 figures calculated from Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED) data. Sources were reviewed on November 21, 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- **CPI tracks consumer prices:** BLS defines it as the average change over time in prices paid by consumers for a representative basket of goods and services.
- **PPI tracks producer prices:** BLS says it measures selling prices received by domestic producers, from the seller's perspective.
- **PCE tracks spending by and for households:** BEA says it covers spending by and on behalf of the personal sector, including nonprofits serving households.
- **The Fed targets PCE:** the Federal Open Market Committee aims for 2% inflation as measured by the annual change in the PCE price index.
- **CPI and PCE differ for four reasons:** BEA groups them into formula, weight, scope and other effects.
- **September 2026 readings:** FinLoom calculations from FRED index levels show CPI up 3.72%, PCE up 3.65% and PPI final demand up 6.29% from a year earlier.

## What Each Index Measures

In the CPI vs PPI vs PCE comparison, the three indexes answer different questions. CPI asks how much prices have changed for what consumers buy. PPI asks how much prices have changed for what domestic producers sell. PCE asks how much prices have changed across all consumer spending, including spending made on consumers' behalf.

That framing explains most of the differences. The buyer, the seller and the basket each change the result. A tax, an import or a hospital bill paid by an insurer can count in one index and not another. Knowing what each one includes makes the headlines easier to read.

### The Consumer Price Index

The [BLS CPI questions and answers](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/questions-and-answers.htm) page defines the CPI as a measure of the average change over time in the prices paid by consumers for a representative basket of goods and services. The most cited version, the CPI-U, covers urban consumers.

BLS says the CPI-U population makes up over 90 percent of the U.S. population and covers households in all areas of the country. It excludes rural nonmetropolitan residents, farm households, people on military installations, religious communities and people in institutions. A narrower version, the CPI-W, covers about 30 percent of the population.

### The Producer Price Index

The [BLS PPI questions and answers](https://www.bls.gov/ppi/faqs/questions-and-answers.htm) page says the PPI measures the average change over time in the selling prices received by domestic producers of goods and services. BLS adds that PPIs measure price change from the perspective of the seller.

BLS notes a primary use of the PPI is to deflate revenue streams to measure real growth in output. The headline series used in this guide is final demand, which FRED publishes as the Producer Price Index by Commodity: Final Demand.

## CPI vs PPI vs PCE Side by Side

The table below summarizes how the three measures differ, based on BLS, BEA and FRED descriptions. It focuses on the headline versions of each index.

| Feature | CPI | PPI | PCE Price Index |
|---|---|---|---|
| Publisher | Bureau of Labor Statistics | Bureau of Labor Statistics | Bureau of Economic Analysis |
| Perspective | Buyer (consumer) | Seller (domestic producer) | Spending by and for households |
| Covers imports | Yes | No | Spending-based, not seller-based |
| Sales and excise taxes | Included | Excluded | Not covered in sources reviewed |
| Formula | Modified Laspeyres | Not covered in sources reviewed | Fisher-Ideal |
| Main use noted in sources | Consumer price change | Deflating revenue to measure real output | Federal Reserve's 2% inflation target |
| FRED index base | 1982-1984 = 100 | November 2009 = 100 | 2017 = 100 |

Where a cell says a detail was not covered, the sources reviewed for this CPI vs PPI vs PCE guide did not state it, so no claim is made. The remaining rows come straight from BLS, BEA and FRED descriptions.

### Reading the Table

Each row of the CPI vs PPI vs PCE table points to a design choice. The perspective row is the most important. CPI and PCE both track consumer prices, but from different angles. PPI looks from the other side of the sale, at what producers receive rather than what buyers pay.

The tax and import rows show why CPI and PPI can diverge. BLS says sales and excise taxes are included in CPI prices because consumers must pay them, but excluded from PPI prices because they are not revenue to the producer. BLS also says the CPI includes imports while the PPI excludes them.

### Why the Index Bases Differ

Each FRED series uses a different base period, shown in the last row. An index base sets the reference point equal to 100. A CPI reading of 336.298 for September 2026 means prices are about 3.4 times their 1982-1984 average. The base does not affect percentage changes, which are what headlines report.

That is why index levels cannot be compared directly across the three measures. A PPI level of 159.699 and a PCE level of 132.257 do not show that one inflation rate is higher. Only percentage changes over the same period make a fair comparison, which is what the next section uses.

## Why CPI and PCE Give Different Numbers

CPI and PCE both track consumer prices, yet they rarely match. BEA's [FAQ on comparing the PCE price index and the CPI](https://bea.gov/help/faq/555) groups the differences into four categories: a formula effect, a weight effect, a scope effect and other effects.

BEA explains the formula effect. The PCE price index is based on the Fisher-Ideal formula, while the CPI is based on a modified Laspeyres formula. The formulas treat changes in what people buy differently. The FRED description of PCE notes that if beef prices rise, shoppers may buy less beef and more chicken, and the PCE index is known for reflecting such changes.

### Weight and Scope

The weight effect reflects how much each item counts in the index. BEA says it accounts for the relative importance of the underlying items in each index. Housing is the classic example. In BEA's worked example, imputed rent of owner-occupied housing was the largest contributor to the weight effect.

The scope effect reflects what each index covers. BEA says PCE measures spending by and on behalf of the personal sector, which includes households and nonprofit institutions serving households, while the CPI measures out-of-pocket spending by households. In BEA's worked example, hospital and nursing home services were the largest PCE items outside the CPI's scope.

### BEA's Historical Example

BEA's FAQ gives a worked example from the third quarter of 2006. The PCE price index rose 3.0 percent at an annual rate, while the CPI rose 3.7 percent. The 0.7 percentage point gap reflected positive contributions from the weight effect (0.84 point), other effects (0.29 point) and the formula effect (0.16 point), partly offset by the net scope effect (minus 0.50 point).

The example is old, published by BEA in 2010, but it shows how the pieces fit together. The size and direction of each effect shift over time. That is why the gap between CPI and PCE changes from month to month rather than holding at a fixed amount.

## How PPI Differs From CPI

Within CPI vs PPI vs PCE, the PPI and CPI measure prices on opposite sides of a sale. BLS says the PPI reflects prices from the seller's perspective, while the CPI and similar measures reflect prices from the purchaser's perspective. A price the producer receives can differ from the price the consumer pays because of taxes, imports and distribution.

BLS lists other differences too, including the mix of goods and services covered, the types of prices collected and coverage of services. One example is owners' equivalent rent, which is part of the CPI but not the PPI. Because housing is a large share of consumer spending, that single item can push the two indexes apart.

### Does PPI Lead CPI?

People often watch PPI as a possible early signal for consumer prices, on the idea that producer costs pass through to shelves. The sources reviewed for this guide do not state that PPI predicts CPI. Since the two indexes cover different goods, services, taxes and imports, a rise in one does not guarantee a matching rise in the other.

It is fair to say the two are linked through the supply chain, but the link is loose. A jump in energy prices may show up in both. A change in rent shows up in CPI but not PPI. Treating PPI as one input among many is more defensible than treating it as a forecast.

### Why PPI Swings More

PPI readings can move sharply. In the September 2026 figures below, PPI final demand rose faster than CPI or PCE. The sources reviewed do not break down why, but PPI's seller perspective and its different mix of goods and services mean it can move at a different pace.

The Federal Reserve's stated target uses PCE, not PPI. A consumer-focused measure lines up with the Fed's description of price stability for households and businesses. PPI is still useful for businesses tracking input costs and for economists measuring real output.

These CPI vs PPI vs PCE readings all use the same month, so the comparison is like for like. Each change divides the newer index level by the older one and subtracts one.

### What the Readings Show

In this snapshot, CPI and PCE ran close together, with CPI slightly higher. PPI final demand rose much faster. Both consumer measures sat above the Federal Reserve's 2% longer-run goal for PCE inflation. These are single-month comparisons, so a different month could show a different pattern.

The calculation is simple to check. For CPI, 336.298 divided by 324.245, minus one, gives about 3.72%. The same method applies to the other two rows. The [inflation rate by year](/money-banking/inflation-rate-by-year/) guide shows how CPI inflation has moved over a longer period.

### A Note on Missing Data

FRED's CPI series shows no value for October 2025. Without that month, a year-over-year CPI change for October 2026 cannot be calculated from the series, which is why the table uses September for all three measures. PPI final demand had an October 2026 reading of 160.254, up about 6.52% from October 2025.

Gaps like this are a reminder to check the data before relying on a headline number. Official releases and FRED series notes explain revisions and missing periods. Using the same month for every measure keeps the comparison fair.

## Why the Federal Reserve Uses PCE

The Federal Reserve explains its target in an FAQ. It says the Federal Open Market Committee judges that inflation of 2 percent over the longer run, measured by the annual change in the PCE price index, is most consistent with its mandate for maximum employment and price stability. FRED's description of PCE also calls it the Fed's preferred measure of inflation.

The Fed adds that when households and businesses can reasonably expect inflation to stay low and stable, they can make sound decisions about saving, borrowing and investing. That is why PCE readings draw close attention around Federal Reserve meetings, even though CPI often gets more headlines.

### Why Savers Watch All Three

Inflation affects the real return on cash. A savings account or CD paying less than inflation loses buying power over time. Short-term rates, such as those on Treasury bills, are one way savers compare returns against inflation. The [treasury bills vs CD](/money-banking/treasury-bills-vs-cd/) comparison shows how those rates stood in November 2026.

In the CPI vs PPI vs PCE trio, each index offers a slightly different lens. CPI tracks what consumers pay out of pocket. PCE is the Fed's target. PPI hints at cost pressure earlier in the supply chain. Watching all three, rather than one, gives a fuller picture of price trends.

### Inflation and Longer-Term Plans

Inflation also shapes how people think about goals years away. Money held as cash keeps its dollar value but can lose buying power. Investments can grow faster than inflation over long periods but carry risk. The [saving vs investing](/money-banking/saving-vs-investing/) guide covers how to weigh those trade-offs for different goals.

None of the three indexes predicts what any single household will pay. A household's own spending mix, location and habits shape its personal inflation rate. The national measures are averages that describe the economy as a whole.

## The Bottom Line

The CPI vs PPI vs PCE comparison comes down to perspective and design. CPI tracks what consumers pay, PPI tracks what domestic producers receive, and PCE tracks spending by and for households. BEA attributes CPI and PCE differences to formula, weight, scope and other effects. The Fed targets 2% PCE inflation. In September 2026, CPI and PCE rose about 3.7% from a year earlier, while PPI final demand rose about 6.3%.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Main Difference Between CPI and PPI?

CPI measures prices paid by consumers, while PPI measures selling prices received by domestic producers. BLS says CPI includes imports and sales taxes, while PPI excludes both.

### Why Does the Federal Reserve Prefer PCE?

The Federal Open Market Committee sets its 2% longer-run inflation goal using the PCE price index. PCE covers spending by and on behalf of households, a broader scope than CPI.

### Why Are CPI and PCE Different?

BEA groups the differences into four effects: formula, weight, scope and other factors. PCE uses a Fisher-Ideal formula, while CPI uses a modified Laspeyres formula.

### Does PPI Predict Future CPI?

Not reliably. The two indexes cover different goods, services, taxes and imports. PPI can signal cost pressure, but a PPI rise does not guarantee a matching CPI rise.
