# Debt Consolidation Calculator: Will a New Loan Save Money?

This debt consolidation calculator compares paying off up to three debts at your current payments with combining them into one loan. It shows the interest on each path, the new monthly payment and whether consolidating saves money once an origination fee is included.

```calculator
debt-consolidation
```

## How the Comparison Works

Each current debt is paid at its monthly payment until it is gone, with interest charged monthly. The consolidation loan is sized so that the cash you receive covers all balances after the fee. With $16,000 across three cards and a 4-year loan at 11% with a 3% fee, the new payment is about $426.32 and consolidating saves about $3,809.85 in interest. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## A Lower Payment Is Not Always a Saving

A longer loan term can cut the monthly payment while raising total interest. Compare the interest on both paths, not only the payment, before deciding.

## Avoiding New Debt

Consolidation only helps if the old balances stay paid off. Running up new charges on cleared cards can leave you with more debt than before. The calculator does not model new charges.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Debt Consolidation Save Money?

It can when the new rate and fees cost less than the interest on your current debts. The calculator compares both paths with your own numbers.

### Why Is the Loan Bigger Than My Balances?

An origination fee is often deducted from the proceeds. The calculator sizes the loan so the cash you receive still covers every balance.

### What If a Payment Is Too Low?

If a payment does not cover a debt's monthly interest, it would never be paid off. The calculator flags this so you can raise the payment.
