# Business Valuation Calculator: Multiple and Capitalized Earnings

This business valuation calculator gives two quick estimates of what a business may be worth: an earnings multiple and capitalized earnings. It then adds cash and subtracts debt to estimate the value of the owners' equity.

```calculator
business-valuation
```

## Earnings Multiple Method

Multiply yearly earnings by a multiple. At 3 times $250,000 of earnings, the business is worth about $750,000 before cash and debt. Multiples vary widely by industry, size, growth and risk, so the multiple you choose shapes the result most. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Capitalized Earnings Method

This method divides next year's earnings by the required return minus growth. With a 20% required return and 3% growth, $250,000 of earnings is worth about $1,514,706, an implied multiple of about 6.06. The gap between the two methods shows how sensitive valuation is to assumptions.

## From Business Value to Equity Value

Business value is what the operations are worth. Adding cash and subtracting debt gives an estimate of what the owners' stake is worth. Working capital needs, owner pay adjustments and deal terms can shift the final price.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Value a Small Business?

Common approaches include earnings multiples, capitalized or discounted earnings and asset-based methods. This calculator covers the first two as planning estimates.

### What Earnings Should I Use?

Use the measure your multiple is based on, such as seller's discretionary earnings or EBITDA. Mixing measures gives misleading results.

### Is This a Formal Appraisal?

No. It is a planning estimate. A formal valuation for a sale, tax or legal purpose should come from a qualified appraiser.
