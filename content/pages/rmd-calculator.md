# RMD Calculator: Required Minimum Distribution by Age

This RMD calculator works out the required minimum distribution from a traditional IRA or workplace retirement account. Enter the account balance on December 31 of last year and your age at the end of this year, and it returns this year's minimum withdrawal using the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table, plus a projection of future RMDs.

```calculator
rmd
```

## When Required Minimum Distributions Start

The [IRS says](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-required-minimum-distributions-rmds) you generally have to start taking withdrawals from an IRA, SIMPLE IRA, SEP IRA or retirement plan account when you reach age 73. It explains that the RMD for any year is the account balance at the end of the previous year divided by a distribution period from the Uniform Lifetime Table.

## How the RMD Calculator Works

The calculator looks up your age in [IRS Publication 590-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b), Appendix B, Table III, and divides your prior year-end balance by that distribution period. At age 73 the period is 26.5, so a $500,000 balance gives an RMD of about $18,868, or 3.77% of the account. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

The distribution period shrinks each year, so the share you must withdraw rises with age. At 85 the period is 16.0, which means withdrawing 6.25% of the balance.

## Which Table Applies

The Uniform Lifetime Table is for unmarried owners, married owners whose spouse is not more than 10 years younger, and married owners whose spouse is not the sole beneficiary. The IRS says to use a different table when the sole beneficiary is a spouse who is ten or more years younger. Inherited accounts follow separate rules.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

The projection assumes a steady return and that each RMD is taken in full. Taxes on withdrawals are not included. Roth IRAs and inherited accounts are not covered. For a binding figure, check with your account provider or a tax professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Is an RMD Calculated?

Divide the account balance on December 31 of the prior year by the distribution period for your age in the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table.

### At What Age Do RMDs Start?

The IRS says you generally must start taking withdrawals from IRAs and retirement plan accounts when you reach age 73.

### What Is the Distribution Period at Age 75?

The IRS Uniform Lifetime Table lists 24.6 for age 75. A $400,000 balance would give an RMD of about $16,260 that year.
