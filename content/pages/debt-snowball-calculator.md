# Debt Snowball Calculator: Snowball vs Avalanche Payoff Plan

This debt snowball calculator builds a payoff plan for up to five debts. Enter each balance, APR and minimum payment, plus the total you can pay each month, and it shows the order debts are paid off, how long it takes and the interest you could pay. It runs the avalanche method too, so you can compare the two side by side.

```calculator
debt-snowball
```

## How the Debt Snowball Calculator Works

Each month, every debt accrues interest at its APR divided by 12 and receives its minimum payment. Whatever is left of your budget goes to one target debt. With the snowball method, the target is the smallest balance; with the avalanche method, it is the highest APR. When a debt is paid off, its minimum stays in the budget and rolls into the next target.

With the defaults, three debts totaling $16,500 and a $650 monthly budget are cleared in 2 years and 6 months either way. The snowball method pays about $2,863 of interest and the avalanche method about $2,586, a difference of about $277. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Snowball or Avalanche?

The [CFPB's guide to reducing debt](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/how-reduce-your-debt/) describes both. The highest interest rate method, often called the avalanche, pays off the costliest debt first and can save money over time. The snowball method pays off the smallest debt first so you see progress quickly, but it may cost more in the long run.

The CFPB suggests choosing based on what motivates you: saving the most money, or seeing debts disappear sooner. When your smallest debt also has the highest rate, the two methods give the same result.

## How to Use the Results

Look at the payoff order and the month each debt is cleared. If the interest gap between the methods is small, the snowball's early wins may be worth it. If the gap is large, the avalanche usually saves more. ## What the Calculator Leaves Out

New charges, fees, promotional rates, rate changes and minimum payments that fall as balances shrink are not modeled. Minimums are held fixed and capped at what each debt still owes. The plan stops at 50 years if the budget is too low.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Debt Snowball Method?

You pay the minimum on every debt and put any extra money toward the smallest balance. When it is paid off, that payment moves to the next smallest debt.

### Is the Avalanche Method Better Than the Snowball?

The avalanche usually costs less interest because it targets the highest APR first. The snowball can clear individual debts sooner, which some people find more motivating.

### What Happens if My Budget Is Below the Minimums?

The calculator asks for at least the total of your minimum payments. Paying less than the minimums can lead to late fees and damage to your credit.
