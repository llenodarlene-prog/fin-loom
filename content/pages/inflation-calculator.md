# Inflation Calculator: What a Dollar From Any Year Is Worth Today

This inflation calculator uses the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics to compare a dollar amount across years. Enter an amount and two years, and it shows the equivalent value, the cumulative price change, and the average yearly inflation in between.

```calculator
inflation
```

## How the Inflation Calculator Works

The calculation scales an amount by the change in the price index between two dates:

**Adjusted value = Original amount × (End CPI ÷ Start CPI)**

For example, the CPI-U averaged 172.2 in 2000 and 336.940 in September 2026, so $100 in 2000 had about the same buying power as $195.67 in September 2026. That is a cumulative price increase of about 95.7%, an average of about 2.6% a year. These are Fin Loom calculations from the published index.

When you leave the month blank, the calculator uses that year's annual average, which is the average of its published monthly index values. Choosing a month uses that single month instead, which is useful for comparing a specific paycheck, price, or contract date.

## What the Results Mean

**Equivalent value** is the amount that would buy roughly the same basket of goods and services at the end date. **Cumulative inflation** is the total percentage change in the index. **Purchasing-power change** shows how much less (or more) the original dollars would buy at the end date. **Average annual inflation** spreads the total change evenly across the years, as a compound rate.

The chart traces the CPI-U annual average across your selected years, and the table lists each year's index and its change from the year before. For a fuller history with the annual rate for every year, see the [inflation rate year by year](/money-banking/inflation-rate-by-year/).

## About the Data

The calculator embeds the CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted (1982-84 = 100), BLS series CUUR0000SA0, from January 1913 through September 2026, as published on the [FRED database of the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCNS). It runs entirely in your browser and makes no live data requests.

BLS did not publish an October 2025 index because of the 2025 lapse in appropriations, so that month cannot be selected, and the 2025 annual average uses the 11 published months. The 2026 figures cover January through September.

## Limits of an Inflation Calculator

The CPI-U measures average price changes for urban consumers. Your own costs can rise faster or slower depending on what you buy, where you live, and how your spending changes over time. The calculator also does not account for wage growth, taxes, or investment returns, so it describes buying power rather than whether savings kept pace.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where Does the Inflation Data Come From?

The calculator uses the BLS CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items index, not seasonally adjusted, from January 1913 through September 2026.

### Why Is There No October 2025 Option?

BLS did not collect or publish an October 2025 CPI because of the 2025 lapse in appropriations, so the 2025 annual average uses 11 months.

### Is the Result the Price of a Specific Item?

No. It shows equivalent overall buying power. Individual prices, such as rent, gasoline, or tuition, can change much faster or slower than the index.
