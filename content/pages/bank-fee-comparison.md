# Bank Fee Comparison Worksheet: Yearly Cost of Two Accounts

This bank fee comparison worksheet shows what two checking or savings accounts would cost you in a year. Enter each account's monthly fee, waiver status, ATM, overdraft and wire fees, and APY from the bank's fee schedule, then how often you expect each event. It totals the fees, subtracts interest earned and shows which account is cheaper for the way you bank.

## How the Bank Fee Comparison Worksheet Works

Yearly cost equals the monthly fee times 12, unless you meet the waiver, plus ATM fees times uses, overdraft fees times overdrafts, wire fees times wires and any other yearly fees, minus interest on your average balance. With the defaults, an account with a $12 monthly fee, $3 ATM fee, $35 overdraft fee and a 0.01% APY costs about $281 a year at the usage shown. A no-fee account with a $25 wire fee paying 3.5% on a $5,000 balance earns about $150 more in interest than it charges, about $431 a year better in net terms. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Where to Find Each Fee

Banks publish fee schedules and account disclosures. Look for the monthly maintenance fee and how to waive it, out-of-network ATM fees, overdraft and returned-item fees, and wire transfer fees. The worksheet does not include any bank's fees, so enter the current figures yourself.

## What the Worksheet Leaves Out

ATM owner surcharges, foreign transaction fees, paper statement fees and fee caps are not included unless you add them as other fees. Interest uses a single average balance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do I Compare Bank Account Fees?

List each account's fees, estimate how often each applies to you, total them for a year and subtract the interest each account would pay on your balance.

### Do Monthly Fee Waivers Change the Comparison?

Often. Many accounts waive the monthly fee for a minimum balance or direct deposit. Tick the waiver box if you would meet the condition.

### Why Include Interest in a Fee Comparison?

An account paying interest offsets some of its fees. Netting interest against fees gives a fairer view of what each account really costs.
