# Car Refinance Calculator: Compare Payments and Interest

This car refinance calculator compares your current auto loan with a new one for the same balance. It shows the change in monthly payment, the interest left on each loan and whether total remaining payments go up or down once fees are included.

```calculator
car-refinance
```

## How the Comparison Works

The calculator works out the payment that pays off your current balance over the payments you have left, then the payment on a new loan for the same balance plus any fees rolled in. Refinancing $20,000 from 9% to 6% with 48 payments on both loans and $200 of fees lowers the payment from about $497.70 to $474.40, and total remaining payments fall by about $1,118.56. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Watch the Loan Term

A longer new term can lower the monthly payment even when total cost rises, because you pay interest for longer. Compare the change in total remaining payments as well as the monthly figure before deciding.

## Fees and Timing

Lenders and states may charge title or lender fees to refinance. Rolled-in fees are repaid with interest. Refinancing early in a loan usually saves more interest than refinancing near the end, when most interest has already been paid.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When Does Refinancing a Car Loan Make Sense?

It can make sense when a lower rate cuts total remaining payments after fees. Compare total cost, not only the monthly payment, especially if the new term is longer.

### Does Refinancing Extend My Loan?

Only if you choose a longer term. Keeping the same number of payments shows the pure rate effect, while a longer term lowers the payment and can add interest.

### Are There Fees to Refinance a Car?

There can be lender and title transfer fees, depending on the lender and state. Enter any fees you plan to roll into the new loan to include them.
