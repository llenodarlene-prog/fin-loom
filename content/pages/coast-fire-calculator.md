# Coast FIRE Calculator: Your Coast Number and Retirement Gap

This Coast FIRE calculator shows how much you would need invested today so that, with no further contributions, your savings could grow to your retirement goal by a chosen age. Enter your age, retirement age, planned spending, current investments, and your own return and inflation assumptions.

```calculator
coast-fire
```

## What Coast FIRE Means

FIRE stands for financial independence, retire early. "Coasting" means you have saved enough that compound growth alone could carry your savings to the amount you need at retirement. You would still need income to cover living costs until then, but new retirement contributions would no longer be required under the assumptions you choose.

## How the Coast FIRE Calculator Works

First, the calculator sets a FIRE number: annual retirement spending divided by your withdrawal rate. Then it discounts that number back to today using a real return, which is (1 + expected return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. The result is your Coast FIRE number.

With the defaults, $50,000 of yearly spending at a 4% withdrawal rate needs $1,250,000 in today's dollars. At a 7% return and 2.5% inflation over 35 years, the Coast FIRE number today is about $277,852. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Why the Assumptions Carry So Much Weight

Over decades, small changes in the assumed return change the answer a lot. Lowering the return from 7% to 6% in the default example raises the Coast FIRE number substantially, because growth compounds over 35 years. The withdrawal rate also moves the FIRE number directly: a lower rate means a larger goal.

Real returns are not steady. A market fall close to retirement can leave a shortfall even if the long-run average matches your assumption. Testing several return and inflation settings gives a range rather than a single answer.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Taxes, investment fees, Social Security, pensions and part-time income are not included. Spending is assumed to stay level in today's dollars. The result is an estimate, not a guarantee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate Coast FIRE?

Divide annual retirement spending by your withdrawal rate to get a FIRE number, then divide that by one plus the real return raised to the years until retirement.

### What Return Should I Use?

Any return is an assumption. Many people test a range, because a lower return raises the amount needed today and markets do not grow at a steady rate.

### Does Coast FIRE Mean I Can Stop Working?

No. It means retirement savings could grow to the goal without new contributions. You would still need income to pay current living costs until you retire.
