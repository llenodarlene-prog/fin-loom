# Social Security Break-Even Calculator: Claim Early or Wait?

This Social Security break-even calculator compares claiming retirement benefits at two different ages. Enter the monthly benefit estimates from your own Social Security statement for each age, and it shows the age at which the larger, later benefit catches up with the earlier one in total dollars received.

```calculator
ss-breakeven
```

## How the Break-Even Calculator Works

Claiming early gives you smaller checks for more years. Waiting gives you larger checks for fewer years. The calculator adds up each option month by month and finds the point where the later claim's running total overtakes the earlier one.

With the defaults, $1,400 a month from age 62 versus $2,000 a month from age 67 breaks even at about age 78 and 8 months. The early claimant collects $84,000 before 67, and the later claimant's extra $600 a month closes that gap in about 11 years and 8 months. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Why the Calculator Uses Your Own Estimates

Your benefit at each age depends on your earnings record and birth year. Rather than estimate it, the calculator uses the figures from your personal Social Security statement, which you can view in your online account with the Social Security Administration. That keeps the comparison tied to your real numbers.

An optional cost-of-living adjustment applies the same yearly increase to both options. Leaving it at zero keeps everything in today's dollars.

## More Than a Break-Even Age

The break-even age is one input to the decision, not the answer. Health and family longevity, other income, whether you are still working, taxes, and spousal or survivor benefits all affect which age suits you. Married couples in particular may want to look at how the higher earner's claiming age affects a surviving spouse.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Taxes, Medicare premiums, the earnings test, spousal and survivor benefits, and investing early benefits are not included.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is a Social Security Break-Even Age?

It is the age at which total benefits from claiming later equal the total from claiming earlier. Living past that age means waiting paid more in total.

### Where Do I Find My Benefit Estimates?

Your personal Social Security statement shows estimated monthly benefits at different claiming ages. You can view it through your online account with the Social Security Administration.

### Does the Break-Even Age Include Taxes?

No. It compares gross monthly benefits. Taxes, Medicare premiums and other income can change the net amount you receive at each age.
