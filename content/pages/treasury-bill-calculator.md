# Treasury Bill Calculator: Price, Discount Rate and Yield

This Treasury bill calculator works out what a T-bill costs and what it earns. Enter the face value, either the discount rate or the price per $100, and the days to maturity. It returns the purchase cost, the interest you receive at maturity and the investment rate, using the formulas the U.S. Treasury publishes.

```calculator
treasury-bill
```

## How Treasury Bills Work

[TreasuryDirect explains](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/treasury-bills/) that bills are sold at a discount or at face value and pay the full face value at maturity. The interest is the difference between what you pay and what you get back. Bills are sold for terms of 4, 6, 8, 13, 17, 26 and 52 weeks, with a $100 minimum purchase.

Because a bill pays no coupon, its return is set entirely by the purchase price. A lower price means more interest.

## How the Treasury Bill Calculator Works

The price per $100 of face value is 100 × (1 − discount rate × days ÷ 360), the formula in TreasuryDirect's [guide to understanding pricing](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/understanding-pricing/). Treasury's own example: a $1,000, 26-week bill at a 0.145% discount rate sells for $999.27, earning $0.73.

With the defaults, a $1,000 bill with 182 days to maturity at a 4% discount rate costs about $979.78 and pays $20.22 of interest. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Discount Rate Versus Investment Rate

The discount rate is measured against face value and a 360-day year, so it understates the return on the money you actually invest. The investment rate, also called the coupon equivalent yield, is measured against the price you pay and a 365- or 366-day year. That makes it easier to compare with savings accounts, CDs and bonds.

For bills of up to half a year, Treasury computes it as ((100 − price) ÷ price) × (days in year ÷ days to maturity). For longer bills, Treasury uses a quadratic formula, which this calculator applies. Its results match Treasury's published worked examples.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Auction prices are rounded by Treasury, so real amounts can differ by cents. Taxes and any brokerage fees are not included. The calculator does not predict future auction rates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Is a Treasury Bill Price Calculated?

Price per $100 equals 100 times one minus the discount rate times days to maturity divided by 360. TreasuryDirect publishes this formula.

### Why Is the Investment Rate Higher Than the Discount Rate?

The investment rate divides interest by the lower price you pay and uses a 365-day year, while the discount rate uses face value and a 360-day year.

### What Is the Minimum Treasury Bill Purchase?

TreasuryDirect lists a $100 minimum, and bills are sold in $100 increments. You receive the full face value when the bill matures.
