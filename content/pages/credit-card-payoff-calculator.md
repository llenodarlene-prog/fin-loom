# Credit Card Debt Payoff Calculator: When Could Your Balance Be Paid Off?

This credit card debt payoff calculator estimates how long it could take to clear a card balance and how much interest you could pay along the way. Enter your balance, APR, and monthly payment, then add an extra payment to see how much sooner the debt could be gone and how much interest it could save.

## How the Credit Card Payoff Calculator Works

The calculator works one month at a time. Each month it adds interest, then subtracts your payment:

**Monthly interest = Balance × APR ÷ 12**

**New balance = Balance + Monthly interest − Payment**

It repeats until the balance reaches zero, and counts the months. The first payment is counted as next month, so the payoff date is the month of the final payment.

For example, a $5,000 balance at 24% APR accrues $100 of interest in the first month. A $200 monthly payment would clear it in 36 months, with about $2,000.56 of total interest, a Fin Loom calculation from the engine above. With a $150 minimum, the same balance could take 56 months and about $3,322.09 of interest. Adding $100 a month on top of that $150 could shorten the payoff to 26 months, saving about $1,872.72 of interest under these assumptions.

## Fixed Minimums and Percent-Based Minimums

Many cards set the minimum payment as a percent of the balance plus that month's interest, with a dollar floor. Because that minimum shrinks as the balance falls, paying only the minimum can stretch repayment over many years. The Advanced options let you model that rule: a 1% of balance plus interest minimum with a $25 floor would take about 234 months, or 19.5 years, to clear the $5,000 example, with about $8,886.95 of interest. That is also a Fin Loom calculation. Your card agreement and monthly statement show the exact rule your issuer uses.

## What the Results Mean

**Months to pay off** and the **estimated payoff date** show when the final payment could be made. **Total interest** is every interest charge added along the way, and **total paid** is the balance plus that interest. **Interest saved** compares your plan with paying the minimum alone.

The comparison cards show the same balance with the minimum only, $50 extra, $100 extra, and your custom amount. The chart plots both balances month by month, and the table shows payments, interest, principal, and the ending balance for each year of your plan.

If a payment is not larger than the monthly interest, the balance cannot fall, and the calculator says so instead of showing a date. Results are capped at 600 months.

## Limits of a Credit Card Payoff Estimate

The calculator assumes no new purchases, a constant APR, and on-time payments with no fees. Card issuers usually calculate interest on the average daily balance and may change the APR, apply promotional rates, or add late fees, so statement figures can differ. Paying down revolving balances can also change credit utilization, one of the factors described in [how credit scores work](/money-banking/what-is-a-credit-score/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Is Credit Card Interest Calculated Here?

Each month the calculator charges the balance times the APR divided by 12, then subtracts your payment. Real cards often use the average daily balance, so results are estimates.

### Why Does the Calculator Say My Balance Is Not Paid Off?

Your payment does not exceed the monthly interest, or payoff would take more than 600 months. Raising the payment shows a payoff date.

### Does an Extra Payment Make a Big Difference?

It can. In the $5,000 example at 24% APR, adding $100 to a $150 minimum could cut payoff from 56 to 26 months under these assumptions.
