# Options Profit Calculator: Call and Put Profit at Expiration

This options profit calculator shows the profit or loss from buying a call or put and holding it to expiration. It works out the cost, the value at expiration, the break-even stock price and the maximum loss, with a table of results at different stock prices.

## How Option Profit Is Calculated

At expiration, a call is worth the stock price minus the strike, and a put is worth the strike minus the stock price, never less than zero. One contract usually covers 100 shares. Buying one call with a $100 strike for $4 plus $1 of fees costs $401. If the stock ends at $110, the call is worth $1,000, a profit of $599. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Break-Even and Maximum Loss

A bought call breaks even when the stock reaches the strike plus the premium and fees per share, here $104.01. The most you can lose when buying an option is what you paid, because you can let it expire unused.

## What This Calculator Does Not Show

Before expiration, an option's price also depends on time left and expected volatility, so selling early can give a different result. Selling options, spreads, assignment and taxes are not modeled. Options carry a high risk of loss.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate Profit on a Call Option?

Subtract the strike from the stock price at expiration, floor it at zero, subtract the premium, then multiply by 100 shares per contract and subtract fees.

### What Is the Break-Even Price for a Put?

It is the strike minus the premium and fees per share. The stock must fall below that price at expiration for a bought put to make money.

### Can I Lose More Than the Premium When Buying Options?

Not when buying a call or put. The maximum loss is the premium plus fees. Selling options can carry larger losses, which this calculator does not cover.
