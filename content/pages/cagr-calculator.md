# CAGR Calculator: Compound Annual Growth Rate

This CAGR calculator finds the compound annual growth rate between two values. Enter where an investment, business figure or any other amount started, where it ended and how many years passed, and it returns the steady yearly rate that links the two, plus the total change and how long that rate takes to double a value.

```calculator
cagr
```

## How the CAGR Calculator Works

The formula is CAGR = (ending value ÷ beginning value)^(1 ÷ years) − 1. With the defaults, growth from $10,000 to $18,000 over 5 years is a CAGR of about 12.47%, even though the total change is 80%. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

Fractional years work too. Enter 2.5 for two and a half years.

## Why CAGR Differs From Average Return

A simple average of yearly returns can mislead when returns swing. An investment that rises 50% and then falls 50% has an average return of 0%, yet it ends at 75% of where it began. CAGR captures that loss because it compounds. For that reason it is a better summary of what actually happened between two dates.

## What CAGR Does Not Show

CAGR smooths the path. Two investments with the same CAGR can have taken very different routes, one steady and one volatile. It also ignores money added or withdrawn along the way; with contributions, a money-weighted return is the right measure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the CAGR Formula?

CAGR equals the ending value divided by the beginning value, raised to one divided by the number of years, minus one. Multiply by 100 for a percentage.

### Can CAGR Be Negative?

Yes. If the ending value is lower than the beginning value, CAGR is negative, showing the steady yearly rate of decline over the period.

### Is CAGR the Same as Annual Return?

Not exactly. CAGR is the steady rate that links two values. Actual yearly returns may have been higher in some years and lower or negative in others.
