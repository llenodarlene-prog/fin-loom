# Home Affordability Calculator: How Much House Can You Afford?

This home affordability calculator estimates how much house you could afford from your income, monthly debts, down payment and loan terms. It uses two debt ratios that you set, a housing ratio and a total debt ratio, so you can test conservative and stretch budgets.

```calculator
home-affordability
```

## How Affordability Is Estimated

The calculator caps your monthly housing payment at the lower of two limits: your gross monthly income times the housing ratio, and your income times the total debt ratio minus other debts. On $100,000 of income with $500 of monthly debts, a 28% housing ratio and a 36% total debt ratio, the cap is about $2,333 a month. With $40,000 down at 6.5% for 30 years, that supports a price of about $336,610. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Choosing Your Ratios

The ratios are your inputs, not fixed rules. Lenders set their own limits, which vary by loan type, credit and other factors. A lower ratio leaves more room in the budget for savings and surprises, while a higher one stretches the price you could reach.

## What the Estimate Leaves Out

PMI, HOA dues, closing costs and moving costs are not included. Maintenance and repairs also add to the cost of owning. Leaving a cushion below the maximum is a sensible way to absorb them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Much House Can I Afford on $100,000 a Year?

With $500 of monthly debts, $40,000 down, a 6.5% rate and 28% and 36% ratios, this calculator estimates about $336,610. Your result depends on your inputs.

### What Is the 28/36 Rule?

It is an often-cited budgeting guideline: housing up to 28% of gross income and total debts up to 36%. The calculator uses these as defaults that you can change.

### Does Affordability Include Property Taxes?

Yes. The housing payment includes principal, interest, property tax and insurance. PMI and HOA dues are not included, so add a cushion if they apply.
