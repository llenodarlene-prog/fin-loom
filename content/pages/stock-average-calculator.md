# Stock Average Calculator: Find Your Average Cost Per Share

This stock average calculator finds the average price you paid for a stock bought in several purchases. Enter the shares, price, and any fees for each buy, and it shows your average cost per share, total shares, total invested, and, if you add a current price, an estimate of your unrealized gain or loss.

```calculator
stock-average
```

## How the Stock Average Calculator Works

Each purchase adds its cost and its shares to the position. The average cost is the total cost divided by the total shares:

**Total cost = Sum of (shares × price + fees)**

**Average cost per share = Total cost ÷ Total shares**

For example, buying 100 shares at $50 and later 50 shares at $40 costs $5,000 plus $2,000, or $7,000 for 150 shares. The average cost is $7,000 ÷ 150, about $46.67 per share. The average sits closer to $50 than to $40 because more shares were bought at the higher price. These are Fin Loom calculations.

A simple average of the two prices would give $45, which is wrong whenever the purchases are different sizes. Weighting by shares is what makes the result match what you paid.

## Why Fees Change the Result

Commissions and other purchase fees add to what you paid, so the calculator includes them in the total cost. With fees included, the average cost per share is also your break-even price: the share price at which selling every share would return exactly what you put in, before any selling costs or taxes.

The results also show the average cost before fees, so you can see how much the fees added. On small purchases, even a few dollars of fees can move the break-even price noticeably.

## Averaging Down and Averaging Up

Buying more shares after a price drop lowers the average cost, which is often called averaging down. Buying after a price rise raises it, called averaging up. The calculator shows the effect of either before or after you trade, and the bar chart compares each purchase price with the resulting average.

A lower average cost does not by itself make a position safer or more likely to recover. It also increases the money committed to one company. Weighing the business itself, as covered in [stock analysis fundamentals](/markets-stocks/stock-analysis-fundamentals/), and the size of the position, as covered in [stock risk metrics](/investing/stock-risk-metrics/), helps put a lower average in context. Fin Loom does not recommend buying, selling, or holding any security.

## Limits of a Stock Average Calculator

The calculator treats every purchase as part of one position with no sales in between. It does not adjust for stock splits, reinvested dividends, or corporate actions, which brokers handle in their own records. For taxes, brokers and the IRS track cost basis by individual lot, so the tax result of a sale can differ from what one blended average suggests.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate the Average Cost of a Stock?

Add the cost of every purchase, including fees, then divide by the total number of shares you bought. That gives a share-weighted average rather than a simple average of prices.

### Does the Average Cost Include Fees?

Yes, if you enter them. Fees are added to total cost, so the average cost per share also equals your break-even price before selling costs and taxes.

### What Is a Break-Even Price?

It is the share price at which the position's market value equals what you paid, including purchase fees. Selling costs and taxes would raise the true break-even point.
