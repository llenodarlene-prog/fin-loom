# Salary to Hourly Calculator: Convert Annual Pay

This salary to hourly calculator turns an annual salary into an hourly rate, plus daily, weekly, biweekly and monthly equivalents. It also shows your pay per hour actually worked once paid days off are taken out, which helps when comparing a salaried offer with an hourly one.

```calculator
salary-hourly
```

## How to Convert Salary to Hourly Pay

Divide the annual salary by the paid hours in a year. For a full-time job paid every week, that is hours per week × 52. A $60,000 salary at 40 hours a week is $60,000 ÷ 2,080 hours = $28.85 an hour. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

Monthly pay is the salary ÷ 12, and biweekly pay is the weekly figure × 2. All results are gross pay, before taxes and payroll deductions.

## Pay per Hour Actually Worked

Paid vacation, holidays and sick days raise the value of each hour you work. With 15 paid days off on a 5-day, 40-hour week, you work 120 fewer hours a year, so $60,000 works out to about $30.61 per hour worked. That figure is useful when comparing a salary with an hourly job that does not pay for time off.

## What the Hourly Figure Leaves Out

The calculator converts pay only. Benefits such as health insurance, retirement matching and bonuses add value that an hourly rate does not show. Overtime rules also differ between salaried and hourly roles, so check how your employer classifies the job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do I Convert an Annual Salary to an Hourly Rate?

Divide the salary by paid hours per year. At 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, that is 2,080 hours, so a $60,000 salary equals about $28.85 an hour before tax.

### How Many Work Hours Are in a Year?

A full-time schedule of 40 hours a week for 52 weeks is 2,080 paid hours. Hours actually worked are lower once paid holidays and vacation are taken out.

### Is the Hourly Rate Before or After Tax?

Before tax. The calculator converts gross pay. Federal and state income tax, Social Security, Medicare and other deductions reduce take-home pay.
