# Profit Margin Calculator: Margin, Markup and Price

This profit margin calculator finds gross profit, margin and markup from a cost and a selling price. It also works backward: enter a target margin to see the price you would need to charge, along with totals for the number of units you sell.

```calculator
profit-margin
```

## How Profit Margin Is Calculated

Gross profit is the selling price minus the cost. Margin is that profit divided by the selling price. With a $60 cost and a $100 price, profit is $40 and the margin is $40 ÷ $100 = 40%. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Margin vs Markup

Markup divides the same profit by the cost instead of the price. In the example, markup is $40 ÷ $60 = 66.67%. Margin can never reach 100%, while markup has no upper limit. Mixing the two is a common pricing mistake, which is why the calculator shows both.

## Pricing for a Target Margin

To hit a target margin, divide the cost by one minus the margin. For a 50% margin on a $60 cost, the price is $60 ÷ 0.5 = $120. The table in the calculator shows the price needed for several common margins at your cost.

## Gross vs Net Margin

This tool measures gross margin, the profit left after the direct cost of each unit. Net margin subtracts operating expenses, interest and taxes too, so it is lower. A product can have a healthy gross margin and still lose money if overhead is high.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is a Good Profit Margin?

It depends on the industry, because cost structures differ widely. Compare your margin with similar businesses and with your own past results rather than a single benchmark number.

### What Is the Difference Between Margin and Markup?

Both use the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price, and markup divides it by the cost. A 40% margin equals a 66.67% markup.

### How Do I Price for a 50% Margin?

Divide your cost by 0.5. A product costing $60 needs a $120 price for a 50% gross margin, which is the same as a 100% markup.
