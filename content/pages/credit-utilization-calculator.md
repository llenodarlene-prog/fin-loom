# Credit Utilization Calculator: Ratio by Card and Overall

This credit utilization calculator shows how much of your available credit you are using. Enter the balance and credit limit for up to five cards, and it returns each card's ratio, your overall ratio and how much you would need to pay down to reach a target you choose.

```calculator
credit-utilization
```

## How the Credit Utilization Calculator Works

Utilization is a balance divided by its credit limit. The overall ratio adds all balances and divides by all limits. With the defaults, $1,200 on a $5,000 card and $900 on a $3,000 card give $2,100 on $8,000 of credit, or 26.3% overall. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

The pay-down figure is the balance minus the target ratio times the limit. If the result is below zero, you are already at or under the target.

## Why Credit Utilization Is Watched

The [CFPB's guidance on credit scores](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-get-and-keep-a-good-credit-score-en-318/) says scoring models look at how much of your credit limit you are using. It notes many experts recommend using less than 30% of your total limit, and that consumers with the highest scores often use less than 10%.

[myFICO explains](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/credit-scores/amount-of-debt) that amounts owed make up 30% of a FICO Score, and that a high utilization ratio on revolving accounts can signal a borrower is overextended. FinLoom's guide to [what a credit score is](/money-banking/what-is-a-credit-score/) covers the other factors.

## Overall Ratio Versus Each Card

Scoring models can consider both your overall ratio and the ratio on individual cards. One card near its limit can stand out even when the overall figure looks low, which is why the calculator shows both and counts the cards above your target.

## Timing Your Payments

Card issuers usually report the statement balance to the credit bureaus. Paying part of a balance before the statement closes can lower the balance that is reported, even if you pay in full every month.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

The tool shows ratios only. Credit scoring formulas are proprietary, so it does not estimate a score. Interest charges and credit limit changes are not modeled.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate Credit Utilization?

Divide a card's balance by its credit limit. For all cards, add the balances and divide by the total of the limits, then multiply by 100 for a percentage.

### What Is a Good Credit Utilization Ratio?

The CFPB says many experts recommend staying under 30% of your total limit, and that people with the highest credit scores often use less than 10%.

### Does Paying in Full Mean Zero Utilization?

Not always. Issuers usually report your statement balance, so a balance can show on your report even if you pay it in full by the due date.
