# Roth vs Traditional IRA Calculator: Which Leaves More After Tax?

This Roth vs traditional IRA calculator compares the two accounts on the same footing: the same pre-tax earnings set aside each year. Enter the amount, the years until retirement, an assumed return and two tax rates, and it shows the after-tax value of each account at retirement.

```calculator
roth-traditional
```

## How the Roth vs Traditional IRA Calculator Works

A traditional IRA contribution can be tax-deductible now, and withdrawals are taxed later. A Roth contribution is made with after-tax money, and qualified withdrawals are tax-free. To compare them fairly, the calculator starts from the same pre-tax earnings. The traditional IRA invests all of it. The Roth invests what is left after paying tax at today's rate.

Both balances grow at the same assumed return. At retirement, the traditional balance is taxed at your retirement rate. With the defaults, $7,000 a year for 30 years at 6%, a 22% rate today and 12% in retirement, the traditional IRA is worth about $486,998 after tax and the Roth about $431,658. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## The Tax Rates Decide the Answer

With the same return in both accounts, the comparison comes down to two numbers. If your tax rate in retirement is lower than today, the traditional IRA ends higher. If it is higher, the Roth ends higher. If the rates are equal, both end with the same after-tax value. Change the two rates to see the result flip.

## 2026 IRA Limits

The [IRS announced](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500) that the 2026 IRA contribution limit is $7,500, up from $7,000 for 2025, with a $1,100 catch-up for people aged 50 and over. The [IRS IRA contribution limits page](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-ira-contribution-limits) explains how the limit applies across all of your IRAs. The calculator warns you if the amount entered is above the limit.

Income limits also apply. For 2026, the IRS says the Roth IRA phase-out range is $153,000 to $168,000 for single filers and heads of household, and $242,000 to $252,000 for married couples filing jointly. The calculator does not check eligibility.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

The calculator does not model income limits, state taxes, required minimum distributions, early-withdrawal penalties or the five-year rule for Roth earnings. It assumes a constant return and year-end contributions. Real returns vary, and future tax rates are uncertain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Roth or Traditional IRA Better?

It depends mostly on tax rates. If you expect a lower rate in retirement, a traditional IRA tends to come out ahead; if higher, a Roth tends to. Equal rates give equal results.

### What Is the IRA Contribution Limit for 2026?

The IRS set it at $7,500 for 2026, plus a $1,100 catch-up for people aged 50 and over. The limit applies across all of your traditional and Roth IRAs combined.

### Why Does the Roth Invest Less Each Year?

The comparison starts from the same pre-tax earnings. A Roth contribution is made after tax, so less of each pre-tax dollar goes into the account than with a traditional IRA.
