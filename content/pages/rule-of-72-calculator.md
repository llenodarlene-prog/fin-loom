# Rule of 72 Calculator: How Long to Double Your Money

This Rule of 72 calculator estimates how long money takes to double at a steady annual rate, and compares the shortcut with the exact answer. It also works in reverse: enter a number of years to see the rate you would need to double your money in that time.

```calculator
rule-of-72
```

## How the Rule of 72 Works

Divide 72 by the annual rate in percent. At 8%, money doubles in about 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years. The exact doubling time with annual compounding is ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + rate), which at 8% is 9.01 years, so the shortcut is very close. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

The rule works in reverse too: to double in 10 years, you need about 72 ÷ 10 = 7.2% a year. The exact figure is 7.18%.

## How Accurate Is the Rule of 72?

The table in the calculator shows the shortcut and the exact answer side by side. The rule is closest for rates around 6% to 10%. At very low rates it slightly understates the time, and at high rates it overstates it. Some people use 70 for low rates, which the calculator also shows.

## Using It With Inflation and Fees

The same rule shows how fast inflation halves buying power: at 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years. Fees work the same way in reverse. A fund fee lowers the net rate, which stretches the doubling time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Rule of 72?

It is a shortcut for estimating doubling time. Divide 72 by the annual rate in percent to get the approximate number of years for an amount to double.

### How Accurate Is the Rule of 72?

It is close for typical rates. At 8% it gives 9 years against an exact 9.01 years, and it becomes less precise at very low or very high rates.

### Can the Rule of 72 Find a Required Rate?

Yes. Divide 72 by the number of years. To double money in 10 years, you need roughly a 7.2% annual return, or 7.18% exactly.
