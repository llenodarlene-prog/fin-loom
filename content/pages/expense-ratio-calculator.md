# Expense Ratio Calculator: What Fund Fees Cost Over Time

This expense ratio calculator compares two funds that earn the same return before fees. Enter a starting amount, monthly contributions, a time frame, an assumed return and each fund's expense ratio, and it shows the ending balance for each fund, the total fees and the gap between them.

```calculator
expense-ratio
```

## How the Expense Ratio Calculator Works

An expense ratio is the yearly fee a fund charges, expressed as a percentage of your balance. The calculator works month by month. The balance grows at the monthly equivalent of the annual return, then the fee, one-twelfth of the expense ratio, comes out of the balance, then the month's contribution is added.

With the defaults, $10,000 to start, $300 a month, 30 years and a 7% return before fees, a fund charging 0.03% ends at about $424,208 and one charging 0.75% at about $363,913. The gap is about $60,295, even though the higher-fee fund's total fees are about $30,755. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Why the Balance Gap Is Larger Than the Fees

Every dollar taken as a fee also stops growing. Over many years, the growth those dollars would have earned adds up. That is why the difference in ending balance is larger than the difference in fees paid.

## Where to Find a Fund's Expense Ratio

Fund companies list the expense ratio on each fund's fact sheet and in its prospectus. Vanguard's fact sheet for its S&P 500 ETF, for example, lists 0.03%. FinLoom's comparisons of [FXAIX vs VOO](/investing/fxaix-vs-voo/) and [VOO vs SPY](/investing/voo-vs-spy/) show how small fee gaps between similar funds translate into dollars.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Only the expense ratio is counted. Trading costs inside the fund, account fees, sales loads and taxes are not included. Both funds are assumed to earn the same return before fees, which is reasonable for funds tracking the same index but not for funds with different strategies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is an Expense Ratio?

It is the yearly fee a fund charges, shown as a percentage of your investment. A 0.50% expense ratio costs about $5 a year for each $1,000 invested.

### How Much Do Expense Ratios Cost Over Time?

Small differences compound. In the default example, a 0.72-point fee gap leads to about $60,000 less after 30 years, because fees also remove future growth.

### Does the Calculator Include Other Fund Costs?

No. It counts only the expense ratio. Trading costs inside the fund, account fees, sales charges and taxes are not included in the comparison.
