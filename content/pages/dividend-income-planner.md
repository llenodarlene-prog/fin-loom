# Dividend Income Planner: Project Yearly Dividend Income

This dividend income planner projects how much dividend income a portfolio could produce over time. Enter today's portfolio value, its dividend yield, an assumed yearly dividend growth rate, any yearly contributions and whether dividends are reinvested. It shows income year by year, total dividends, and the portfolio size needed to reach an income goal at today's yield.

```calculator
dividend-income
```

## How the Dividend Income Planner Works

Year one income is portfolio value times yield. Each year, the dividend paid on each dollar grows by the growth rate, and contributions and any reinvested dividends are added to the portfolio. With the defaults, $100,000 at a 3.5% yield earns about $3,500 in year one. With 5% dividend growth, $6,000 added each year and dividends reinvested, income reaches about $43,584 in year 20. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

A $12,000 income goal needs about $342,857 at a 3.5% yield, which the default plan's income reaches in year 11.

## The Assumptions Behind the Projection

Share prices are held flat so the projection isolates dividend income. In reality, prices rise and fall, which changes both the portfolio's value and the yield on new money. Dividend growth is also an assumption; companies can raise, cut or suspend dividends. FinLoom's guide to [dividend vs dividend yield](/investing/dividend-vs-dividend-yield/) explains how the two measures relate.

## What the Planner Leaves Out

Taxes on dividends, fund fees and changes in yield on new purchases are not included. The figures are illustrations, not forecasts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Much Do I Need to Live Off Dividends?

Divide your yearly income goal by the portfolio's dividend yield. At a 3.5% yield, $12,000 a year of income needs about $342,857 invested.

### Does Reinvesting Dividends Increase Income?

Yes, under these assumptions. Reinvested dividends buy more of the portfolio, which pays more dividends the following year.

### Are Dividends Guaranteed?

No. Companies can reduce or stop dividends at any time, so projected income depends on the growth assumption holding.
