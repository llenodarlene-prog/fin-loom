# Portfolio Rebalancing Calculator: Allocation, Drift and Trades

This portfolio rebalancing calculator analyzes your allocation and works out the trades to bring it back to target. Enter up to six holdings with their current value and target weight. It shows each holding's current weight, how far it has drifted, which holdings are outside your chosen band, and the amount to buy or sell. You can also add new cash and see how to split it.

```calculator
portfolio-rebalance
```

## How the Portfolio Rebalancing Calculator Works

Each holding's weight is its value divided by the portfolio total. Drift is the current weight minus the target. The trade to target is the target weight times the portfolio value, plus any new cash, minus the holding's current value.

With the defaults, a $100,000 portfolio holds $62,000 in a U.S. stock fund against a 50% target, $18,000 in an international fund against 20% and $20,000 in bonds against 30%. Restoring the targets means selling $12,000 of the U.S. fund and buying $2,000 of the international fund and $10,000 of bonds. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Why Portfolios Drift

When one holding rises faster than the others, it becomes a larger share of the portfolio, and the overall risk can change without any new decision. Rebalancing restores the mix you chose. Some investors rebalance on a schedule, others when drift passes a set band, which the calculator lets you set.

## Rebalancing With New Money

Directing new contributions to underweight holdings can reduce drift without selling. Enter the cash amount under advanced options to see how the trades change.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Values are what you enter for one date; live prices are not used. Selling in a taxable account can create capital gains, and trading costs are not included. The calculator does not recommend any investment or allocation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate Rebalancing Trades?

Multiply each target weight by the portfolio value, then subtract the holding's current value. A positive result is a buy and a negative result is a sell.

### How Often Should a Portfolio Be Rebalanced?

Some investors use a calendar schedule and others a drift band, such as five percentage points. The right approach depends on costs, taxes and your plan.

### Can I Rebalance Without Selling?

Often, yes. Putting new contributions into underweight holdings can bring weights back toward target without selling and possibly creating taxable gains.
