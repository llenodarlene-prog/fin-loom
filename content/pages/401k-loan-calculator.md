# 401(k) Loan Calculator: Payments, IRS Limits and Lost Growth

This 401(k) loan calculator shows what borrowing from your retirement plan would cost. Enter your vested balance, the loan amount, the plan's rate, the term and how often you are paid. It returns your payment, the IRS maximum for your balance, the interest repaid to your account and an estimate of the growth the money could miss while it is out of the market.


## IRS Limits on 401(k) Loans

The [IRS explains](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-regarding-loans) that plans are not required to offer loans. If they do, the maximum is the greater of $10,000 or 50% of your vested balance, or $50,000, whichever is less. Prior loans in the last 12 months can reduce the $50,000 figure.

Repayment must occur within 5 years, in substantially equal payments of principal and interest made at least quarterly. The IRS notes that a loan used to buy your main home may be repaid over a longer period.

## How the 401(k) Loan Calculator Works

The payment is a standard level payment over the number of periods. With the defaults, $20,000 at 8.5% over 5 years paid every two weeks means 130 payments of about $189.09, with about $4,582 of interest. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

The interest goes back into your own account, so it is not lost the way bank loan interest is. The real cost is the growth the borrowed money might have earned in the market. The calculator estimates that using the return you enter.

## Default Risk

The IRS says a loan in default is generally treated as a taxable distribution of the outstanding balance. Leaving a job can change the repayment timeline under your plan's rules, so check the plan document before borrowing.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Loan fees, taxes on a deemed distribution and the 12-month reduction for prior loans are not modeled. Lost growth is an estimate based on a steady return.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Much Can I Borrow From My 401(k)?

The IRS limit is the lesser of $50,000 or the greater of $10,000 or half your vested balance. Your plan may set a lower limit or offer no loans.

### How Long Do I Have to Repay a 401(k) Loan?

The IRS requires repayment within 5 years in substantially equal payments made at least quarterly. A loan to buy your main home may run longer.

### What Happens If I Default on a 401(k) Loan?

The IRS says a defaulted loan is generally treated as a taxable distribution of the outstanding balance, and extra taxes may apply depending on your age.
