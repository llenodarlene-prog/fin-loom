# Emergency Fund Calculator: Your Target, Gap and Timeline

This emergency fund calculator turns your essential monthly expenses into a savings target, shows how far your current savings go toward it, and estimates how many months regular deposits could take to close the gap. You choose how many months of expenses to cover, so the target fits your own situation.

```calculator
emergency-fund
```

## How the Emergency Fund Calculator Works

The target is essential monthly expenses multiplied by the number of months you want covered. With the defaults, $3,000 a month for 6 months gives an $18,000 target. Savings of $5,000 leave a $13,000 gap, and $500 a month closes it in 26 months with no interest. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

If you add an APY under advanced options, each month's balance earns the monthly equivalent of that rate before the next deposit is added. Interest shortens the timeline a little, but the deposit amount usually has the larger effect.

## How Much Should an Emergency Fund Be?

There is no single right number. The [CFPB's guide to building an emergency fund](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/) says the amount depends on your situation, and suggests thinking about the unexpected expenses you have faced before and what they cost. It also notes that even a small amount can add financial security.

That is why the calculator lets you pick the months of cover. A household with steady income and two earners may choose fewer months than a self-employed worker with uneven pay. Starting with a smaller first goal, then raising it, is a common approach.

## Which Expenses to Include

Count the costs you would still need to pay if income stopped: housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, transportation, and minimum payments on debts. Leave out spending you could pause, such as dining out or travel. Using essential expenses rather than total spending keeps the target realistic.

## Where to Keep the Money

Emergency savings work best where they are safe and easy to reach. FinLoom's guide to [saving vs investing](/money-banking/saving-vs-investing/) explains why short-term money usually belongs in insured savings rather than investments that can fall in value.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Deposits and the APY are held steady, and no withdrawals are modeled. Taxes on interest are not included. Real expenses change, so revisiting the target once a year keeps it accurate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Is an Emergency Fund Target Calculated?

Multiply essential monthly expenses by the months you want covered. For example, $3,000 a month for 6 months gives a target of $18,000.

### How Many Months Should an Emergency Fund Cover?

It depends on your income stability, household and costs. The CFPB suggests basing the goal on your situation and past unexpected expenses rather than one fixed rule.

### Does Interest Change How Fast I Reach the Goal?

A little. Interest adds to the balance each month, but on typical savings rates the monthly deposit drives most of the progress toward the target.
