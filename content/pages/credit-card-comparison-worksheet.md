# Credit Card Comparison Worksheet: Rewards, Fees and Interest

This credit card comparison worksheet estimates what up to three cards could be worth to you each year. Enter each card's annual fee, purchase APR, rewards rate and sign-up bonus from its disclosures, then your monthly spending and any balance you carry. It shows rewards earned, fees, interest, first-year value and value in later years for each card.

```calculator
card-compare
```

## How the Credit Card Comparison Worksheet Works

Yearly value equals rewards on your spending, plus any sign-up bonus in the first year, minus the annual fee and the interest on any balance you carry. With the defaults, $1,500 a month on a no-fee card earning 1.5% gives $270 a year, plus a $200 bonus in year one. A card earning 2% with a $95 fee gives $360 of rewards and $265 after the fee, plus a $250 bonus. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## Why Carrying a Balance Changes Everything

Interest is charged on balances you do not pay in full. At a 22% APR, carrying a $2,000 average balance costs about $440 a year, more than many cards return in rewards. The [credit utilization calculator](/money-banking/credit-utilization-calculator/) shows how carried balances also affect the share of credit you are using.

The [CFPB's guidance on credit scores](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-get-and-keep-a-good-credit-score-en-318/) notes that paying the balance in full each month helps keep interest costs as low as possible.

## What the Worksheet Leaves Out

Rewards are treated as a flat rate at face value. Category bonuses, spending caps, point valuations, foreign transaction fees and changing terms are not included. Enter terms from each card's official disclosures; this worksheet does not rate or recommend cards.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do I Compare Credit Card Rewards?

Multiply your yearly spending by each card's rewards rate, add any first-year bonus, then subtract the annual fee and any interest you would pay.

### Is a Card With an Annual Fee Worth It?

It can be if the extra rewards on your spending exceed the fee. The worksheet shows value both in the first year and in later years.

### Should I Choose a Card for Rewards if I Carry a Balance?

Interest on a carried balance often costs more than rewards earn, so the APR usually deserves more weight than the rewards rate.
