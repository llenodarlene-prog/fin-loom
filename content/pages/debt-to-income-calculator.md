# Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator: Find Your DTI

This debt-to-income ratio calculator shows how much of your gross income goes to debt payments each month. Enter your income, monthly or yearly, and your monthly payments for housing, auto loans, student loans, card minimums and other debts. It returns your DTI, the housing share of income and how much your payments would need to fall to reach 36%.

```calculator
dti
```

## How the Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator Works

The [CFPB defines debt-to-income](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-debt-to-income-ratio-en-1791/) as all your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income, which is your pay before taxes and other deductions. Its example: $1,500 for a mortgage, $100 for an auto loan and $400 for other debts total $2,000 a month. On $6,000 of gross monthly income, the DTI is 33%.

The calculator uses the same method and those numbers as its defaults. It also shows housing alone, which some lenders look at separately.

## Why Lenders Look at DTI

The CFPB says DTI is one way lenders measure your ability to manage the payments on money you plan to borrow. A lower ratio leaves more room in the budget. Each lender sets its own limits and may count debts differently, so the figure here is a guide rather than a lending decision.

## Ways to Lower Your DTI

The ratio falls when payments go down or income goes up. Paying off a small loan removes its whole payment from the calculation. The [debt snowball calculator](/money-banking/debt-snowball-calculator/) shows how quickly a structured plan could clear several debts.

## What the Calculator Leaves Out

Utilities, groceries and other living costs are not debts, so they are not counted. Lenders may add property taxes, insurance and association fees to the housing payment, which would raise the ratio.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate Debt-to-Income Ratio?

Add up all monthly debt payments and divide by gross monthly income, then multiply by 100. The CFPB's example of $2,000 on $6,000 gives 33%.

### Is DTI Based on Gross or Net Income?

Gross. The CFPB describes gross monthly income as what you earn before taxes and other deductions are taken out.

### Do Living Expenses Count Toward DTI?

No. DTI counts debt payments such as mortgages, loans and card minimums. Groceries, utilities and similar living costs are not included.
