# Interest-Only Loan Calculator: Payments Before and After

This interest-only loan calculator shows the monthly payment while you pay only interest, how much it rises once principal repayment starts, and the extra interest compared with a loan that amortizes from the start.

## How Interest-Only Payments Work

During the interest-only period, each payment covers just the interest, so the balance does not fall. On a $400,000 loan at 7%, that is about $2,333.33 a month. After 10 years, the full balance must be repaid over the remaining 20 years, so the payment jumps to about $3,101.20. These are Fin Loom calculations from the inputs shown.

## The Cost of Paying Interest Only

Because no principal is repaid early on, total interest is higher. In the example, total interest is about $624,286.98, roughly $66,251.39 more than a 30-year loan that amortizes from the first payment.

## Plan for the Payment Jump

The step up when the interest-only period ends can be large. Many interest-only loans also have adjustable rates, which this calculator does not model, so the later payment could differ.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is an Interest-Only Loan?

It is a loan where early payments cover only interest for a set period. After that, payments rise to repay the principal over the remaining term.

### Why Does the Payment Go Up Later?

The full balance must be repaid in fewer years once the interest-only period ends, so each payment includes a larger share of principal.

### Do Interest-Only Loans Cost More?

Usually yes over the full term, because the balance stays higher for longer and accrues more interest than a loan that amortizes from the start.
