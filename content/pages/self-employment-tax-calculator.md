# Self-Employment Tax Calculator: Estimate Your 2026 SE Tax

This self-employment tax calculator estimates the Social Security and Medicare tax that freelancers, contractors, and sole proprietors pay on their own earnings for tax year 2026. Enter your net self-employment earnings, and it follows the Schedule SE steps to show the tax, how it splits between Social Security and Medicare, and the half you can deduct.

## How Self-Employment Tax Is Calculated

Employees split Social Security and Medicare tax with their employer. Self-employed people pay both halves, which is why the combined rate is 15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare.

The tax does not apply to every dollar of profit. According to [IRS Topic 554](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554), "the amount subject to self-employment tax is 92.35% of your net earnings from self-employment," and you usually owe the tax only when net earnings are $400 or more. The calculator applies those steps in order:

- **Net Earnings:** Business or 1099 income minus ordinary and necessary business expenses.
- **Taxable Base:** Net earnings times 92.35%.
- **Social Security Part:** 12.4% of the taxable base, up to the wage base.
- **Medicare Part:** 2.9% of the whole taxable base, with no cap.

With $50,000 of net earnings, the taxable base is $46,175. Social Security tax is $5,725.70, Medicare tax is $1,339.08, and the total self-employment tax is $7,064.78, an effective rate of about 14.1% of net earnings. These are Fin Loom calculations from the 2026 rates.

## The 2026 Social Security Wage Base

Social Security tax stops once combined wages and self-employment earnings reach the annual wage base. For 2026, [IRS Topic 751](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751) puts that limit at $184,500. Medicare tax has no limit.

If you also have a job, the W-2 wages already taxed for Social Security use up part of that $184,500. The calculator subtracts them before applying the 12.4% rate, so a person with $150,000 in wages has only $34,500 of room left for self-employment earnings.

## Additional Medicare Tax

A separate 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies when self-employment income, together with wages, passes $200,000 for single and head of household filers, $250,000 for married couples filing jointly, or $125,000 for married filing separately. The calculator shows it on its own line because it is not part of the self-employment tax you can deduct.

## The Deductible Half

You can deduct half of your self-employment tax when figuring adjusted gross income. That deduction lowers income tax, not the self-employment tax itself. In the $50,000 example, the deductible half is $3,532.39. ## What This Calculator Leaves Out

The estimate covers self-employment tax only. It does not include income tax, state tax, the farm and nonfarm optional methods, church employee income, or partnership rules. It assumes any W-2 wages you enter were fully subject to Social Security and Medicare.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Self-Employment Tax Rate for 2026?

The rate is 15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security on earnings up to $184,500 and 2.9% for Medicare on all earnings, applied to 92.35% of net earnings.

### Do I Pay Self-Employment Tax on Small Side Income?

You generally owe it when net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more. Below that, no self-employment tax is usually due.

### Does Self-Employment Tax Replace Income Tax?

No. Self-employment tax funds Social Security and Medicare. Income tax is separate and is figured on taxable income after deductions, including half of the self-employment tax.
